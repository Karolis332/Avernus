## Sources

### Q1 and Q2: Chapters 4 and 5

1. https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/20913417-running-descent-into-avernus-chapter-4-and-5
   Repost of a DM blog with input from a co-author of the book. Chapter 4 needs little rework: add gnolls outside, run the citadel as a plain crawl, and choose how gory it is. Idyllglen has seven events and you can run only a few. Chapter 5: decide the ending first, bring Kreeg back before Zariel, and never let one Persuasion roll decide it. Close with a one-year jump and ask each player what their character does.
2. https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/47025/roleplaying-games/remixing-avernus-part-8-the-end
   The Alexandrian does not script the finale because it depends on the table. Core goals are breaking Bellandi's pact, cutting Elturel's chains and lifting the city out. An unredeemed Zariel is one option for the last opponent. He recommends resolving the main loose ends, echoing all three acts, and building a final test on the campaign's theme, with a betrayal at the last moment as an option.
3. https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/47036/roleplaying-games/remixing-avernus-epilogue-part-2-elturels-return
   Lists Zariel's fates: dead, alive and unredeemed, or redeemed (judged most likely). Redeemed aftermath ideas include a farewell scene before she leaves for Mount Celestia, or her staying to reach out to her cultists. Asks who replaces her as Archduchess and how the Blood War shifts. Asks whether Lulu goes with Zariel or stays; readers suggest Zariel joins the party as an NPC, or enters the Companion willingly with Lulu.
4. http://thecampaign20xx.blogspot.com/2019/09/dungeons-dragons-guide-to-baldurs-gate.html
   Long chapter-by-chapter guide (only the first 100k characters read). Notes the Scab's low ceilings and blood pools, and that the Citadel door needs Lulu. Idyllglen deaths are real. Gives the book's levels: 12 at the citadel, 13 for the finale. The book's redemption check lowers its DC when Lulu and Olanthius are present. Killing Zariel voids her contracts.
5. https://www.dndcampaignresources.com/baldurs-gate-descent-into-avernus-resources
   Roundup page. Says Chapters 4 and 5 hinge on the Zariel and Bel betrayal, which only works if you prep the NPC dynamics. Notes Eventyr's Bitter Rivals as a motivation reset before the endgame, and that the Alexandrian remix replaces the railroad with open faction nodes.

16. Local source: Eventyr Ch4 guide ("DM's Resources for Chapter 4: Sword of Zariel"), `dm-app/library/library-text.js`, search terms "Scab" and "Bleeding Citadel".
    Calls the chapter dense, linear and meant for one adventuring day with a magical long rest in the middle. Suggests a map of the Scab in three layers, widening the crowded gnoll corridor, and letting the traitor Trantolox pause the last fight to bargain. Remind players this is their last fight before the Citadel restores them.
17. Local source: Eventyr Ch4 guide, search terms "Idyllglen", "Lulu" and "Zariel".
    Suggests letting Yael's ghost give a quiet tour of the Citadel (a stained glass window showing Zariel's fall) before the vision starts, so the memory does not depend on Lulu being present or alive. Lists the seven Idyllglen events, with a one-hour calm in the middle. For the Yeenoghu fight, make the Zariel statue something the party defends, so the fight matters even though Zariel arrives to end it. She can be delayed to about five rounds for a strong party.
18. Local source: Eventyr Ch5 guide ("DM's Resources for Chapter 5: Escape from Avernus"), search terms "Zariel" and "Lulu".
    Reduces the chapter to three tasks in any order: deal with Zariel, break the chains, return Elturel. Redeem, bargain or fight are the three Zariel paths. Warns that a single check feels anticlimactic and suggests a group check where each PC argues, plus a fight in Elturel beforehand. Notes the book's DC drops when Lulu or Olanthius is present.
19. Local source: Eventyr Ch5 guide, search terms "Zariel" and "Idyllglen" (deals and opening).
    Says the information you give the party steers the ending: decide whether redemption or a fight is plan A. Lulu pushes redemption while Gargauth pushes allies who could free him. Bargains with Zariel include killing a demon lord, handing over the Shield, an ultimate sacrifice, or serving her. A deal can be offered by Zariel herself to save face.

### Q3: Making Zariel appear early

6. https://halflinghobbies.com/baldurs-gate-descent-into-avernus-10-things-you-need-to-know-before-you-run-it/
   Says Zariel should be felt before she shows up: rumors, and how other NPCs (Bel) react to her, build her as a political, capable leader. Recommends giving every player a personal tie to someone in Elturel. Lulu's powers should return as her memories do. Suggests the optional bargain death-save rule (a devil offers 1 HP for a dark deed, enforced by Geas) as temptation. Let the players pick the Sword's wielder.
7. https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/47005/roleplaying-games/remixing-avernus-part-7d-c-fortress-locations
   The flying fortress: Zariel is usually on the command deck, sometimes in her quarters (opulent and unsettling, with two empty sword brackets under a demon skull). Her half of Bellandi's Pact sits in a vault only she can open. Gives a roll-based chance she is present when the party raids. The source gives no dialogue, so her voice is up to the DM.
8. https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/tag/descent-into-avernus/page/2
   Index page of his remix series. Its review notes the book lets players redeem, bargain with, or join Zariel. Useful as a route to the Part 8 and Fortress pages above.

### Q4: Zariel and Lulu

9. https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/dungeons-dragons-discussion/story-lore/190012-descent-into-avernus-alt-ending
   A DM disliked the book's single-check redemption. In their game Zariel refused, summoned corrupt Hellriders, a loyal ghostly army led by Yael appeared, and Lulu took the killing blow meant for the last PC. That loss broke Zariel's hatred and she was redeemed. A reply warns that an "it was all a dream" reveal frustrates some tables.

### Q5: Returning after long breaks

10. https://www.enworld.org/threads/when-you-continue-playing-after-a-multi-month-break-tips-needed.682305/
    Forum thread. Have players recap first so you learn what they remember, then fill gaps briefly. Open with action, use a small time jump if the ending allows, and hand out a one-to-two page reference sheet with an NPC glossary. Track small details after each session. Treat the return like a first session.
11. https://www.rjd20.com/2022/08/how-to-return-to-your-dnd-game.html
    Message the group first. Skip the long recap and start with action. Optionally write a short piece of fiction recapping the campaign and why each character matters. Give the party a villain tied to them with a mystery to keep them invested.
12. https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-restart-campaign-hiatus-long-break/
    Fix the cause of the pause first. Cut threads nobody cared about. A timeskip can refresh motivations if players decide what their characters did. Give each player a short spotlight and bring back consequences of earlier choices.
13. https://slyflourish.com/easy_tricks.html
    A tricks list from DMs on social media. Recap ideas: players recap at the start; a short narrative recap before each turn; inspiration for the recapper. Re-engagement: tell players when they are on deck, ask quiet players how their character feels. Pacing: group initiative, only call rolls when failure matters.

### Q6: Pacing for three level-13 PCs

14. https://anvilnink.com/dd-for-3-players/
    Use fewer enemies instead of many weak ones, and trim enemy numbers or HP by roughly a fifth to a quarter. Plan two or three encounters a day, not four to six. Build exits (fleeing, surrender, environment). Give every PC an NPC thread. Offer extra healing and clear recovery options.
15. https://thedmlair.com/blogs/news/how-to-run-d-d-for-small-groups
    Count actions per round on each side and avoid fielding more enemies than PCs. Allies should be weaker than the PCs, run by the players in combat, with no plot armor. Small groups cope with more magic items and consumables. Very tough foes need a non-combat way out.

## Top takeaways for this table

1. **Decide the ending first.** Pick how the finale resolves before the Arkhan siege ends, then work backward (sources 1, 2).
2. **Zariel must be felt before she is seen.** Rumors, the reactions of Kreeg, Bel and the generals, and the Or'Henn visions already do this (6).
3. **One roll never decides Zariel.** Make the talk a scene where she also wants the Sword. Use a group check where each PC argues, and let bad rolls cost something instead of ending the story (1, 4, 18).
4. **Lulu is the lever.** The book lowers the redemption DC when Lulu is present, and DM tables found Lulu's sacrifice or choice to be the emotional turn (4, 9, 3).
5. **Lulu's payoff should be a choice with weight.** Either she goes with Zariel or stays; she is the price or the proof, not a prop (3, 9).
6. **Bring back old threads.** Kreeg, Gargauth, Yael, Olanthius and the Hellrider generals give the finale callbacks from all three acts (1, 2).
7. **Treat the Sword's wielder as a player choice.** The wielder's alignment shift shapes the endgame, so let the table pick (6).
8. **Run only the Idyllglen events that earn their time.** The seven events can shrink to three, and deaths inside count (1, 4).
9. **After a break, let players recap first, then open with action.** Keep any recap to a few minutes plus a one-page NPC sheet (10, 11, 13).
10. **Give each PC a spotlight early in the session.** Short, personal, tied to a thread (12, 14).
11. **Plan two or three encounters per day, not more.** Three PCs burn resources fast (14).
12. **Fewer, stronger enemies and an escape route.** Avoid more foes than PCs, add flee or surrender options, and give a non-combat way out of the Arkhan siege (14, 15, 5).
13. **Allies run by the players, never plot-armored.** Moonkite, Veska and Ser Lucan help without overshadowing three PCs (15).
14. **Close with a time jump.** Ask each player what their character does a year later (1).
15. **Frame the finale as three tasks in any order.** Deal with Zariel, break the chains, return Elturel; the information you hand the party steers which comes first, so decide your plan A (18, 19). Pick a final test around the campaign's theme.** For this table that is the Tiamat bargain and the Law versus mercy, with a late betrayal on the table (2).
