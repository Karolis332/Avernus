Or'Henn — The Ashen Shepherd

DM's behind-the-screen primer · D&D 5e (2024) · Descent into Avernus · PC of "Mikas" · elderly Red Dragonborn druid, Level 11

The one-line pitch. Or'Henn is a dispossessed keeper, not a refugee. He was the chief living retainer of an Avernian noble's garden-estate; a demon warband razed it and a barlgura looted his badge-of-office (the staff Cinderbloom). He kept his druid magic but lost his title, authority, and lawful claim. The whole arc is him clawing back a domain — and the Emporium's devils are happy to sell him the paperwork, each sheet with a hook in it.

1True Backstory (the full, canonical version)

The Ashen Garden

Or'Henn was hatched in the Ashen Garden — a chartered enclave of living retainers attached to the estate of an Avernian devil noble. The household deliberately kept mortal gardeners, handlers, and scribes alive because living hands can cultivate what Hell can corrupt but cannot create. Devils can twist, torment, and pervert life; they cannot grow it. So the noble house farmed the one thing Avernus lacks: genuine, living nature — tended by a stable of enslaved-but-skilled keepers. This is why the enclave existed and why Or'Henn's kind were valuable rather than merely tortured.

The predecessor & the staff

His teacher was an enslaved druid taken from the Material Plane — dragged to Avernus and put to work. This predecessor taught Or'Henn to drive roots through iron, bone, and ash (the flavor behind Cinderbloom's Rootwright Magic), and on his passing left Or'Henn Cinderbloom. Crucially, Cinderbloom is NOT his class powers. It is three things at once:

  • The badge of the estate's chief keeper — the symbol of office.
  • The key to the menagerie wards — it opens/commands the estate's beast-containment (mechanically echoed by Keeper of Fell Beasts).
  • Legal proof of keepership — infernal law recognizes the bearer as the lawful groundskeeper.
Load-bearing ruling. Or'Henn keeps his druidic magic without the staff. Wild Shape, spellcasting, Circle features — all his, always. The staff is his title and inheritance, not his character sheet. This is deliberate: it preserves his agency and makes recovery a story goal, not a power crutch. Do not let a table read the staff-loss as "the druid is nerfed until he gets it back." He is fully functional; what he lacks is standing.

The razing

The estate was destroyed by a Yeenoghu-aligned warband — explicitly a vanguard, not the demon lord himself (demons that die on Avernus reform in the Abyss, so "Yeenoghu attacked" never means the god in person). Or'Henn fled without the staff. What he actually lost was not power but position: his office as chief keeper, his authority over the surviving beasts, and any lawful claim to the Ashen Garden.

Tie-in to Session 7 (strongly recommended). The party just defeated the Yeenoghu + Dumal front in the Demon Zapper climax. Bolt Or'Henn's backstory onto that victory: the warband that razed his estate was the same demonic incursion the party already broke. That makes the new PC's grievance and the old party's triumph the same event — instant shared stakes, and it explains why the survivors/loose assets are surfacing now. The shield crest (a devil noble house) is the open thread; name it when the party asks, and you can hang the estate's former master off it.

His real ambition

Not vengeance, not going home. Or'Henn wants to recover Cinderbloom, then settle or weaponize the estate's remaining obligations and build a domain of his own. He is an heir chasing an inheritance in a plane that runs on contracts — which is exactly the bait the Emporium dangles. Play him as patient, proprietary, and comfortable with infernal legalism; he is a native of Avernus, not a horrified visitor.

2The Recovery Arc — structure & pacing

The staff-recovery questline is a clean 3-beat chase to a boss, threaded through three of the five contracts. The other two (II, IV) are optional side-deals for gear and for Asimov's benefit.

estate boundary seal → Blood-War salvage manifest → barlgura Kharzug the Root-Eater → the Weeping Furrow trench → staff given as tribute to Lady Urexiel, Keeper of the Scabrous Menagerie (the boss)

The breadcrumb chain (what each contract does for the chase)

  • Contract I — LEAD the manifest. Recovering the estate's brass boundary seal earns an authenticated salvage manifest that names Kharzug the Root-Eater as the barlgura who removed Cinderbloom, and fixes his last deployment at the Weeping Furrow.
  • Contract III — TRACKER the ashwood-flake compass. Inside Morrow's collar is a flake of living ashwood; held over flame it bends toward the tree/staff it was cut from — a 1-hour compass before it burns away. This is the tool that actually points at the staff.
  • Contract V — CROWBAR the writ of reclamation. The writ names Cinderbloom as Or'Henn's lawful property and lets him seize it from any holder below pit-fiend rank. It also confirms the staff passed through the Weeping Furrow to Lady Urexiel. This is the legal lever that lets him take the staff back from Urexiel without simply murdering a Keeper.

Avernus has no fixed distances

No honest infernal document gives a distance. The Weeping Furrow is found by three supernatural signs, not by "two days east":

  • ◆ broken chains crossing the sky
  • ◆ iron rain falling upward
  • ◆ a trench that weeps black sap

Suggested run order (3 beats)

  1. Beat 1 — Contract I (the manifest). Party returns to the ruined estate, recovers the boundary seal, earns the manifest. Reveal: Kharzug + Weeping Furrow. Trap primed: Or'Henn is now legally the "final acting groundskeeper" (see Encumbrance I).
  2. Beat 2 — Contract III (the compass), then Contract V (the writ). Bag Morrow for the ashwood-flake compass; take the iron-orchard job for the writ. Now the party has a pointer and a legal claim. Confront Kharzug at the Weeping Furrow — recover the staff, or learn it was handed onward to Urexiel.
  3. Beat 3 — Lady Urexiel (the boss). The staff is tribute in her Scabrous Menagerie. Resolve by writ (present the reclamation writ), by bargain, or by fight. This dovetails into the Mother Ash capstone — one of the three Green Remnants sits in her menagerie, so beating/dealing-with Urexiel can hand off both the staff and the first upgrade component.

3The Contracts — full Encumbrances (the traps, stated openly)

"Good news: the Emporium has leads. Bad news: every lead has read the fine print." — Asimov, from inside the soul capacitor.

Binding mechanic (know this cold). Every contract is a proposed instrument — it binds nothing until three things happen: (1) Or'Henn marks it in ash, (2) the issuer countersigns, and (3) Fhet'Ahla — Mahadi's amnizu notary — affixes the seal of the charter. Until Fhet'Ahla seals it, players can freely decline. Once sealed, they're hooked. Each instrument is voidable only by both signatories, before Fhet'Ahla, on surrender of the original and payment of his fee — so backing out is possible but costs. Asimov (soul rides the capacitor, can read infernal fine print) is the party's early-warning system for the Encumbrances; let him flag them if the players ask, or via Contract IV.

How the traps interlock (the elegant part). Encumbrance I makes Or'Henn liable for the three loose specimens; Encumbrance V makes him heir to every asset/enemy/debt of the estate. Sign both and he has legally adopted the entire wreck of the Ashen Garden — which is exactly the domain he wants, just weaponized against him. That's the campaign: he can accept the burdens and become a landed keeper, or spend the arc voiding instruments before Fhet'Ahla. Both are good outcomes; make him choose.

4The Mother Ash — the staff upgrade (capstone)

Vaelisha, the Mother Ash — the Last Green of Avernus. Before this plane became the first layer of Hell it was a living world; when it fell, all its nature burned save one tree, hidden in a warded hollow and kept barely alive. Every infernal gardener's staff — Or'Henn's and his predecessor's alike — was cut from her boughs. To rekindle Cinderbloom the party gathers what remains of Avernus's nature and feeds it to her; her renewed sap re-infuses the staff.

The rite (quest)

Collect three Green Remnants — the last living scraps of Avernus's nature — then perform an 8-hour infusion rite at Vaelisha. Suggested remnants:

  • the last seed of Idyllglen;
  • living moss from a fallen titan's ribs in the Bleeding Wastes;
  • a sap-heart cutting held in Lady Urexiel's Scabrous Menagerie — dovetails directly with Contract V and the boss beat, so the same confrontation that recovers the staff can also yield the first upgrade component.
Gating advice. Do not open the Mother Ash arc until the staff itself is recovered. Cinderbloom must exist in Or'Henn's hand before it can be Rekindled; recovering it is the emotional payoff of the whole chase, and the upgrade is the reward beyond the reward. Sequence: recover staff (Urexiel beat) → then Green Remnants → then Vaelisha rite. The Urexiel Green Remnant is your natural bridge between the two arcs.

6Adjudication Notes — running Or'Henn

(a) Wild Shape & the infernal form list

Non-Beast forms must be EARNED individually. Or'Henn does not auto-gain the Monstrosity/Fiend/Elemental forms in the Wild Shape Codex. Each must be unlocked by studying, defeating, binding, or tending that specific creature. When earned, he gains only its physical shape and natural attacks — no innate spellcasting, no Shapechanger, no Summon, no other magical traits, unless you explicitly grant them. He is a Circle of Wildfire druid (RAW cap: CR 1); by your ruling he Wild Shapes as a Bonus Action into CR ≤ 2 — Beast and earned-infernal forms alike, synergizing with Keeper of Fell Beasts. CR 3 forms stay locked (aspirational; the Hell Hound is his signature goal).

(b) Cinderbloom balance (very rare, as tuned in review)

(c) The staff is title, not class

Restated because it's the most important framing: Or'Henn keeps all his druid magic without Cinderbloom. The staff is inheritance/authority/keeper's-badge. Losing it costs him standing, not spells. Never adjudicate the arc as "underpowered until recovery."

(d) Asimov, the soul capacitor & the Soul Council's clock

Asimov is the party's own fallen Soulknife. He died at Yeenoghu's hands in the Session-7 climax, and his soul was taken into the soul capacitor — a bounty-hunter's soul-vessel governed by the Soul Council. His soul occupies it now in place of Rasheem — the previous occupant, and the one who fed Asimov his bounties back when Asimov was the living hunter. The role runs in a chain: the trapped soul relays bounties to a living bounty hunter, and when that hunter dies they take the occupant's place. Asimov's death to Yeenoghu is why the vessel holds him now and not Rasheem — and why Asimov relays to the party exactly as Rasheem once did to him. The Soul Council has given Asimov one week to find another bounty hunter — that ticking clock is why he relays the soul-bounties and why the party needs Or'Henn. From inside the vessel he can read infernal fine print: mechanically he's the party's rules-lawyer against the Encumbrances (let him warn players who consult him), and Bounty IV (Articles of Temporary Embodiment) is his showcase — an hour of embodiment that reads the exact text of a contract binding a creature and exposes one hidden clause of his own confinement. Continuity: the party knew Asimov; Or'Henn never did — Or'Henn is the fresh recruit, met at the Emporium as a post-ravager refugee.

7Threads & Foreshadowing

The coffer's imprisoned true name (Contract V) — DM decides whose

The blank name-substitution writ inside the obsidian coffer holds one imprisoned true name; Malfius won't say whose. Pick one and seed it:

Option A — Or'Henn's dead master. The devil noble who owned the Ashen Garden. Releasing his name onto Or'Henn's contracts makes Or'Henn legally become his former owner — inheriting the estate's enemies in the most literal, horrifying way. Ties the whole arc into a single knot.
Option B — the enslaved predecessor druid. The Material-Plane druid who taught Or'Henn and left him the staff. A mercy-and-horror choice: freeing the name could liberate a long-tormented soul — or overwrite Or'Henn with a broken one. Emotional, personal.
Option C — a Yeenoghu-front demon lieutenant. The named commander of the warband that razed the estate (and the Session-7 front). Releasing it swaps Or'Henn's identity for a wanted demon's — every devil debt in Avernus suddenly hunts him. Highest-stakes, most explosive.

The three uncatalogued specimens (Contract I)

Three beasts from the estate menagerie are loose and unaccounted for — the live source of Encumbrance I's breach-marks, and a ready-made monster-of-the-week trio. One of them can be Morrow (Contract III), collapsing two threads into one. The other two are yours to design (candidates: something that escaped into the Bleeding Wastes near a Green Remnant; something a rival keeper is already hunting). Each day they stay loose ticks the clock on the seal.

Hooks toward the next arc — Bel's Forge

Next destination is Bel's Forge (session-6-bels-forge-primer.md). Bridges: (1) the infernal war machine in Contract V is Forge-made — trace its maker's mark to Bel; (2) infernal-brass gear (Contract II) and the writ's authority both point toward Bel's industrial-legal apparatus; (3) a Green Remnant or the Mother Ash's warded hollow can sit along the road to the Forge. Use the staff-recovery arc as the connective tissue between the closed Dumal/Yeenoghu arc and Bel's Forge.
