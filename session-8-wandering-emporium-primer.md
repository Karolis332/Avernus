# Session 8 Primer — The Wandering Emporium

**Date:** TBD
**Party:** Drenwal (Light Cleric, Bhaalspawn), Aurora (Archfey Warlock, Moonkite-bonded), + **NEW PC: Or'Henn the Ashen Shepherd** (Circle of Wildfire druid, Level 12, elderly Red Dragonborn, native to Avernus). Asimov is present *as a voice* — his soul rides the **Soul Capacitor** now.

## DM SUMMARY — WHAT THIS SESSION IS

**Starting point:** The party has just won at the Last Hill (Session 7 — Yeenoghu's avatar banished; Drenwal wounded his brother "Den," who broke free and escaped, wounded and unaided). The Lady Vengeance is beached and damaged there. **Asimov died to Yeenoghu** and his soul is now in the **Soul Capacitor**, governed by the **Soul Council**, which has given him **ONE WEEK** to find a new *living* bounty hunter or the vessel's arrangement collapses. That clock is the engine of the session.

**What the party wants:** Reach the **Wandering Emporium**, recruit a living hunter for the capacitor before the week runs out, and collect the leads Asimov can relay. The hunter they find is **Or'Henn** — a post-ravager refugee whose garden-estate was razed by the *same* Yeenoghu-aligned front the party just broke. Shared grievance, ready-made buy-in.

**What actually happens:** The party *returns* to the Emporium — familiar ground, a host they've dealt with before — now arriving as the **Heroes of the Last Hill**. Mahadi receives them, the market is open to them, they work the shops and the floor, they meet and recruit **Or'Henn**, and Asimov + Fhet'Ahla lay out the **five soul-bounties** as *proposed instruments*. The session ends with the party picking their first bounty and pointing at the next destination.

**⚠ CONTINUITY — this is a RETURN visit.** The party has **already been to the Wandering Emporium and met Mahadi.** Do *not* run this as a first discovery. They know the caravan, the truce rule, and the tiger-faced host; Mahadi knows *them* (and has an eye on Red Ruth's leftovers, which they carry). Play arrival as **recognition, not wonder** — the genuinely new elements this session are the **one-week clock**, the **full run of the market** (see *The Emporium — Full Directory* below), and **recruiting Or'Henn**. The truce and Mahadi beats below are written as *reminders*, not introductions.

## COLD OPEN — THE CLOCK STARTS (20 min)

Read aloud:

> The Last Hill is quiet now. Yeenoghu's vanguard is meat and ash; Dumal is gone — you wounded him and he fled, bleeding, into the ash; not dead, not caught; the sky over the ridge has gone from wound-red to the dull rust of ordinary Avernus. Behind you the **Lady Vengeance** lists in the black soil, one runner buckled, hull scorched, going nowhere without a forge and a week.
>
> And in the pack at Drenwal's hip, the **Soul Capacitor** — the iron-and-brass vessel that once held Rasheem and now holds *your own dead* — grows **warm**. Not hot. Warm, like a hand on your shoulder.
>
> *"...okay. Ow. Yes. I'm here. I'm — this is deeply undignified."* The voice is Asimov's, threaded through the tick of settling metal. *"So. Good news, bad news. Good news: I can hear the Council in here, and they've made me an offer. Bad news: they've also started a clock."*
>
> *"They gave me a week. Seven days to find another living bounty hunter to feed, or this whole arrangement — me, the vessel, the leads — comes apart, and I go wherever Rasheem went before me. I'd rather not find out where that is."*
>
> Far out across the plain, low on the horizon, a *familiar* string of lights is moving — torches, lanterns, brazier-glow, a whole *caravan* crawling on wheels the size of houses. You have walked that market before. **The Wandering Emporium.** Mahadi's caravan has found the Last Hill — or been drawn to it.

**DM notes:**
- **[CAMPAIGN CANON]** The capacitor / Soul Council chain is established in `orhenn-dm-primer.html §6(d)` and the digest. Beat to land here: Asimov now occupies the vessel *in place of Rasheem* (who once fed Asimov his bounties); the role passes down the chain when a hunter dies. **The one-week deadline is the whole reason to hurry.** [D&D CANON] soul coins are iron, warm to the touch, and let the holder speak with the soul inside — lean on that texture; the capacitor reads like a bigger cousin of a soul coin.
- **The party knows Asimov** (he was theirs). They will feel the death. Give Aurora and Drenwal a moment. Then let the clock cut it short — grief is a luxury with a deadline.
- **They know this place.** The party has visited the Emporium and dealt with **Mahadi** before — no need to re-introduce either. What's *new* is why they're hurrying (the clock) and who they'll leave with (Or'Henn). 🎲 Optional color: the caravan turning up right after the Last Hill can be plain luck — or Mahadi steering toward the fresh "Heroes of the Last Hill" (and toward Red Ruth's leftovers; see Act 2). **[D&D CANON]** aside: **Mad Maggie** (Fort Knucklebone) and Mahadi were once lovers who parted badly — if the party is close to Maggie, she has sharp opinions about where they're headed.

## ACT 1 — ARRIVAL AT THE EMPORIUM (35 min)

### The approach

Read aloud:

> Up close, the **Wandering Emporium** is not a town — it's a *machine that decided to sell things.* A caravan of **infernal war machines**, each hauling a stall or a tent lashed to its back: a devil's-ride dragging a barbershop, a demon-grinder turned rolling forge, a soul-fueled crawler stacked with cages. When it stops, the tents *unfold* off the machines and root into the ash; when it's time to move, the whole market packs itself back onto its wheels in an hour and rolls to the next patch of Hell that can pay.
>
> The smells hit before the gate: roasting meat and rendered fat and something floral over the top of it; hot brass, quenched steel, singed hair; the sweet-rot of demon ichor in glass; and under all of it the low warm hum of soul coins by the crateful. Devils haggle beside warlords. A hobgoblin general shares a bench with a thing made of chains. Nobody is fighting. **Nobody is fighting** — and the wrongness of that, in Avernus, is its own kind of loud.
>
> At the caravan's edge, a courteous imp in a waistcoat is already walking out to meet you — by name, arms wide. You're expected. You're *remembered*. Here, that is not entirely a comfort.

**DM notes:**
- **[D&D CANON]** The Emporium sells food, lodging, repairs, grooming, supplies, information, and *stranger* services to devils, warlords, and travelers. It roams Avernus and operates **with Asmodeus's approval**.
- **The Lady Vengeance angle:** the party needs *repairs* (Act 3, Rassh) as much as a hunter. That's their honest cover story and their honest need — good reason to be here beyond recruiting.
- **The greeter:** the same sort of low-level imp or minor devil that escorts guests to Mahadi — this time welcoming the party back as returning patrons. He still performs the ritual: names, business, and — smiling — whether they're "carrying any *quarrels* today, so we can check them at the door." A familiar formality; play it light.

## ACT 2 — INFERNAL RAPTURE: MAHADI (50 min)

### Mahadi's welcome — returning guests

Read aloud:

> **Infernal Rapture** is the beating heart of the caravan — half restaurant, half spa, wholly a trap you'd walk into gladly, and you've walked into it before. Silk the color of arterial blood. Low couches. Steam scented with cardamom and sulfur. A long table already set, as though your seats were reserved. They were.
>
> The host rises to meet you — and you know him at once: the **tiger's face** where a man's should be, the gold eyes, the hands clasped in welcome that sit **backwards** on his wrists, the robe worth more than most warlords' hoards. **Mahadi.** He remembers you, of course. Mahadi remembers everyone — every name, every preference, every debt of courtesy left unpaid.
>
> *"Heroes of the Last Hill. Yes — word travels, even here, especially here. You broke a demon lord's little garden party. Sit. **Sit.** You are hungry, you are grieving, you are down a ship and, I hear, down a friend."* His gaze rests, exactly, on the warm iron vessel at Drenwal's hip. *"The Emporium can help with two of those. Perhaps three. Everything here is for sale, and the first course — the first course is on the house."*

**DM notes — running Mahadi [D&D CANON]:**
- He is a **rakshasa**: courteous, charming, attentive, *generous on the surface* — calculating, self-interested, and exploitative beneath. Play the warmth 100% sincere on the outside. The menace is in what the warmth *costs*.
- He runs **Infernal Rapture** (restaurant + spa) and pays in soul coins and bargains. He wants **profitable bargains, relationships, influence, information, and souls.** He is an **agent of Asmodeus** and reports valuable intel upward; he respects the infernal hierarchy and will never openly defy a bigger power.
- **THE "ON THE HOUSE" TRAP [D&D CANON]:** accepting his hospitality — the free course, the free spa treatment, the free grooming — can create *dangerous obligations*. Nothing binds mechanically here (that's Fhet'Ahla's job), but a debt of *courtesy* is real currency to a rakshasa. **DC 18 Insight** (`npc-reference.md`): he fears angering Asmodeus, and every kindness is an investment. Let a careful player feel the hook without spelling it out.
- **Info pricing [D&D CANON / campaign]:** ~10 gp per question for general Avernus knowledge; the *good* answers cost bargains, not coins.

### The truce still holds (a reminder, not a first lesson)

They know the rule — but a gracious host says it once more, lightly, so no one pretends otherwise:

> *"You know the house rule, so I'll not belabor it: **leave your feud at the door.** No Blood War, no warband grudge, no old score under my roof — not for anyone, and *especially* not for heroes fresh off a kill. Break Asmodeus's peace and I decide what becomes of you. I am *very* generous, and I am *very* creative, and today I am in *such* a good mood. Don't spoil it."*

**THE TRUCE RULE (adjudication) [D&D CANON]:**

| The player does... | Mahadi's response |
|---|---|
| Threatens, insults, intimidates (words only) | Nothing yet. He *smiles.* A warning, delivered warmly. First offense is free — barely. |
| Draws a weapon / readies a hostile spell but does not use it | Firm warning. The market goes quiet. Guards (see below) drift closer. **This is the last warning.** |
| **Actually attacks a guest or vendor** | The truce is broken. **Mahadi acts by his own authority and Asmodeus's backing:** peace-breakers are punished, **expelled, or killed at his discretion.** See the **TRUCE box** appendix for the full adjudication + ⚔️ enforcement. |
| Defends against someone *else* who broke the truce | Mahadi's enforcers handle the aggressor; a PC defending themselves is fine, but *finishing* a downed aggressor inside the market is itself a breach. |

- **Why this still matters:** they know the rule — but they've just come off a battle high, walking into a room that may hold fiends *from the very front they just broke*. **The temptation to swing is the point.** The rule is already on record, so when a player is tempted, the restraint on the line is the *player's*, not yours. The mechanics live in the **TRUCE appendix**; you shouldn't need them.
- Keep the enforcement threat *credible but off-screen* unless triggered — see the appendix box for the ⚔️ statline pointer.

### Mahadi's live angle — the Red Ruth gambit [HOMEBREW]

This is Mahadi's *reason to court the party* this session. From `npc-reference.md`:

- **[HOMEBREW]** Red Ruth was Mahadi's **oracle-on-retainer** — he pampered her (the canon spa/makeover treatment) in exchange for secrets. **She's dead now, and the PARTY holds her leftovers:** her **Bhaalspawn knowledge** (Drenwal / Dumal / Senna), her **soul-stock**, the **Mordenkainen letter**, and her **heartstone**.
- Mahadi *wants all of it.* He will bargain, flatter, and scheme to buy, trade, or quietly lift Ruth's leavings — offering Bhaalspawn-arc intel, Emporium services, or a "favor to be named" in exchange. He will **never** overtly defy a bigger power to get them, but he is patient and he is a rakshasa; expect a soft approach now and pressure later.

**How to run it this session:** don't make it the main course. Mahadi *notices* the heartstone / the letter / the grief around Asimov, name-drops **Red Ruth** ("a dear associate of mine, recently... *retired*"), and floats the first offer. It's a slow-burn thread — plant it, let it breathe, collect on it across the arc. 🎲 DIAL: how much does Mahadi already know he's holding? Default: he knows Ruth is dead and suspects these particular travelers cleaned out her Brambles — a suspicion he'll pay to confirm.

> **Cross-reference:** Mahadi's temperament, info-pricing, and the Asmodeus-fear Insight DC live in `npc-reference.md` → *Wandering Emporium → Mahadi*. Don't re-stat him; run him.

## ACT 3 — THE EMPORIUM: FULL DIRECTORY & THINGS TO DO (60–70 min)

They know the market — so *let them work it.* This is the complete run of the Wandering Emporium: **exactly six canon establishments** (that's the whole published set — anything else you add is [HOMEBREW]), plus the homebrew stalls this campaign has planted. The party has real needs today — **repair the Lady Vengeance, resupply, gather intel, and find a hunter** — so give them room to shop and scheme. Hit three or four stalls for a lively floor; the rest is there if they wander.

> **📖 Full stocks, prices, haggle DCs & floor encounters:** this section runs the *scene*; for complete stock lists, soul-coin/gp prices, per-merchant negotiation DCs, the haggling subsystem, and the Emporium random-encounter + clientele tables, open the companion **`wandering-emporium-reference.md`** (rendered: `wandering-emporium-reference.html`).

**[D&D CANON] Payment & the golden rule of this market:** the Emporium runs on **soul coins** and on **bargains, favors, and obligations** — *not* gold. Coin buys the ordinary; the *good* stuff costs a deal. And **nothing here is truly free:** Mahadi's "on the house" hospitality is a *debt*, and every return visit that accepts his generosity **compounds** what the party owes him. Track it.

### Infernal Rapture — Mahadi (rakshasa) [D&D CANON]
- **The heart of the caravan** — restaurant + spa + sanctuary, and Mahadi's parlor (you ran his welcome in Act 2). Here the party can **dine** (extraordinary food and drink), **bathe and be pampered**, **rest in comfort under the truce**, **buy information** (Mahadi is exceptionally well-informed), and **commission exotic, rare, or infernal goods** through his network — subject to price and a favor.
- **⚠ The hospitality-debt engine [D&D CANON]:** the free first course, the free soak, the "please, be my guest" — all **place the recipient in Mahadi's debt.** He calls it in later as a service or favor; refusing can escalate to an infernal contract, and **each repeat visit compounds it.** As returning patrons, **the party may already owe him** — decide what (if anything) is on the tab from last time. This is where the **Red Ruth gambit** (Act 2) is transacted.

### Fhet'Ahla — "From Here to Avernus" [D&D CANON]
- **What:** an **amnizu** (that squat, corpulent, wrinkled devil) who runs an **infernal bank** — a soul-coin moneylender whose loans are secured by **infernal contracts.**
- **Run him as:** meticulous, unhurried, faintly bored by mortals who don't read. Coins clink; a ledger writes itself.
- **[CAMPAIGN CANON] His campaign role:** Fhet'Ahla is **Mahadi's contract-NOTARY** — the seal on the charter's soul-bounty *instruments*. Nothing binds or voids without him. This is a natural extension of his real canon role: a canon amnizu whose banking business already runs on infernal contracts, notarizing the Emporium's charter fits him like a glove. **He is the fulcrum of Act 5** — the party will be back at his desk. Foreshadow him here.

### Rassh — "The Firesnake Forge" [D&D CANON]
- **What:** a **salamander** smith whose forge is staffed by **fire snakes.** Buys and sells weapons and armor, **repairs equipment**, and — **canonically — repairs AND refuels infernal war machines.**
- **This is where the Lady Vengeance gets fixed [D&D CANON].** Rassh can service and **refuel** the Hellship (soul-coin fuel) and get her moving again — though a *full* rebuild of the buckled hull may still want Bel's Forge downstream. Give the party a price — soul coins, a favor, or a bounty completed. He's the honest transaction on the floor: burns hot, talks little, delivers.
- **🎲 DIAL — the homebrew salamander:** `npc-reference.md` has a **"Salamander Artificer"** who sells **shattersticks** (5d6 thunder, DC 15 Dex, 10-ft radius). **Recommended: fold that vendor into Rassh** — the Firesnake Forge also stocks shattersticks. (Alternative: keep them as two separate salamander stalls. Note your choice for consistency.) This primer assumes the fold-in.
- **Separately:** **Serephine Coal-Tongue** [HOMEBREW] — the salamander smith of the *Gilded Crucible*, issuer of Bounty II — is a **different** salamander, met via her contract (Act 5), not the same stall as Rassh. Keep them distinct.

### Elliach — "Ichor's Aweigh!" [D&D CANON]
- **What:** a **human mage** who buys and sells **demon ichor** — mutagenic, flesh-warping stuff. Drink it and you *might* gain a boon; you might sprout something.
- **Color and temptation.** Great flavor for Or'Henn (a druid who already lives half-warped by Avernus) or for any player who likes a gamble. Keep the effects table vague and dangerous; this is atmosphere, not a required purchase.

### Z'neth's Larva Farm [D&D CANON]
- **Vendor: Z'neth**, a **hobgoblin warlord** who **buys and sells larvae** — the wormlike soul-forms of the damned, raw material of the Hells.
- **The moral wince of the market.** A pen of squirming larvae for sale by the crate. This is where the party *feels* what a soul is worth in Avernus — relevant, given they're carrying Asimov and Ruth's soul-stock. Z'neth will happily appraise soul-goods; **[HOMEBREW tie]** he's a natural buyer if Mahadi's Red Ruth gambit escalates into a bidding situation.

### Burney the Barber [D&D CANON]
- **What:** a barbershop / grooming stall. Warm, chatty, gives a genuinely good shave — and her treatments can **magically alter or restore a patron's appearance** (a new face, an erased scar, a disguise). Some quiet **healing / restoration** is available here too, though she doesn't advertise it.
- **The secret (DM only, fun):** "Burney" is an **adult copper dragon disguised as a human woman**, an agent of **Bahamut**, **near-impossible for fiends to detect**, who may **discreetly aid good-aligned visitors.** **The party won't know this** unless the campaign opens that door someday. For now she's the one *actually kind* soul on the floor — a small, warm mystery. Bank it; don't spend it.

### The Bone Devil Informant [HOMEBREW]
- From `npc-reference.md` — the homebrew **info-broker** on the floor.
- **DC 16 Persuasion** to get a real answer; a **soul coin grants advantage.**
  > *"Powerful spellcasters know the way — but they'll not lift a finger without blood."*
- **Use him as the connective tissue:** he's the one who'll murmur that "the estate-keeper who lost his garden to the demons is asking after work," pointing the party toward **Or'Henn** (Act 4). He can also confirm rumors about Red Ruth's death for a price — a cheaper, riskier channel than Mahadi.

## ACT 4 — RECRUITING OR'HENN (55 min)

**This is Mikas's spotlight.** He just lost Asimov; give the new character a real entrance. The order that works: the party is *looking* for a hunter (the clock) → the Bone Devil Informant (or Asimov himself) points them at a refugee → they meet Or'Henn → the grievance clicks → Asimov confirms the fit → handoff.

### Or'Henn's introduction — the refugee at the edge of the light

Read aloud (Mikas, this is your cue):

> Past the last bright stall, where the caravan's lantern-light thins into ash-dark, a figure sits apart on an overturned crate. **An elderly Red Dragonborn** — deep crimson scales gone matte with age, horns chipped, fine cracks at the muzzle. One eye is scarred pale grey and blind; the other is calm and gold. His armor is a strange craft of leather and bark and ashwood bound in blackened iron, with small red blossoms — *living* ones — growing at the shoulder. Across his knees rests a round ashwood shield bearing an engraved crest you don't recognize, the sigil of some devil noble house.
>
> He is not begging. He is not haggling. He is watching the market the way a man watches a house that used to be his. When he speaks, his voice is dry and unhurried, and entirely at home in Hell.
>
> *"You have the smell of the Last Hill on you. That warband — the one that fed the hyena-god — I know its work. It came through a garden once. My garden. Sit, if you like. It seems we hate the same dead things."*

**DM notes — who Or'Henn is [HOMEBREW]:**
- **The pitch (from the digest / DM primer):** Or'Henn is a **dispossessed *keeper*, not a wanderer.** He was the chief living retainer of an Avernian devil noble's garden-estate — the **Ashen Garden** — where the household kept living gardeners because *Hell can corrupt life but not create it.* A **Yeenoghu-aligned warband** (a **vanguard**, not the demon lord — demons reform in the Abyss) razed the estate; he fled without his staff of office, **Cinderbloom**, and lost his *title, authority, and lawful claim* — **but kept all his druid magic.** He is here as a **post-ravager refugee**: seeking supplies, shelter, and word of other estates. **He never met Asimov in life.**
- **THE SHARED GRIEVANCE — the hook that sells the recruitment:** the warband that razed his estate is **the same demonic front the party just broke at the Last Hill.** [strongly recommended per `orhenn-dm-primer.html §1`]. The party's victory *is* his revenge-by-proxy; his loss *is* their war's collateral. Play the recognition as a real, quiet click. Instant buy-in without railroading.
- **Load-bearing framing — do NOT play him as a nerfed druid.** Cinderbloom is his *badge and inheritance*, not his class sheet. Wild Shape, spellcasting, Circle of Wildfire features are all his, always. What he lost is *standing*, not spells. (See `orhenn-dm-primer.html §6(c)`.)
- **Voice:** patient, proprietary, comfortable with infernal legalism. A *native* of Avernus, not a horrified tourist. He wants to recover Cinderbloom and rebuild a domain of his own.

### The handoff — Asimov names the candidate

Once Or'Henn and the party are talking, the capacitor warms again:

> *"...him,"* Asimov says, from the iron. *"The Council's rules don't care whether I* like *a hunter — they care whether one will* work. *And this one's already halfway into a hunt he doesn't know he's on. Old man — can you hear me?"*
>
> The vessel pulses. Or'Henn's gold eye narrows at it, unsurprised. In Avernus, a talking box is Tuesday.
>
> *"I'm dead,"* Asimov goes on. *"I hunt from in here now, and I've got leads I can't chase myself. You've got a staff to get back and no lawful way to do it. I've got a bank full of contracts and one week to hand my leads to someone with a pulse. That's not a coincidence, that's a* market. *So. Interested?"*

**DM notes — the mechanical + emotional handoff:**
- **Mechanical:** Or'Henn becoming the capacitor's living hunter is what **resets the Soul Council's clock** and keeps Asimov's arrangement (and the leads) alive. This is the party's win condition for the session.
- **Emotional:** for **Mikas**, this is the through-line — his *old* character (Asimov) literally hands the torch to his *new* one (Or'Henn). Two of his characters in one scene, one dead and relaying, one alive and taking up the work. Give it weight; let him play both sides of the beat if he wants.
- **Continuity guardrail:** the party knew Asimov; **Or'Henn did not.** Or'Henn's rapport is with the *party* and the *grievance*, not with the dead man in the box. He takes the job because it serves *his* goal (Cinderbloom), which the leads happen to advance.
- **Wild Shape / Cinderbloom mechanics:** point Mikas to `orhenn-wildshape-codex.html` and the Cinderbloom card in `orhenn-cinderbloom-and-contracts.html`. Don't re-stat here.

## ACT 5 — THE SOUL-BOUNTIES / CONTRACT BOARD (55 min)

With Or'Henn aboard, Asimov can finally do his job: relay the Emporium's leads as **soul-bounties.** This is where the recovery arc quietly begins.

### How they're presented

Read aloud:

> The capacitor grows warm. Asimov's voice arrives beneath a murmur of turning pages, clinking coins, and distant screams:
>
> *"Good news: the Emporium has leads. Bad news: every lead has read the fine print."*

Then bring the party to **Fhet'Ahla's desk** (or have Fhet'Ahla's clerk, **Malfius Nine-Reeds** [HOMEBREW], attend them). Asimov relays five bounties; Fhet'Ahla lays the *instruments* on the desk.

**THE BINDING MECHANIC — say this at the table [CAMPAIGN CANON]:**

Each of the five is a **proposed instrument.** It binds **nothing** until **three** things happen, in order:

1. **Or'Henn marks it in ash** (his signature — the estate-keeper's mark),
2. **the issuer countersigns,** and
3. **Fhet'Ahla affixes the seal of the charter** (the notarization).

Until Fhet'Ahla seals it, the party can freely **decline.** Once sealed, they're hooked. Each instrument is **voidable only by both signatories, before Fhet'Ahla, on surrender of the original and payment of his fee** — so backing out is possible, but it costs.

- **[CAMPAIGN CANON]** Fhet'Ahla-as-notary is the canon amnizu's contract-banking role, extended to seal the charter's instruments. Malfius Nine-Reeds is his **clerk**, not a second amnizu.
- **[HOMEBREW]** The five **issuers** — Procurator Vhalzareth, Serephine Coal-Tongue, Pellucid Jax, Mistress Nymira Quill, Advocate Malfius Nine-Reeds — are homebrew. 🎲 DIAL: run them **in person** at the Emporium (colorful cameos), or as **sealed instruments delivered to the desk** (their agents present the terms). Either is fine; pick per your energy. Mixed is good — e.g. Serephine appears; the Chancery's Vhalzareth is a paper.

### Asimov is your Encumbrance early-warning system

**[CAMPAIGN CANON — `orhenn-dm-primer.html §6(d)`]** From inside the vessel, Asimov can **read infernal fine print.** Mechanically he's the party's rules-lawyer against the traps: if the players *ask him* to check a contract, let him flag the danger. If they don't ask, let them sign blind — that's the game.

**On the player cards, the trap is deliberately hidden** — the `catch` on each card in `orhenn-cinderbloom-and-contracts.html` renders as *"Fine print — infernal terms, set too small to read at a glance. (DC 15 Investigation, or ask Asimov, to reveal the catch.)"* So the reveal paths are: **DC 15 Investigation**, **ask Asimov**, or later **Bounty IV** (which reads any contract's exact text).

> **DEFER TO THE DM PRIMER.** The full **Encumbrances** (what each trap actually does) are stated openly in `orhenn-dm-primer.html §3`. **Do not restate them at the table or in front of players.** This primer gives you the one-line *Consideration* only (appendix). When a player triggers a reveal, read the Encumbrance from the DM primer.

### The recovery breadcrumb (I → III → V)

**[HOMEBREW]** Three of the five bounties form the Cinderbloom staff-recovery questline. The other two (**II**, **IV**) are optional side-deals (gear; Asimov's benefit). The chain (full version in `orhenn-dm-primer.html §2`):

> **estate boundary seal (I) → salvage manifest → the barlgura Kharzug the Root-Eater → the Weeping Furrow trench → the staff given as tribute to Lady Urexiel, Keeper of the Scabrous Menagerie (the boss).**

- **Bounty I** = the **lead** (names Kharzug + the Weeping Furrow).
- **Bounty III** = the **tracker** (Morrow's collar holds an ashwood-flake compass that points at the staff for 1 hour).
- **Bounty V** = the **legal crowbar** (a writ of reclamation naming Cinderbloom Or'Henn's property; confirms it went to Lady Urexiel).

Run them **I → III → V** for a clean three-beat chase to a boss (Urexiel). You don't need the party to grasp the chain now — just get the cards into their hands and let Bounty I read like the obvious first move.

> **Cards on the table:** hand the five bounty cards from `orhenn-cinderbloom-and-contracts.html` (print one per page). Point players there for the *Consideration* (reward). The *Encumbrance* stays behind your screen (`orhenn-dm-primer.html`).

## ACT 6 — WIND-DOWN (20 min)

### Pick the first bounty

Steer gently toward **Bounty I — The Gardener's Due** as the natural opener (it's the lead that unlocks the chase and it sends them back to Or'Henn's estate — thematically perfect for the new PC's first outing). But let the party choose. If they want gear first (II) or to help Asimov first (IV), that's valid — the arc bends, it doesn't break.

- **Do they SIGN anything this session?** 🎲 DIAL. Cleanest: they **accept Bounty I** and let Fhet'Ahla seal it, feeling the weight of a *first* infernal signature — but hold the rest unsigned. Or they take all five as *proposals* and sign nothing yet, deciding on the road. Either keeps the traps live.

### Cliffhangers & parting gambits

Read aloud (Mahadi, as they leave):

> Mahadi walks you to the edge of his light, hands clasped the wrong way, entirely at ease.
>
> *"A pleasure. Truly. Come back when the old man has his staff — I do so love a reunion."* A pause, warm as the spa steam. *"Oh — and do give my regards to the memory of dear **Red Ruth**. Such a loss. If you should happen to have come into any of her... effects... I would pay handsomely, and discreetly, and I would consider it a personal kindness."* His gold eyes rest, one beat too long, on the pack where the heartstone rides. *"But that is a conversation for another course. Safe travels. Leave your feud at the door on your way out, too."*

- **Mahadi's gambit lands as a hook, not a demand** — the Red Ruth thread [HOMEBREW] is now open and will pull the party back to the Emporium (or bring Mahadi to them).
- **Asimov's clock is RESET** — Or'Henn is the hunter; the week is bought. But the *bounties* now carry their own clocks (Bounty I's breach-marks; the general sense that infernal deals rot if left).
- **Burney the Barber** waves goodbye. (DM: bank the copper-dragon secret. Someday.)

### Next destination

- **Bounty I** sends the party back toward **Or'Henn's ruined estate** to recover the boundary seal — the first beat of the recovery arc.
- Downstream, the arc threads to **Bel's Forge** (`session-6-bels-forge-primer.md`) — the war machine in Bounty V is Forge-made, and the Lady Vengeance's real repair may need Bel's industry. Use the staff-recovery chase as the connective tissue.
- **[HOMEBREW / plot]** the **Mother Ash capstone** (upgrading Cinderbloom to legendary) waits *after* the staff is recovered — do not open it yet (`orhenn-dm-primer.html §4`).

End on Or'Henn, marking Bounty I in ash, or choosing not to — and the caravan's lanterns rolling on into the dark behind them.

**END SESSION.**

## APPENDIX B — TRUCE: IF THE PLAYERS BREAK THE PEACE ⚔️

**[D&D CANON]** Violence is forbidden inside the Emporium. Guests leave feuds at the door. Mahadi enforces the truce **by his own authority and Asmodeus's backing.** Peace-breakers are **punished, expelled, or killed at his discretion.** Adjudicate like this:

**Escalation ladder:**
1. **Hostile words only** → a warm smile and a warning. Free (barely).
2. **Weapon drawn / spell readied, not used** → last warning; the market falls silent; enforcers close in.
3. **First strike lands** → **truce broken.** Mahadi acts.

**When the truce breaks — Mahadi's options (pick by tone & severity):**
- **Expulsion:** the offender (and possibly the party) is *removed* — bounties revoked, the Red Ruth deal poisoned, hospitality withdrawn. **The cheapest lesson.** Strongly prefer this for a first, hot-headed swing — it *costs the party the session's goals* without a TPK.
- **Punishment / price:** Mahadi extracts a *fee* for the breach — a soul coin, a favor, a marked obligation. Fits his rakshasa nature better than a brawl.
- **⚔️ Lethal enforcement (last resort):** if a PC pushes it, Mahadi does not fight fair or alone. A **rakshasa** is CR 13 (Magic Resistance; **Limited Magic Immunity** — ignores spells 6th level and lower unless he chooses otherwise; illusion + Innate Spellcasting), and the market's guests / bouncers back him — treat as a table full of assorted devils who all suddenly agree the intruder is the problem. **Pull the rakshasa (Monster Manual, CR 13) and assorted devil statblocks from your reference of choice (5e.tools / Monster Manual) if it actually comes to blows.** This is a *punishment*, not a balanced encounter; the message is "you cannot win here."

**DM guidance:** the **best** outcome is that no one swings — the *temptation* is the drama. If a player does break it, reach for **expulsion first.** Only go lethal if the table wants the consequence and understands it's a wall, not a boss. Never let a truce-break be *free*; the peace only means something if breaking it costs.
