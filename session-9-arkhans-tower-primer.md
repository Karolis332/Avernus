## RECAP

> **READ ALOUD:**
> The warlord's parasol-bones are still smoking where the harpoon took her out of the sky. Feonor — bone and flesh and a parasol like a closed black wing — lies in the wreck of her own skiff, breathing, beaten, and bound. Or'Henn stands over her with Cinderbloom's absence still raw in his hands. *"Bring back my staff,"* he tells her, and the geas settles into her like a hook through the jaw. She does not thank him. She does not have to. She rises, spits ash, and is gone toward the Weeping Furrow before the ship's shadow has finished passing over her.
>
> Ahead, the sky stops being merely red and starts being *wrong* — a spire of black basalt and dragon-bone leaning into the burning clouds, five scaled skulls mortared above a gate that has never once needed to open for anyone uninvited. Something vast breathes behind those walls, slow and patient, and has for a very long time.

**Status:** Feonor is alive, geas-bound, off-screen. Do not resolve her errand tonight — it's a running thread, not this session's problem. The party is en route to **Arkhan's Tower**, per the Living State Header.

## BEAT 1 — The Dragon Ambush ⚔️

**What it is:** an aerial resource tax. Four of the Tower's chromatic dragons — tuned-down young wyrms, one of each color — jump the Lady Vengeance on approach. This is **not** meant to threaten a TPK; it's meant to burn spell slots, HP, and the ship's hull before the real conversation even starts. Full stats for all four (Skarrix/black, Vhennic/blue, Mirelle/green, Praxamor/red) plus Obatala and Krull: `session-9-arkhan-fight-cards.html`.

> **READ ALOUD — the ambush:**
> The Tower's five skulls were never decoration. Four shapes peel off the spires before the ship has even crossed into bowshot — black, blue, green, red, wingbeats slapping the red air into shockwaves. They don't announce anything. They don't have to. Tiamat's heads have been watching this stretch of sky for longer than Elturel has existed, and today it has brought them something to kill.

### Doctrine — running the 4 rounds

1. **Round 1.** All four dive on the ship's hull simultaneously — this is a show of force, not finesse. Fire the cannons and the harpoon; let the PCs decide whether to defend the deck or take the fight airborne (Aurora on Moonkite can intercept one directly).
2. **Round 2.** Split focus: the black and green dragons keep hitting the hull (acid/poison breath on recharge), the blue and red peel off to breathe on whichever PC is doing the most damage. This is where slots burn — a PC eating an 8d8 lightning line or a 10d6 fire cone should feel it.
3. **Round 3.** Breath weapons likely recharged — second wave, same split. If a PC or the ship is below 25% HP, start pulling punches (near-misses, a dragon "mistiming" a dive) rather than finishing anyone off. This fight is a tax, not an execution.
4. **End trigger (first to occur): the first dragon hits 0 HP, OR the dragons are clearly winning, OR round 4 ends.** On whichever fires first, **Krull arrives, riding Obatala.** Obatala doesn't fight — it lands between the combatants and breathes a warning blast of cold into open air, not at anyone.

> **READ ALOUD — Krull calls the halt:**
> *"ENOUGH."* The word lands like a dropped anvil, in a voice that has given orders to dragons for longer than your species has had a written language. A white-scaled shape the size of a cathedral settles between the combatants, wings still beating dust into the air, and on its neck, small and entirely unbothered, sits a tortle in ceremonial plate. *"You've made your point, children. All of you. Arkhan does not pay for damaged merchandise — stand down, or I will let Obatala finish what you started, and he is considerably less interested in a fair fight than I am."*

**Resolution:** the dragons pull back (any that fled stay fled; any that were about to die simply disengage instead — do not let the math force a kill here). Krull **heals the wounded on both sides** — a genuine field-medicine gesture, not a trick, cast as *cure wounds* / *mass* equivalents from his 14th-level cleric slots. He then extends the offer:

> **READ ALOUD — Krull's offer:**
> *"Arkhan can raise anyone you lost tonight — he keeps that particular courtesy on hand for guests who arrive interesting. Come. He's expecting you, one way or another. Try not to make me regret the healing."*

**Ship-combat notes:** gunners fire the Arcane Cannons as an action (8d10 force); the Aether Harpoon can tether a dragon mid-dive for the ship to focus down, but tethering risks dragging the ship into a dragon's reach — use it to pin the one breathing on the hull, not the one dogfighting a PC's mount. Jeremy's Maintain Core keeps the ship at full power (DC 15 Arcana/Tinker's tools each round); a failed check drops a cannon or speed for the round — exactly the kind of complication that makes this feel like a real fight without threatening the ship's 350 HP pool. **Do not use the Soul Coin Overdrive here** — save that lever for the Escape, where its moral cost actually matters.

## BEAT 2 — The Monument to Tiamat

Krull escorts the party on foot/wingback from the Tower into the **Monument** — a cavern-temple built around the same five-headed statue whose heads just finished testing them in the sky. Arkhan waits at its foot.

> **READ ALOUD — arrival:**
> The Monument swallows daylight that was already dying. Five stone heads rear up out of the dark on a single serpent's neck, each the size of a house, each wearing a crust of old blood that has never quite dried. At the statue's feet, on a throne made of fused dragon bone, sits a dragonborn in obsidian plate, a battleaxe across his knees and a small stoppered vial on a chain at his throat. He does not stand when you enter. He doesn't need to.
>
> *"You survived my welcome. Good — Krull tends to overcorrect when he's embarrassed, and I'd hate for this to end before we've talked. Sit, if you like. Don't, if you'd rather stand ready. Either way — I already know what you want."* He touches the vial at his throat without quite looking at it. *"Tiamat's blood. Everyone who comes here wants Tiamat's blood. The question that actually interests me is what you're willing to give for it."*

**Negotiation — the price:** Arkhan wants the sacrifice of a good-aligned creature in exchange for the vial. He names his price in order:

1. **MOONKITE, first.** He can scent the celestial on Aurora from across the room.

> **READ ALOUD:**
> *"The warlock's little star-beast. I can smell the Upper Planes on it from here — it's practically glowing to anyone who's spent a thousand years around Tiamat's altars. Give me that, and the vial is yours before the hour's out. A fair trade: a caged god's one living celestial, for a dead one's blood."*

2. **If refused — LULU, "the cheaper price."**

> **READ ALOUD:**
> *"No? Sentimental. Fine — I'm not unreasonable." (He smiles like a man doing you a favor.) "The little elephant-thing. The hollyphant. She's smaller, she's quieter, and frankly she's worth less to Tiamat's altar than the star-beast — so I'll even throw in a second vial's worth, call it a volume discount. One good creature's death, your choice which, and we're done here as friends."*

Either refusal without escalation simply stalls the deal — Arkhan doesn't attack over a "no." He gets devious instead.

### Arkhan's devious move (a) — the Gargauth play

Mid-negotiation, Arkhan's innate senses (he deals with fiends and oathbreakers for a living) catch what's bound in Drenwal's Shield.

> **READ ALOUD:**
> Arkhan's eyes flick to the Shield on Drenwal's arm and stay there a beat too long. *"Hm. You carry a passenger, priest. I know that make of prison — old work, proud work. Tell your little exile he and I can speak more plainly than you and I can, if he's willing."*

**DM ONLY:** Arkhan opens a **side bargain with Gargauth** — devil to devil, over Drenwal's head. Gargauth, true to his M7.6 agenda (he wants to be "the god who catches Drenwal," not Tiamat's ally, but he is also patient and opportunistic), **listens** and may pass Arkhan's real terms to Drenwal as a private whisper through the Shield rather than voicing them to the table:

> **READ ALOUD (Gargauth, privately, to Drenwal only):**
> *"He'll take the hollyphant and never miss you again, Drenwal. Or — and he doesn't know I'm telling you this — he'll take Tiamat's blood off the table entirely for a promise of your service down the road, after your 'rebirth.' He thinks he's dealing with me. Let him. It costs you nothing to let two devils think they're clever."*

Play this as Gargauth running his own agenda through Arkhan's opening, not as Gargauth helping the party — he benefits either way the negotiation breaks, because either outcome keeps Drenwal indebted and watching him for guidance.

### Arkhan's devious move (b) — the Krull bait

Arkhan knows Krull covets his place as Tiamat's champion and has for decades. He uses the party as the knife.

**DM ONLY:** At some point in the negotiation — ideally right after the party refuses both sacrifices, or if they stall too long — Arkhan needles Krull in front of everyone:

> **READ ALOUD:**
> *"Krull healed you both tonight, didn't he. Gentle old Krull. He's been gentle a long time — gentle enough that Tiamat's altar hasn't tasted his devotion directly in... what is it now, Krull? A century? She does wonder, sometimes, whether gentleness is a virtue or a vacancy."*

This is engineered to make Krull either (a) overcorrect by turning violently against the party to prove his zeal, or (b) — the **"Treachery!" beat**, per the Eventyr Games template — snap and turn on **Arkhan** instead, finally reaching for the champion's seat himself. Arkhan has read Krull for exactly this and is ready to exploit whichever way he breaks: if Krull turns on the party, Arkhan lets it happen and offers to "mediate" afterward (more leverage over everyone); if Krull turns on **him**, Arkhan uses it as a legitimate excuse to kill Krull mid-negotiation and present it to the party as proof of his own good faith ("you see what disloyalty earns here — I'd never do that to you"). Either branch, Arkhan wins information or leverage. Never let Krull's break resolve the scene for free; it's a complication Arkhan steers, not an escape hatch the party gets for nothing.

## ARKHAN'S STRATEGY — the DM playbook

*Read this before you run the Showdown. It's written to be skimmed mid-combat.*

**What he wants from the party, in order:** (1) the cheapest possible price for the blood — ideally a celestial, ideally not a fight; (2) if forced to fight, to win decisively enough that the party fears him and the Tower stays a known, bargainable quantity rather than an enemy to raze; (3) under no circumstances to lose the Hand of Vecna or Fane-Eater, ever, to anyone, even in defeat.

**The party he's actually fighting (name it, don't genericize it):** **Drenwal** (Light Cleric, the healer — Warding Flare and Radiance of the Dawn are what's keeping the party alive; shutting him down shortens the fight fastest). **Aurora** (Archfey Warlock, airborne on the **Moonkite** — this is the celestial Arkhan already tried to buy in Beat 2; he wants it captured, not killed, so he leans on restrain/grapple against her and the Moonkite rather than full lethal Multiattack where he can help it). **Or'Henn** (Wildfire Druid, Wild Shape, carries the **Soul Capacitor** — a melee threat once he shapeshifts, and a valuable object Arkhan would rather seize than destroy).

**Round 1 — his actual action:** Arkhan's round-1 **action is Multiattack**, three Fane-Eater hits, aimed at **Drenwal** — he wants the healer's output gone before it compounds. His **legendary actions** that same round supplement it: **Command the Line** on Torogar (so Torogar's first hit lands with advantage, boxing in the nearest melee PC) and, if a caster is visibly winding up a control spell, **Hand of Vecna → Vecna's Grip** to shut them down before it resolves.

**Round-by-round priority:**
1. **Lock down control effects first.** Any PC who just landed a hard debuff on him (a banishment attempt, a hold spell) becomes his next Multiattack target — Legendary Resistance (3/day) already soaks the worst of these the instant he fails the save, so this is about punishing the *attempt*, not fearing it.
2. **Target selection by the actual party, in order:** (a) **Drenwal** first, to deny sustain — full Multiattack while he's reachable; (b) **Aurora/Moonkite** second, but **restrain, don't kill** — Vecna's Grip or Command the Line (ordering Torogar to grapple, not gore) whenever he can, because a dead Moonkite is worth nothing and a captured one is still his Beat 2 price; (c) **Or'Henn** third, especially once he Wild Shapes into a melee threat — Fane-Eater's crit rider (extra necrotic, Arkhan heals) is built for exactly this matchup; (d) never Torogar's assigned target unless nothing else is reachable.
3. **Hand of Vecna charge budget (12 total):** spend freely in round 1–2 while at full HP (4–5 charges on *finger of death*/Vecna's Grip to establish dominance early); go conservative once bloodied, reserving at least 3 charges for the *teleport* escape.
4. **Legendary action rotation:** round 1–2, Command the Line + Hand of Vecna; round 3+, mix in Strike whenever a PC is adjacent and undefended.
5. **Team usage.** Torogar is the wall — he never leaves Arkhan's flank unless commanded (and, per above, may be commanded to grapple Aurora instead of gore her). Krull (if still loyal) heals and buffs from range, casting *spirit guardians* on himself near Arkhan. The manticore harries fleeing or ranged PCs. Abishai in the skull-trophies watch and do nothing — **until the party is clearly winning**, at which point 1d4+1 drop in as a correction.
6. **Punishing ranged kiting and flying.** This is the party's natural advantage (ship, Moonkite) and Arkhan's answer is the **lair action** — the statue's breath targets the party and allies only, every round, cycling damage types: a 30-ft cone from one of the five heads, which crown the statue high enough to rake the air above the floor as easily as the floor itself (DC 16, save per head; see the card). Fight near the statue and you are in range. A PC who thinks altitude or range makes them safe eats a 6d6 breath from the ceiling instead. He also keeps Javelin in his kit so "stay at range" doesn't remove him from the fight entirely.
7. **Retreat logic.** Arkhan fights to roughly **half HP (~173)**, then **teleports out** via the Hand of Vecna (3-charge spell) rather than risk Fane-Eater or the Hand to a lucky crit or a banishment that sticks. He does not fight to the death in his own temple — a dead champion is worth nothing to Tiamat, a retreating one can come back with grudges and reinforcements. This is non-negotiable: **he keeps the Hand and the axe no matter what.**
8. **What he does to a fleeing party.** If the party breaks and runs before he's bloodied, he does **not** give chase personally — he sics Torogar (fast, relentless) and the manticore (flying, ranged) on their heels, commands Krull to "collect something interesting" (a downed ally, a dropped item, the Soul Capacitor) if the opportunity presents, and lets the Tower's remaining dragons (Beat 1 survivors) handle the open sky. He stays in the Monument — it's his, and it isn't going anywhere.

## SHOWDOWN

**Trigger:** the party refuses *and* insults Arkhan, tries to steal the vial outright, or attacks first. A simple "no" to the sacrifice does not trigger this — see Beat 2.

**Tone:** lethal, no mercy. This is not a fair fight and the party should probably lose it outright if they slug it out to the end. **The real objective is escape, not victory.** Make that legible at the table early — a PC dropped to 0 here is not merely "down," it is in active danger of being executed where they lie:

> **DM ONLY — downed PCs (RAW, not house rule).** A melee attack against a creature at 0 HP from within 5 ft. is an automatic critical hit, and a critical hit against a creature making death saves counts as **2 failed death saves**, not one. Arkhan's Multiattack is three such attacks: the first hit already deals 2 failed saves, and the second hit (still the same turn) pushes the total to 4 — past the 3 needed to die, before his turn is even over. **Arkhan's Multiattack on a downed PC kills them outright.** Torogar's two scimitars + horns does the same math. This is the pressure that should be driving the table toward the door, not toward one more heroic stand — and it is exactly why "contest the space around the downed PC" (block line of sight/reach, or get them up first) is the only real counter.

**Roster:** Arkhan, Torogar, the manticore, and Krull (if he hasn't already turned per the Krull-bait beat above). Abishai watch from the dragon skulls and only join if the party is winning. Obatala and any surviving tower dragons from Beat 1 are airborne outside, ready to join the Escape.

**The raise-price, post-fight:** Krull does not have *raise dead* — he **brokers Arkhan's innate *raise dead*** (1/day, see the card) for a price he names (a favor, a soul coin, information, service). If the party loses PCs here (or in the Escape), Krull is the one who opens that negotiation, not the one who casts the spell. The 1/day limit is a real constraint — only one body comes back this way per day, so multiple deaths force the party to choose. This is the mercy valve on a fight that's designed to hurt: a death doesn't have to be permanent if they're willing to pay Arkhan's broker, and it keeps Krull as a genuinely ambiguous figure the party might deal with again.

## ESCAPE

The real set-piece. Once the party breaks for the door, run it as a chase: **Monument floor → Tower courtyard/gate → open sky → the Lady Vengeance.** Budget roughly 4–6 rounds total across the three legs.

**Pursuit:** Obatala (full combat stats, not the Beat 1 "calling the halt" version) leads, with any tower dragons still standing from Beat 1 joining as they catch up. Torogar and the manticore harry the ground/low-air legs; Arkhan does not personally chase (see his retreat logic above) unless the party did something that makes this personal (killed Krull in front of him, stole the vial outright, etc.) — in which case he teleports ahead to the ship itself to cut off the escape, once, as a last big scare before letting them go.

**Complications table (roll or pick, one per leg):**

| Roll (d6) | Complication |
|---|---|
| 1 | A tower dragon (Beat 1 survivor) cuts off the direct path — forces a detour or a fight through it. |
| 2 | The Monument's gate begins closing (a defense ward) — a PC must hold it open (contested Strength/Athletics) while others clear it. |
| 3 | Torogar catches the slowest PC — a grapple/trip that costs an action to escape. |
| 4 | Krull (if still neutral/loyal) shouts a genuine warning about a hazard ahead — a rare mercy, use once. |
| 5 | The manticore's tail spikes rain down on the open ground between Tower and ship — area DC save or be slowed. |
| 6 | Clear ground — no complication, bank the tension for the next roll. |

**What Jeremy/the ship can do:** Jeremy brings the Lady Vengeance in low and hot the moment he sees the party running — he doesn't wait for a signal. The Aether Harpoon can snag a fleeing PC mid-fall and reel them aboard (+6 to hit, treat as a rescue action, not an attack). The Arcane Cannons can suppress a pursuing dragon (8d10 force, enough to make Obatala flinch, not enough to seriously threaten it — use it to buy a round, not to win the chase).

**Soul Coin Overdrive — the moral-cost escape button.** If the chase is going badly (a PC about to be caught, the ship about to be grappled), Jeremy can burn a soul coin for the Overdrive: ship weapons +2d10, speed doubled, a 30-ft. force aura, for 1 minute. **This is the panic button, not a free win** — remind the table what spending a coin means (→ campaign primer M7.2: a specific person, annihilated, forever, no judgment, no rest). If a PC calls for it, make them say so out loud. Risk: d20, on a 1–5 the Core backlashes (8d10 to the ship, engines down 1d4 rounds) — using it can make things briefly worse before it makes them better.

## AFTERMATH

Pick the branch that fired and update the Living State Header before you forget.

- **Deal made — Moonkite given.** Devastating for Aurora's arc; the Moonkite thread effectively closes on a tragedy. Arkhan gives the vial cleanly, in full, and is genuinely cordial afterward — he keeps his word to the letter. Gargauth's Bargain ticks up (a devil's deal honored smooths the road for the next one).
- **Deal made — Lulu given.** Lulu's fate becomes a live grief thread for the whole party, especially Or'Henn (who has already lost people to this war) and anyone who's grown attached to her. Same clean vial, same cordial Arkhan. Bhaal's Claim is unaffected; Gargauth's Bargain still ticks up modestly (a bargain is a bargain).
- **Fight and escape, clean-ish.** The party fled with some or all of the Fallen-Three-adjacent leverage intact, no vial, no sacrifice, Arkhan alive and now owed a grudge. Mark the Tower as a **hostile destination** going forward — any return trip starts adversarial. Krull's loyalty status (loyal / turned / dead) is now a live NPC thread — note which.
- **TPK-ish — Krull brokers the raise.** One or more PCs died in the Showdown or the chase. If the party (or their allies) can reach Krull afterward, he brokers **Arkhan's innate *raise dead* (1/day)** for a price he names on the spot — a favor, a soul coin, information about the Fallen Three, or standing as a future debt. The 1/day cap means only one PC comes back this way per day — if more than one died, the party is choosing. Decide now whether Krull extends this out of genuine respect for how they fought, or because a grateful party is a useful one to have owed to him later. Either way, log the debt in the Living State Header.

**In every branch:** note whether the Gargauth side-bargain and the Krull bait actually fired, and what each devil now holds over whom — both are running threads, not one-session beats.
