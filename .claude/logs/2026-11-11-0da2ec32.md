──To get specific, exact movements in Midjourney videos, you need to use Manual Mode, structure your prompts with clear action and camera cues, and fine-tune your motion parameters.
Midjourney's video feature animates static images or uploads using a combination of starting frames, end frames, and text guidance. You can explore the step-by-step workflow detailed in resources like the Midjourney Video Guide and tips on Motion and End Frames.

Step 1: Use Manual Mode Instead of Auto

• Auto Mode: Midjourney guesses how to animate the scene based on its own interpretation of the image. This often leads to unpredictable results.
• Manual Mode: Open your generated or uploaded image, click Animate, and switch from Auto to Manual. This unlocks the prompt box where you can type explicit instructions for what should move.

Step 2: Choose the Right Motion Intensity

• Low Motion (--motion low): Best for subtle actions, portrait stability, gentle environmental shifts (like soft wind or rippling water), and predictable movement.
• High Motion (--motion high): Best for dramatic camera moves or large character actions, though it increases the risk of glitches or unwanted "hallucinations".
• Raw Parameter (--raw): Add --raw to your prompt in manual mode to bypass Midjourney's default stylized aesthetic and stick closer to your literal text instructions.

Step 3: Write Precision Action & Camera Prompts

Vague prompts result in random motion. To get exact movements, use explicit cinematic language:
• Action Verbs & Sequence: Keep initial 5-second clips focused on 1 to 2 clear actions (e.g., "the man picks up the red cup and takes a sip" rather than a crowded sequence of events).
• Camera Control: Specify whether the camera is locked or moving. Use phrases like:
    • "Static shot" or "Fixed camera shot" to keep the background locked.
    • "Slow dolly forward", "Pan right", or "Subtle zoom in" to direct camera movement intentionally.
• Character Restrictions: If you want a character to stay still or not speak, add constraints like "character is silent" or "quiet" to prevent unwanted mouth-flapping or twitching.

Step 4: Use Start and End Frames for Control

• Starting Frame: Select a clean, non-upscaled base image where all characters and key items are clearly visible (you cannot add missing objects later).
• Ending Frame / Loop Control: You can set a specific second image as the End Frame to force the video to transition from point A to point B over the clip's duration, or check the loop settings to create seamless motion.
• Extending Clips: Generate the initial ~5-second clip, then hover over it and click Extend Manual to add a new prompt and keep building the sequence up to ~21 seconds in increments.
