# ELTUREL FALLING

**A living DM primer for *Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus* — heavily homebrewed · D&D 5e · a party of three at Level 11.**

---

## How to use this living primer

This is a **sandbox reference, refreshed each session — not a script.** The players go where they want; your job is to hold the world steady and let it react. Everything below serves that.

- **One volatile section.** Only the **Living State Header** is edited each session — location, clocks, influence, ship status, party snapshot. Every other section is stable and points back to the header for any live value. If you find a date, countdown, or "current" fact ticking anywhere *but* the header, it is a bug — move it there.
- **A web, not a rail.** Every site in **Chapter 3** is enterable right now at level 11. Play gates on **KEYS and INFORMATION, never sequence.** Skip a key (the compass, the writ, Eldra's signet, the adamantine rods, Kreeg's living hand) and the party still gets in — they just pay in blood, coin, or a buried clause. Never a locked door; improvise the price.
- **Two voices, never mixed.** DM prose is blunt and imperative. Player-facing text sits in blockquotes marked **"READ ALOUD:"** — read those aloud; never read a DM note or a **DM ONLY:** line to the table. All read-aloud text here is original.
- **Glyphs = live state at a glance.** ✅ done / held · ⚠️ live / ticking · ❌ lost / dead · ❓ unknown to the party.
- **Beats fire once, on events.** The gut-punches in **Revelations** trigger on things that *happen* — a first entry, a clock expiry, a roll — never on a menu choice, so each lands on any route. Mark it fired; it never repeats.
- **Run the table from the DM Run-Kit.** Do the 5-minute pre-flight, advance time with the clock engine, and when pacing sags drop a front move or a flashpoint.
- **The spine, in one sentence.** When the table drifts, come back to it: **free Elturel.** Every arc — Or'Henn's staff hunt, Drenwal's cure, Aurora's Moonkite, Asimov's ledger of soul-bounties — is a tributary feeding that one river. How any arc *ends the campaign* lives in **The Main Quest**.

## THE STORY SO FAR

Elturel — the City of the Second Sun — did not fall by accident, and it did not fall by the Sword. It was **sold.** High Overseer **Thavius Kreeg**, in a moment of desperation history has yet to name, set his signature and the city's own **Creed Resolute** to a devil's contract, and the ground opened. The city and every soul in it were dragged whole into Avernus, the first layer of the Nine Hells, to hang beneath a bleeding sky while chains haul it down, degree by degree, toward the river Styx — touch that black water and every soul aboard becomes a devil forever. Zariel's blade — the **Sword of Zariel**, the fallen angel's own weapon of ascension — is the counter-lever: long ago it was carried *out* of Hell by the paladin **Yael** and a hollyphant named **Lulu**, who spent their lives, their magic, and their very memories to raise the **Bleeding Citadel** as its vault. Yael is dust. Lulu is not. She rides with this party now, an amnesiac little elephant of gold, and every fragment of memory she recovers is another mile of the road back to the Sword. The party does not yet know Kreeg's name is on the deed. They only know the city is dying, and something down here can save it.

## THE MAIN QUEST — Free Elturel (Citadel, Zariel's Sword, the Fallen Three, Kreeg's contract)

**The spine. Everything in M4–M9 is a tributary that feeds one of the two levers below. When the table drifts, come here: this is the map of how any arc ends the campaign.**

Elturel hangs in the red sky of Avernus on chains of light, and the chains are failing. The city was sold — not conquered, *sold* — by High Overseer Thavius Kreeg, who signed its people to Zariel to spare himself a debt. To free it, the party pulls on one or both of two levers: **the Sword** that severs the chains by force (Zariel's own blade, hidden in the Bleeding Citadel), and **the Contract** that unwinds the sale by law (Kreeg's instrument of sale — a *copy* the party can reach now, an *original* Zariel keeps for the end). Neither lever is required alone. How many the party brings, and which, decides how cleanly Elturel comes home — that arithmetic lives in **The Victory Tiers** below, and it is the one place this document rules with a closed fist.

> **★ THE EMOTIONAL KEY — read this before anything else in the endgame.** The road to the Sword is **Lulu**. The hollyphant did not merely witness the Sword's hiding — she *performed* it. The paladin **Yael** and Lulu carried the blade out of the Nine Hells and, to keep it from Zariel forever, sacrificed everything to raise the **Bleeding Citadel** around it: Yael gave her *life*, Lulu gave her *magic* and her *memory*. Lulu's amnesia is not a wound to work around — it is the lock. Her returning fragments **are** the map to the Citadel and the Sword. The campaign's climax is a broken little elephant remembering what she paid to hide. Weight every Lulu beat accordingly.

## CHAPTER 3 — AVERNUS: THE BURNING WASTES

> **READ ALOUD:** There is no sun here — only a sky the color of a wound that will not close, and light that comes from everywhere and warms nothing. The ground is cracked black glass and drifting ash, and it goes to the horizon in every direction you turn. Far off, dust-plumes crawl in long lines where war-machines grind toward a battle no one remembers starting. The river you can smell before you see it: the Styx, sluggish and grey, muttering the names of everyone who ever drank from it. And overhead — chained to the sky by links each as long as a keep is tall — hangs Elturel. Your city. It turns slowly on its chains, close enough that you can count the dark streets where the lamps have gone out, and it sits lower today than it did yesterday. Not falling. *Sinking.* Patient as a stone in water. Somewhere under all that iron and fire, people are still praying, and the prayers are still going nowhere.

Avernus is the first layer of the Nine Hells — the Blood War's front line, a wasteland of basalt flats, blood pools, wrecked fortresses, and roving devil legions. It has weather (ash-storms, firefalls), it has factions (Zariel's armies, demon incursions up from the Abyss, scavenger warlords, the Emporium's neutral commerce), and it has one law above all others: **nothing here is free, and everything is watching.** The party is at level 11, well-armed, ship-borne, and known. That is enough to survive Avernus. It is not enough to leave it. Leaving requires the Sword.

### 3.1 — HOW TO RUN THIS CHAPTER: THE SANDBOX

**This chapter is a sandbox, not a track.** Every site in the gazetteer (Ch.3.5 and the location index below) is enterable **right now**. Nothing is locked behind a sequence, a level, or a previous site. The only gates are:

- **What the party KNOWS** — a location they've never heard of can't be a destination yet (mark ❓). They learn sites from NPCs, omens, drag-marks, and Lulu's returning memory.
- **What the party HOLDS** — some doors need a key, a signet, a writ, or a name. (Eldra's signet talks Olanthius down; Kreeg's living hand voids the contract; the Solar's Helmet + Lulu's memory finds the Cage. See Ch.3 keys / M4.)

**The DM's job is consequences, not direction.** The party declares where they go; you narrate the road, fire the omens, resolve the front moves, and let the world react. Do **not** herd them toward Bel's Forge because it's "next." Bel is the *standing* next main-quest lead — but if they'd rather chase Or'Henn's staff, race the Rust Queen to the Fortress, or walk into the Crypt cold, that is the correct campaign. The clocks (Ch.3.3) supply all the pressure the plot needs; you supply the reactive world.

> **DM ONLY — the one nudge you're allowed:** if the table stalls, don't pick their destination — hand them a *reason*. Fire an omen, have an NPC arrive with a lead, or advance a front (Ch.3.3). Pressure, not steering.

### 3.2 — TRAVEL BY OMEN, NEVER BY MILES

**Avernus has no fixed distances.** Do not track miles, hexes, or exact hours. Give travel as **days + an omen** — a supernatural sign that hints at what the party is walking toward. A place announces itself before it is seen: the Weeping Furrow moans three times over the horizon before the drag-marks even resolve. Use omens as pacing beats, and use them to *seed the destination* so arrival feels earned.

**Per leg:** state the day-count from the route table, then roll 1d8 (or pick) on the omens table. Between omens, resolve one front move (Ch.3.3) or a random encounter if the pace sags.

#### THE NEUTRALITY LAW (read this first — it governs the whole site)

The Emporium runs on Mahadi's charter of protected commerce. **Drawing steel or casting a hostile spell inside its bounds voids your protection** — and everyone else's over you.

| If a PC starts violence… | Consequence |
|---|---|
| First hostile act | Mahadi's wards flare; the aggressor is **marked** (a brand of cold light on the back of the hand, DC 20 to remove). Every stallholder and their muscle may now act against the marked without breaking the peace. |

| Killing a protected guest (incl. "Vessper Hain") | Protection collapses table-wide. Kreeg — if still alive and present — flees that instant (revelation #2 goes mobile and cold). Every contract Fhet'Ahla sealed becomes contestable. |

> **DM ONLY:** This is the leash on a party that might want to just grab Kreeg and go. It is not a "no." It is a *price*. Let them feel the wards hum when a hand drifts to a hilt. Violence here is always available and always ruinous.

#### The approach — the slag mountains {#bel-approach}

The Forge announces itself long before the party arrives. Travel from the Emporium: **2–4 days** across the plains; the horizon glows orange from a day out and never dims.

> **READ ALOUD:** For a day now the sky ahead has not gone dark. There is a false dawn stitched permanently to the horizon — a bruise of orange that pulses like something breathing. By the second day you can taste it: iron filings on the tongue, the flat mineral stink of a smithy the size of a country. The ground stops being ground. It becomes slag — cooled black glass ridged like old scar tissue, warm through your boots, and where it cracks, the crack glows. Somewhere ahead a hammer falls. You feel it in your teeth before you hear it. Then another. And another. A heartbeat with an anvil for a chest.

**Terrain (approach):** the slag ridges are difficult terrain in patches; fresh cracks vent heat. Anyone who ends a turn on a **glowing seam** takes **1d6 fire** (telegraph the glow — it's a hazard, not a gotcha). No random encounter is mandatory here; if wanted, roll once on the M-hub Avernus table.

**On sight of the Forge itself:**

> **READ ALOUD:** It is not a building. It is a mountain that someone hollowed out and set on fire from the inside. A furnace-fortress squats over a lake of magma, ten thousand chimneys venting soot into a sky that has forgotten stars. Chains as thick as your leg run out of the walls and down into the glow, and on those chains hang shapes — pale, weeping, human-sized — turning the great bellows with the slow patience of the damned. Fire giants the height of siege towers work the anvils and do not look up. And over all of it, on a throne of iron they let cool just enough to sit on, waits the thing that used to own this whole burning layer of Hell.

#### Area B — The work-halls & THE TRIAL {#bel-trial}

Bel does not grant audience to anyone who cannot survive his workforce. The fire-giant gauntlet is his filter. **He tests whether the party is worth his time — not to kill them.** He *may* call it off the moment they impress him, and he is entertained by cleverness as much as by force.

**The gauntlet:** **4× Fire Giant** (MM, CR 9 each — AC 18, HP 162, greatsword +11 / 28, rock +11 / 29; **fire immune**). Add a **Forge-heat aura**: 1d6 fire to anyone who starts its turn within 10 ft of a giant.

> **READ ALOUD — the trial begins:** A giant sets down its hammer. The sound of it hitting the anvil-plate is like a door slamming in a cathedral. It turns, and its eyes are two forge-coals in a face like a cliff. It does not draw a weapon. It picks one up — a sword you could use as a gangplank — and speaks, in a Giant so slow it feels geological: *"The Warlord watches. Show him you are worth the cooling of his iron. **How to run it for an L11 party of 3 + allies:**

| Mode | How | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Two waves of two** ⚔️ | 2 giants, then 2 more when the first pair is bloodied/down. Call it the instant a PC hits 0 OR the second wave is bloodied → *"Enough. You'll do."* | Party wants the fight; allies (Veska, Ser Lucan, gith monk) field to balance the action economy |

A druid who commands fire *impresses* fire giants. | Or'Henn's spotlight; rewards his kit thematically |

**DM ONLY:** any single bypass is enough — do not stack them. If the party fields Veska and Lucan and still tries to bypass socially, let the *presence* of two known Hellriders grant advantage on the check. The trial's job is a strong opening beat and a read on the party, not a resource-drain grind.

### The Crypt of the Hellriders

> **Chapter 3 — Avernus.** ◻️ UNVISITED. The Sword-road's second lever. If the party never comes here, the path to the Bleeding Citadel leans wholly on Bel's Forge and Lulu's own recovering memories — this site is the shortcut and the emotional detonation.

**What it is (DM only).** A pocket of Elturel that fell into Avernus with the city and did *not* rot the way the rest did. It is the memorial crypt of the Hellriders — the order that first charged into Hell behind Zariel. The dead-magic cold here is not infernal; it is *grief that refuses to burn*. Devils avoid it. So does Zariel. It holds one occupant: **Olanthius**, a death knight, kneeling at an empty sarcophagus, guarding a road no one has walked in a hundred years.

**Why the party comes.** Someone points them here — Lulu's fragmentary dread of "the man who watched," Kreeg/Vessper hedging on how the Sword left Hell, or Veska naming the crypt as the Crimson Watch's old shrine. Olanthius knows two things nobody else living will say plainly: **the road to the Bleeding Citadel**, and **why the Sword was hidden in the first place.**

#### Approach & Entry

One pace forward, and it is *cold* — a clean, church-stone cold that has nothing to do with Hell. Ahead, a doorway of grey Elturgard granite stands upright and unbroken, as if the city tore this one building loose whole and set it down gently. Above the lintel, worn but legible: a rider, a lance, and a rising sun. No fire has touched it. Nothing here wants to.

**DM ONLY:** The threshold is a soft ward — Zariel's own devils cannot cross it, and infernal effects (Avernus's fear-heat, wandering imps) do not follow the party inside. Anyone who worshipped or served a martial/honor god (Drenwal, Veska, Lucan) feels it as *recognition*, not threat.

#### Area 1 — The Nave of Names (the name-walls)

Two long walls of granite carved top to bottom with names — the fallen of the first Charge and every Hellrider since who died in Elturel's service. Cold candles that will not light. A floor of grey flagstone worn smooth by knees.

| Feature | Mechanic / Payoff |
|---|---|
| Finding **ELDRA VASH** among the First Charge | DC 12 Investigation to comb the walls; **auto-success if the party already knows to look for her** (from the Ashen Garden grave / signet) |
| The Crimson Watch panel | A later, separate section — Drenwal's father's order, enshrined here. See the tomb note below. |
| Rubbing / speaking a name aloud | No mechanic — but see the **Lulu trigger** and the **signet moment** |

**DM ONLY — the Eldra Vash thread.** Eldra Vash rode in the First Charge and died there. Her Crimson Watch signet was recovered by the party at the **Ashen Garden** grave (Or'Henn's razed estate — Kreeg's old sanctuary). Her name on this wall, or her signet in hand, is the master key to Olanthius (see Area 3). #### Area 2 — The Hellrider Tombs & the Crimson Watch Shrine

Stone effigies of armored riders lie atop their tombs, lances across their chests, carved mid-charge in the frieze above — the eternal charge, forever a half-second from impact. One alcove is set apart: the **Crimson Watch**, the order Drenwal's father served.

**DM ONLY — live emotional site (Drenwal).** This is the shrine of Drenwal's father's order. Drenwal is a Bhaalspawn who killed his brother Dumal; the Watch's fall to Bhaal-cult rot is *his* inheritance, spoken aloud in stone. Play this before Olanthius if you can — let Drenwal read his father's order's honor roll in a room that still holds the line, then learn how it ended.

Olanthius, once engaged (Area 3), will confirm the rot: the **Crimson Watch was hollowed from within by a Bhaal cult** in the years after the Charge — the same infection that touches Veska's and Lucan's histories. He watched good riders die for an order already gutted from the inside. This is offered as truth, not accusation — but Drenwal may take it either way. (Ties to Veska/Lucan lore; if either ally is present, give them a line.)

READ ALOUD (Crimson Watch alcove):

The crimson enamel on the shield-boss has flaked to the colour of dried blood. Someone has left an offering on the plinth: a single soldier's ration-token, so old it has fused to the stone.

**Treasure here.** Among the tombs (DC 15 Investigation, or freely if Olanthius allows it): a **+1 lance** enshrined with the First Charge dead, and a **Hellrider's Aegis** — a battered shield that grants advantage on saves vs. the *frightened* condition while in Avernus (homebrew, thematic; adjust to a standard *+1 shield* if you prefer). Taking anything without Olanthius's leave is a grave insult and forfeits the "advantage on social checks" state below.

#### Area 3 — The Empty Sarcophagus (Olanthius)

At the crypt's heart, a death knight kneels before an open, empty sarcophagus. No name is carved on it. His armor is Hellrider steel gone black; a cold green fire lives behind his visor. He does not rise when the party enters. He has knelt here a hundred years.

**Whose tomb is it?** His own. He carved it, opened it, and could not lie down in it — because a death knight who has not atoned cannot be buried among the honored dead. **He buried his honor here instead of his body.** That is the whole of his despair.

READ ALOUD (his first line):
> He does not turn. The voice comes out of the green light like something dragged up a long, cold well. "You've come a long way to look at a grave with no one in it. That's fitting. I've knelt at it a hundred years and found the same." A pause. The kneeling figure's gauntlets tighten on the sarcophagus lip. "If you've come to be forgiven for something, you're in the wrong crypt. There's no forgiveness cut into these walls. Only names. Say your business, or leave me my names."

##### Olanthius — the guilty WITNESS (canon)

He is **not** the one who hid the Sword and he did **not** steal it. He *watched* the paladin **Yael** and the hollyphant **Lulu** carry the Sword of Zariel out of Hell — and he did nothing. He let them go, then stayed to guard the road they took, telling himself it was duty. It was cowardice dressed as vigilance. He knows:

- **The road to the Bleeding Citadel** — where Yael and Lulu went, and how to follow.
- **Why the Sword was hidden** — *"to save the world from what she became."* (Zariel. The Sword is her old blade; wielding it is a covenant with what she is now.)
- **The Coram plant** — he SAW the star fall (see below).

##### THE KEY — Eldra Vash's signet / her name

Eldra Vash was his. Her death in the First Charge is the wound under everything. Presenting her **Crimson Watch signet** (from the Ashen Garden) or **speaking her name** cracks the century of stone.

READ ALOUD (the signet moment — make this land; top-3 scene):
> You hold out the signet. The green fire in the visor *gutters* — for one instant it is not a death knight's flame at all but something smaller, older, a man's grief with a man's face behind it. His gauntlet rises, stops, will not touch it. "Where," he says, and it is not a question, it is a wound. "Where did you— that's *hers.* She wore it into the Charge. I told her the sun would hold. I told her Zariel would hold." The great black shape bows until his brow nearly touches the empty stone. "I have guarded a road for a hundred years so I would not have to remember the face of the woman who rode it first. And you carry her ring to me like it's *nothing.*" When he lifts his head, the flame has steadied — but it has changed. "Ask. Ask me anything. I am done being a locked door."

**Mechanical effect of the key:** advantage on **all** social checks with Olanthius, and he speaks *plainly* — no riddles, no toll. He gives up the road to the Citadel, the reason the Sword was hidden, and the Coram plant freely.

##### THE LULU TRIGGER (do not miss this)

If **Lulu is present** when Olanthius speaks — especially at the signet moment or when he names Yael — it fires **one of her largest memory fragments.** She *was there.* She carried the Sword past this very man.

READ ALOUD (Lulu's fragment):
> The little hollyphant goes very still. Her trunk lowers. And then, in a voice that is not her usual bright chirp but something ancient and cracked open: "...*You.* You were at the gate. You stood in the red light and you *let us pass.* Yael said your name. Yael said— she said, 'He won't stop us, he hasn't the heart left to stop anything,' and she was *crying* when she said it—" Lulu's small body trembles. "I remember the *weight* of it. The Sword. It was so heavy and Yael's hands were burning and I said I'd carry the memory so she wouldn't have to and I *did,* I took it, I took all of it and I made myself forget so no one could ever pull the road out of me—"

**DM ONLY:** This is a permanent Lulu progression beat — after this, she recovers the *route framing* to the Bleeding Citadel (she carried it, buried under her own forgetting; Olanthius's presence is the shovel). Even if the party never gets the signet, **the Lulu trigger fires on hearing Olanthius reference Yael or the gate** — it is a witness-recognition, not a bribe. This is the single strongest reason to bring Lulu here.

##### THE CORAM PLANT (he volunteers this with the key, or on a DC 16 Insight if he warms at all)

READ ALOUD:
> "There is one more thing, and I don't have a name for it. Near the end — after Zariel fell, before the city — a star came down. Not a devil's fire. A *small* light, gentle, the kind of thing that stops to help a dying man it has no reason to help. Zariel *hated* it. I have never seen her hate anything the way she hated that little falling mercy. She bleeds it still — I've heard the Hellriders who kept faith whisper that where Zariel's own blood falls, the mercy she could not forgive grows back. I don't know what it is. I know she'd unmake creation to be rid of it."

**DM ONLY:** This is the **Coram-of-Small-Mercies** seed — the homebrew redemption deity, the Drenwal-rebirth candidate. Olanthius doesn't know the name; he knows the *shape* and that **Zariel bleeds it.** Track this as the party's first hard confirmation Coram exists and is reachable.

#### If the party does NOT have the key

No signet, no name — Olanthius is a locked door, but not a hostile one. He gives nothing for free.

**Path A — Move him socially.** DC 18 (Persuasion or a genuine, roleplayed appeal to duty/honor; grief-bait fails and hardens him). Success gets the road and the "why," but *not* the plain warmth — he stays clipped and the Coram plant needs a further DC 16 Insight.

**Path B — The Worthiness Trial.** He offers one of two, party's choice:
- **The Vigil.** Stand watch at the empty sarcophagus through one full "night" of Avernus (a long rest's worth) without leaving, without drawing steel, letting the crypt's grief press on you. Each character makes a DC 14 Wisdom save at the end; **on any two successes among the party**, Olanthius judges them steady enough to trust and opens as if keyed. (No combat. This is a patience/faith test — reward players who lean in.)
- **The Half-Heart Duel.** He rises and fights — but at **half heart**, testing whether they fight with honor, not to see them die. **Olanthius is a death knight, CR 17** (statblock: *Monster Manual* death knight; run him with legendary-tier caution against an L11 party — he fights defensively, pulls killing blows, uses *Hellfire Orb* only as a warning shot, never on downed PCs). **Killing him is NOT the intended path.** If the party fights *with* honor — accepts a yield, doesn't gang a downed foe, sheathes when he sheathes — he ends the duel himself and opens up. If they play it as a murder, he fights to the death.

**DM ONLY — the cost of destroying him.** If Olanthius is destroyed, **his knowledge dies with him.** The road to the Bleeding Citadel, the "why the Sword was hidden," and the Coram plant are all lost from this source; the Citadel route **defaults to Bel's Forge + Lulu's memories only** (slower, and Lulu's recovery is incomplete without the witness-recognition trigger). Make the party feel this as a real loss, not a scolding. His sword (*a Hellrider's blade, functions as a +2 longsword, sheds cold blue light*) and his blackened Hellrider plate remain — small consolation.

He names it grimly — "you don't befriend the thing in the Maw, you point it" — and warns that using Kostchtchie as a lever is a fire that burns the hand that lights it.

### Kostchtchie's Maw

> **Status:** ◻️ Unvisited · **Optional lever** · A bargain, not a dungeon. Pull it if the party wants a knife at Zariel's back for the endgame — ignore it with no penalty.

**What it is (DM ONLY):** An impossible glacier heaved up out of the fire-plain, steaming where frost meets brimstone. Frozen inside it, mid-roar, is **Kostchtchie, demon lord of the frost giants** — a would-be usurper Zariel caught, iced, and pinned as a trophy and a warning. He is not asleep. He is awake in there, every second, and his mind hammers at anyone who wanders within a mile: *FREE ME.* Zariel keeps him because a leashed rival is worth more than a dead one; she has never gotten around to killing him and now can't be bothered. That neglect is the party's opening.

**The lever, stated plainly:** Free Kostchtchie and he goes to war on Zariel. He is not an ally, not grateful, not steerable — but his war divides her attention, and that division is worth real mechanical relief at the Ch.5 climax. He will also, eventually, cost the party something. Both halves are true. Sell neither short.

> **READ ALOUD:**
> The heat of Avernus dies against it before you see it — a cold that walks out to meet you across the ash. Then it clears the ridge: a mountain of ice where no ice should be, blue-white and sweating steam into the red sky, groaning as fire and frost fight along its skin. Frozen dead stud the lower slopes like flies in amber — devils, giants, a knight still gripping his hammer, mouths all open in the same silent word. And deep in the glacier's heart, huge and dim and mid-bellow, something looks back at you. It does not speak with a voice. It puts the word directly behind your eyes, and the word is a hand closing on your skull: *FREE ME. FREE ME. FREE ME.*

---

#### Part 1 — The Approach (frozen dead + the killing cold)

The glacier's aura is the first test. Everyone within it fights Avernus in reverse: the fire-plain's heat gone, replaced by a cold that gnaws.

| Hazard | Mechanic |
|--------|----------|
| **Killing cold** | Each hour within ~1 mile: DC 12 CON save or 1 level of exhaustion. Advantage with cold-resistance, fire immunity, or `Cinderbloom`-warmth (Or'Henn radiates heat — grant allies within 10 ft advantage; roleplay the old dragonborn as the party's furnace). |
| **The frozen dead** | The slopes are lined with prior claimants iced mid-attempt. Loot is real (below) but the ice is unstable — DC 13 DEX/Athletics to free a corpse's gear without triggering a slide (2d6 bludgeoning + prone, DC 13 DEX halves). |
| **The word** | The telepathic *FREE ME* is constant. After 1 hour inside the aura, a PC must make a DC 13 WIS save or suffer disadvantage on Insight checks against Kostchtchie (Part 3) — his voice softens their judgment. Long rests inside the aura don't clear it. |

**DM ONLY — the corpses tell the story.** A PC who examines the dead (DC 14 Investigation) sees the pattern: every one of them died *reaching in*, not fighting out. Whatever they came to bargain for, the ice took them the moment they touched an anchor wrong or trusted the voice. This is the honest foreshadow — the party has been warned, in bodies.

> **Treasure — the frozen claimants:** a *frost brand* longsword still in a giant's fist; 3 soul coins (see the glacier hook below — do NOT let these read as ordinary loot); a signet of a dead Sinister ambassador worth 250 gp to the right buyer at the Wandering Emporium; and, on the knight-corpse, a sealed but water-ruined map to **Kostchtchie's Maw itself** — proof others sold this location as a "weapon against Zariel." It is being sold as a weapon because it is one. It is being *found frozen to a corpse* because it is also a trap.

---

#### Part 2 — The Binding (three ice-anchors, sunk by Zariel's own hand)

Kostchtchie is pinned by three colossal spikes of black-veined ice driven through the glacier and into the bedrock of Avernus. Zariel set them personally; her sigil crawls across each one, slowly, like frost forming. Break all three and the prison fails.

Each anchor can be broken two ways — and the irony is the point.

| Anchor | Break by FORCE | Break by RITUAL |
|--------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Anchor of the North Root** | AC 17, 100 HP, **immune to cold, immune to fire** (Zariel warded her own trophy against the plane it sits in), vulnerable to force/thunder/bludgeoning from adamantine | DC 17 Arcana **or** Religion, 10 min |
| **Anchor of the Deep Root** | as above | DC 17 Arcana **or** Religion, 10 min |
| **Anchor of the Chained Root** | as above | DC 17 Arcana **or** Religion, 10 min |

> **DM ONLY — the fire immunity is the joke and the warning.** A party that assumes "demon prison in Hell = burn it down" will waste a turn discovering the anchors laugh at fire. Or'Henn's whole kit is fire. Let him land one gout, watch it *feed* the frost, and reframe: this thing was built by someone who knew exactly who might come. Pivot them to adamantine weapons (the party wants the **Wrecked Flying Fortress** rods anyway — hook), thunder magic, or the ritual.

**Ritual failure = the hoarfrost wave.** Each failed ritual roll (or each round the party spends smashing an anchor) draws Zariel's frozen wardens out of the ice. Roll on the pressure they choose:

| Wave | Statblock (Open5e / MM) | Notes |
|------|-------------------------|-------|
| Standard | **Ice Devil** (CR 14) — reflavor as a hoarfrost sentinel | 1 per failed anchor; brutal for L11, use as a clock not a grind |
| Lighter | **Frost Salamander** (CR 9) ×2 | swarming, mobile, good for pressure without a TPK |
| Nastier finale | 1 Ice Devil + 2 Frost Salamanders on the **third** anchor | Zariel's binding does not die quietly |

Treat the whole of Part 2 as a **skill challenge under fire**: the party is racing the ritual clock while wardens spawn. The tension is "break all three before we're overrun," not "clear a room."

---

#### Part 3 — The Bargain (the price behind the price)

When the last anchor cracks, the glacier does not shatter — it *breathes*. Kostchtchie's mind stops hammering and starts *talking*, and for the first time the word is not FREE ME. It is an offer.

> **READ ALOUD:**
> The pressure behind your eyes eases all at once, and what replaces it is worse: patience. The thing in the ice tilts its enormous frozen skull, and when it speaks the cold goes reasonable, almost warm. *"You feel her, don't you. Up on her hill of iron, so certain. Free me and I will burn her sky. I will pull that fallen light down by its wings and put my hands around the throat of her war. You want her looking anywhere but at you — I am the only thing in this pit she is afraid of. Break the last root. I will do the rest."*

**The Insight beat.** A PC may read him: **DC 18 Insight** (disadvantage if they failed the Part 1 WIS save — his voice already got under their skin).

| Insight result | What the PC learns |
|----------------|--------------------|
| **≥18** | Every word is *true* — he means to burn Zariel's sky. And he means *everything else too*: the party is a tool he will discard, their allies are collateral, "the rest" includes them if they're standing in his way when he's out. He is not lying. He is simply telling only the useful half. |
| **11–17** | He's sincere about hating Zariel. Whether he's safe is unreadable. |
| **≤10** | He seems like exactly the ally you need. |

**DM ONLY:** There is no gotcha here — a party that rolls well *knows the deal is real and the price is real*, and can choose it eyes-open. That's the whole design. Kostchtchie kept is a lever they didn't pull; Kostchtchie freed is a lever that cuts both ways and they knew it.

---

#### Payoff — what freeing him actually buys

Freed, Kostchtchie storms off to make war on Zariel. Bank **one** of the following for the endgame (DM/party choice, declared when relevant):

- **Skip one Citadel-window penalty** — a timing/attrition cost the party would otherwise eat during Ch.4's Bleeding Citadel run is waived; Zariel's forces are pulled off to meet the frost lord.
- **Downgrade one Zariel-pressure encounter** at the endgame — drop a wave, halve reinforcements, or knock a solo threat down a tier.

**And, at the Ch.5 climax, name it as a battlefield modifier:** *Divided Attention* — Zariel's forces fight at a distraction. Once per round the DM may not use her Legendary Resistance / a lair action of choice, because a frost-giant warhost just breached her flank. Describe the far horizon burning blue.

---

#### Honest price — the later consequence (schedule ONE)

Kostchtchie is neither grateful nor controllable. Before the campaign ends, spend one of these:

- **His warband hits a site the party loves.** Fort Knucklebone, the Wandering Emporium, or the **Lady Vengeance** herself takes a frost-giant raid off-screen or on. Someone the party protected pays.
- **He demands tribute.** A messenger of ice arrives: hand over a relic, a soul, or a service — or the next battlefield modifier flips *against* them (he pulls his war back to spite them).
- **He remembers Or'Henn.** If Or'Henn's fire touched the glacier, Kostchtchie marks him. A personal grudge — the frost lord's champions come hunting the "little ember dragon" who dared warm his prison.

> **DM ONLY:** Make the consequence land *after* the payoff pays off. The party should feel the lever was worth it and *then* feel the bill arrive. That's the texture of dealing with a demon lord — no free wins in Avernus.

---

#### Soul-coin hook — the coined dead in the ice (Or'Henn / Asimov)

Studded through the glacier, frozen alongside devils and giants, are **coined souls** — victims Kostchtchie's captors or prior claimants left embedded. The moment Or'Henn comes within the aura, the **Soul Capacitor itches**: Asimov's relayed soul-bounty sense flares, pointing at the ice like a compass to grief.

- Or'Henn (or any PC) can spend an action + DC 15 Arcana/Religion to work a coined soul free of the ice and free the soul (via `Lulu` / `Moonkite` / `Jeremy`, per the soul-coin rules). Each freed soul is a coin *spent to liberation, not annihilation*.
- **Every soul freed here earns celestial goodwill** and feeds the **Ch.5 freed-souls mechanic** — bank each one. Lulu will *feel* it; Moonkite's light steadies. This is one of the few places in Avernus a coin dies clean.

> **READ ALOUD (a coin, freed):**
> The coin comes loose from the ice with a sound like a held breath let go. For one heartbeat there is a face in it — a stranger, or someone's mother, or no one — and then it's warm in your palm, then it's light, then it's gone up through the red sky in a thread of gold that even the fire-plain can't quite swallow. Somewhere very close, Lulu's ears prick, and she makes a small, glad sound.

**DM ONLY:** If Or'Henn frees souls here but the party leaves Kostchtchie bound, that's fine — the soul-coin payoff and the demon-lord lever are independent. A party can raid the glacier for coins and *walk away from the bargain entirely.*

---

#### If never visited

No downside. An unpulled lever. Kostchtchie stays iced and roaring, Zariel keeps her full attention on the party through Ch.4 and Ch.5, and the endgame runs at standard pressure. The coined souls stay frozen. The party never knows what the steaming glacier on the horizon was — only that something cold was screaming out there, and they had bigger fires to put out.

### The Wrecked Flying Fortress

> ◻️ **UNVISITED — full detail.** A Zariel war-engine that fell out of the sky and never finished dying. It lies canted across a plain of black volcanic glass, one wing driven into the ground to the shoulder, the whole hull tilted maybe thirty degrees so every deck reads like a ship heeling in a storm. Debris still drifts *upward* from it toward a puckered wound in the sky where it came through. **Nothing aboard has been told it crashed.** Turrets still track. A conduit still hums. Somewhere in the dark below-decks a war-machine is still, technically, at its post.

> **THE DOUBLE PAYOFF — SAY IT PLAINLY: the adamantine control rods in this wreck pay BOTH debts at once. One raid satisfies Bel's price AND repairs the crippled Lady Vengeance (restores full flight + the second force cannon + Soul Coin Overdrive). Do not split this into two fetch quests.**

**PINNED RULING — the one-set / two-claimants problem (resolve it exactly this way):** A fortress this size ran a *redundant bank* of control rods — a primary array plus cold spares, the way any war-engine carries backups. The engine-well holds **more than one usable set.** The Lady Vengeance needs a **matched pair** to fly whole; **Bel wants the remainder** as his toll and his leverage-material against Zariel (he does not need working ones — he needs *Zariel's* adamantine with her forge-marks on it). So: **one raid, enough rods for both, no contradiction.** If a player asks "won't Bel take the ship's rods?" — no. Bel appraises the box and takes his cut of the haul; he explicitly does not steal (per the Forge entry). Jeremy claims the matched pair for the refit; Bel takes the surplus and the cipher-rod for his intelligence value. Both parties leave satisfied. Do not let the table talk you into inventing a shortage.

> **READ ALOUD:** The wound in the hull hangs above you like the mouth of a cave that used to be a warship. Torn adamantine peels back in curls the size of doors. Cold air sighs out of it, and with the air comes a smell you know from the Palace of Gore — old blood gone to iron. Everything inside is tilted. The first handhold is a snapped strut, slick with something that hasn't dried in a very long time.

### The Tower(s) of Urm

### The Mirror of Mephistar

> **Chapter 3 — Avernus Hexcrawl · Optional Intermediary · ◻️ UNVISITED**
> A rival archdevil's answering service. Never a place you *must* go — a valve you can open if the party wants archdevil-grade certainty about Urexiel's vault, the contract law, or the Solar's Helmet. Skip it entirely and nothing breaks; the same truths surface slower elsewhere.

**What it is.** Mephistopheles, Lord of Cania, does not descend to Avernus for petitioners. ### The Ashen Garden

**Reaching it.** The Garden is a dead terrace-estate off the ash-flats, decades older than Elturel's fall. No wards live anymore. From the sky it reads as a pale scar — collapsed terraces drifted over with ash so fine it holds a footprint like snow and gives it back to you on the walk out.

> **READ ALOUD:** You know this soil. Before your foot finds it, before the ash lifts, some older part of you has already recognized the smell of this place — green things burned and then rained on, over and over, until the burning became the smell of the green. **DM ONLY — run this site through Or'Henn's eyes.** Grief first, loot second. Let the other PCs stand back a beat. Every mechanical beat below is a wound with a die attached; pace it. ### The Weeping Furrow

> **Staff-hunt node 2** ◻️ UNVISITED (homebrew) — full detail. A purpose-built set-piece for Or'Henn's fire and Drenwal's radiance: a regenerating brute that only stops healing when you burn or sear him. This is the node that turns "the raid took my staff" into "and here is exactly who carried it up-chain."

**What it is:** a trench system gouged across the Avernian waste — the one place in Hell where the ground *bleeds grief*. Warm brine seeps from the fissures, pools in the trench-bottoms, and feeds a lattice of black ashwood roots that grew out of Cinderbloom's shed residue. **Kharzug the Root-Eater** — the buffed barlgura who intercepted the Ashen Garden's tribute caravan and could not stop eating the staff-magic — dens in the midden at the trench's heart.

**WHO HOLDS THE TRAIL:** Kharzug (full block below). He never kept Cinderbloom — he surrendered it up-chain as tribute because **Lady Urexiel outranks him** — but the Rootwright magic took root *inside his gut* and he has been gnawing the residue for a season. Kill him, rout him, or break him, and the trail points to Urexiel.

#### The Approach — three signs, never miles

Avernus has no fixed distances. Give the leg to the Furrow as **days and omens**. The trench announces itself three times before the party ever sees Kharzug — one sign per travel beat.

**FIRST SIGN — the ground weeps.**
> READ ALOUD: The cracked hardpan turns damp underfoot. Not water — the fissures are seeping a thin, warm brine that tastes of iron and old grief when the wind flicks it across your lips. Every step wrings a faint sob out of the earth, as if the plain itself is mourning something it cannot name, and cannot stop.

**SECOND SIGN — the furrows.**
> READ ALOUD: The land ahead is scored into long parallel trenches, each deeper than a man is tall, running to the horizon like claw-marks left by something vast dragged screaming across Avernus. Black roots the thickness of ship's hawsers lace the trench walls. Where the brine pools in the bottoms, the roots dip, and drink, and swell, and pulse — slow, and full, and satisfied.

**THIRD SIGN — the eater's larder.**
> READ ALOUD: Bones. Not scattered — *planted*. Ribcages stand upright in rows like fence-stakes, femurs driven butt-down into the trench floor, each one wrapped in living root and each still faintly warm to the hand. Whatever dens down here does not bury its leavings. It gardens them. From the dark ahead comes a wet, contented, unhurried chewing.

> **DM ONLY — Or'Henn's read.** On the Root-Sign the ashwood is *his* magic gone feral. Or'Henn feels it before any check (DC 10 Nature to articulate it): the residue yearns toward its parent staff, which means the staff is **alive and near-ish** — but *not here*. Let him coax the roots for advantage on any check to navigate the trench.

### The Scabrous Menagerie

> **◻️ UNVISITED — homebrew · the campaign's CONVERGENCE DUNGEON.** Four of the party's live threads terminate in one vault: Or'Henn's staff **Cinderbloom**, **Kreeg's puzzle box** (the legal lever on Elturel), a **Green Remnant** (1 of 3 for the Rekindling rite), and Or'Henn's three **escaped estate specimens** (the Contract-1 liability). Give this site the most room of any staff-hunt location. Every prior node — the Ashen Garden, the Weeping Furrow, Kharzug — funnels here.

**WHO HOLDS IT:** **Lady Urexiel**, Keeper of the Scabrous Menagerie. **CANON — she is a yugoloth**, not a devil: a mercenary broker who *commissioned* the raid that razed the Ashen Garden and slaughtered Or'Henn's household (his mentor Eldra and the retainers). Demon warband did the killing; **Urexiel paid for it**; devil buyers took the loot up-chain. Kreeg was the target/fugitive — she was the paymaster, never his master. She is vain, transactional, and a compulsive collector of beautiful locked things. She does **not** know the puzzle box is the deed to a city; to her it is a pretty bauble that "does not want to be looked at," which only makes it more collectible.

#### THE APPROACH — the caged chorus

> **READ ALOUD:** The road stops being a road and becomes an aisle. Cages line it on both sides, stacked three high and iron-barred, and the things inside have learned that footsteps mean either meat or a buyer. So they perform. A six-winged thing keens a lullaby in a dead priest's voice. Something with too many mouths recites the prices of its neighbors. A creature you have no name for presses a hand — a human hand, clean and manicured — through the bars and waves, small and polite, like a child at a window. The whole avenue sings, and none of it is for you. It is an audition. Every beast here is trying to prove it is worth keeping, because the alternative is the Killing Floor at the far end, and they can all smell it.

**DM ONLY:** The chorus is the first pressure. A **DC 14 Wisdom save** on entry or a PC spends its first round in the Pens transfixed by one specific cage (roleplay hook, no mechanical lock beyond lost movement). Or'Henn is immune — three of the voices in the chorus **recognize him** and go silent, then frantic (→ Estate-Beast Wing).

#### THE TROPHY VAULT — the reveal

> **READ ALOUD:** The vault is smaller than the Pens and much quieter, because the things in here do not perform — they are already owned. Glass and iron cabinets, each with a brass plate. A staff of blackened ashwood laid on velvet like a saint's relic, still faintly warm. Urexiel follows your gaze and mistakes it for admiration. "*That one,*" she says, delighted. "*Everyone does that. I have no idea what it is. Isn't it wonderful? Name a price.*"

#### THE REVENGE BEAT — the ledger, and the order of operations

**DM ONLY — PLACE UREXIEL'S LEDGER IN THE VAULT** (a lacquered account-book on the appraisal stand, or chained at her hip). It records the raid she commissioned on the Ashen Garden: the warband's fee, the up-chain buyers, the tally of what was taken — and the line item **"one living retainer, incidental,"** which is Asimov.

> **READ ALOUD (on reading the ledger):** The bookkeeping is immaculate. A demon vanguard, hired by the page. A garden, itemized as it was emptied. And low on the ledger, beneath the salvaged wards and the appraised beasts, a single incidental cost recorded in the same neat hand as everything else — a life, entered like a broken cup. Not even named. Just *incidental.*

This hands **Or'Henn AND the whole Asimov-grieving party a justified target** — proof, in her own hand, that she paid for the raid.

> **RULING — ORDER OF OPERATIONS:** Killing Urexiel **before extracting the ledger** loses the full paper trail — the buyers, the chain of commission, and any leverage over whoever above her funded it. **Flag this to the party in-fiction** before the killing blow (Asimov's voice from the Capacitor, or Or'Henn clocking the book): *the answer to who really did this is in that book, and it burns when she does.* A party that grabs the ledger first turns a revenge kill into a **thread that reaches up-chain** (a later paymaster, a Blood War buyer, a hook past this dungeon). A party that just kills her gets catharsis and nothing to follow.

#### GATE GREETING & DISPOSITION

> **READ ALOUD (Urexiel at the vault threshold):** She does not rise. She is arranging something behind glass with a jeweler's patience, and she speaks without turning around. "*You came up the singing aisle and you're still on your feet, so you're not stock. That leaves two kinds of guest.*" Now she turns, and her smile is a merchant's smile, warm and total and entirely for sale. "*Buyers — or the beginnings of a very fine new exhibit. I do love a matched set of three. Or three and a half.*" Her eyes settle on Or'Henn, and something crosses her face that is not fear and is not quite recognition — the look of a collector who has seen this piece in a catalogue. "*...You. I know your crest.*"

**Disposition ladder:** Urexiel deals before she fights. She respects a writ (law is law, and she is below the rank that can flout it). She respects coin. She has **no personal grievance** with the party — the Ashen Garden was a job, not a vendetta — which makes the *ledger reveal* land harder: to her it was never personal, and that is the horror of it. She turns hostile the instant she decides the party is worth more caged than paid.

### Vaelisha, the Mother Ash

> **[homebrew ◻️ UNVISITED — staff-hunt CAPSTONE · Cinderbloom → Cinderbloom Rekindled]**
> The one place in Avernus that does not want to hurt anyone. Not a dungeon. A sanctuary — the last living tree of Hell, and the site of the 8-hour rite that turns Or'Henn's staff legendary. Run it gentle. If the party has bled for every green scrap this far, let the quiet be the reward.

**WHERE:** A fire-spared hollow off the ash-plains, reachable anytime once the party has heard her name (Vaelisha surfaces in Vaelisha lore from Mad Maggie, from the Ashen Garden's records, or from Or'Henn's own druidic instinct). Travel to her is 2–4 days of hexcrawl; the encounter dice go quiet on the last leg (the red glare softens, green reaches the nose before the eye).

**WHO SHE IS:** The last root of the world that grew here before the fire. Silver-grey bark, boughs like poured pewter, leaves that hold light instead of throwing it. Her voice is a dryad-like guardian spirit braided into the tree's own awareness — **DM's choice** whether one speaks or both do. Ancient. Sorrowful. Kind without condition. She is not a quest-giver and never bargains. She gives.

---

**READ ALOUD (crossing her edge):**
> For two days the air has been a fist — dry, iron, hot enough to crack a lip mid-sentence. Then, without a step you can name, it lets go. Cool draws across the back of your neck like water. There is moss under your boots, real moss, soft and stupidly green, and somewhere above you a bird is singing — a bright rolling three-note song, over and over. You look up. There are no birds. There is a tree, silver as an old coin, so tall the smoke closes over her crown, and every leaf on her is turned toward you the way a face turns toward a door. The song keeps going. Nothing here wants to kill you. After Avernus, that lands in the chest harder than any blade — and right behind the relief comes the other thing, quiet and cold: *how is she still standing, and for how long.*

#### (4) LORE — the last root (DC 14 Nature or History, conversational)

Ask, and she remembers. A successful **DC 14 Nature/History** (or simply a patient conversation — she rewards curiosity over rolls) opens the cosmology gently:

- Avernus was green once. She recalls it the way you recall a childhood house — rooms you cannot re-enter.
- Celestial power did not always come from the sky here. It once ran **through living things** — root, sap, and leaf were conduits.
- A soft seed only: she speaks of a light that was **caged** so the fire could rule, and of a small mercy that survives in the act of tending rather than killing. (**Caged Star / Coram-of-Small-Mercies** groundwork — plant, do not explain.)

#### (5) THE COST OF LEAVING — fires once, on departure after the rite

> **READ ALOUD:**
> As you turn to go, the song stops. Not cut off — set down, gently, the way you set down something you are done holding. Her voice, when it comes, is only a little louder than the wind in her leaves.
> *"I am glad you came. I am glad it is brighter now, in your hands, than it was in mine."* A pause. Every leaf is still. *"I do not think I will stand next season. The fire comes a little closer each turning, and I am the last, and there is no one to plant me again. That is not a thing for you to fix. It is only a thing I wanted someone alive to know, before it happened. Go and be green somewhere else."*

No save. No mechanic. No clock. This is Or'Henn's arc landing — the elder who has lost his home, his staff, his garden, standing in front of the one thing older and more alone than he is.

> **DM ONLY:** She lets him take a **cutting** if he asks (a single silver-grey shoot, dormant, wrapped in wet moss). This is the seed of his rebuilt garden in the Ch.5 aftermath — carry the flag forward. If Or'Henn does not think to ask, Aurora or Lulu can nudge; do not force it, but make it available. The cutting is the answer to her grief the campaign will pay off later.

---

> **DM ONLY — if the party dawdles after collecting all three Remnants:**
> Seed that Vaelisha is being hunted. Signs, escalating: (a) a scorched patrol-track angling toward the hollow on the next hexcrawl leg; (b) ash-wraiths thicker on the approach; (c) a Zariel scout seen circling the green from a distance. She is not guaranteed forever. If the table stalls indefinitely, she can be found half-burned on a return trip — the rite still works at a diminished, sorrowful pitch, and the "cost of leaving" beat becomes "the cost of waiting." Use sparingly; the intent is to make the safe place feel precious, not to punish.

## CHAPTER 4 — THE SWORD OF ZARIEL

> **STATUS: ◻️ NOT YET REACHED — future content, full depth.**
> This is the destination the whole campaign bends toward. The party knows the Sword is real, knows it hangs in the **Bleeding Citadel**, and knows the Citadel surfaces from the ash only in a **33-day rhythm**. What they do not yet feel in their bones — and what this chapter must land at the table — is that the Sword was never Elturel's curse. It was Elturel's ransom, paid in advance by two people who loved a lost angel: the paladin **Yael** and the hollyphant **Lulu**. One of them is dead in the Citadel's heart. The other is asleep in the party's own baggage, dreaming in fragments. **The road to the Sword runs through Lulu.**

---

### 4.0 — THE REFRAME (read this before you run any of it)

Forget the published assumption that some villain hid the Sword to keep the party from it. In this campaign the truth is quieter and crueler:

- **Zariel fell.** Her blade fell with her — a weapon so charged with the memory of what she *was* that Hell could not simply keep it. It had to be carried *out*.
- **Yael and Lulu carried it.** A paladin of the old Hellrider order and Zariel's own hollyphant companion fled the Nine Hells with the blade. To hide it from Zariel forever, they raised a fortress out of Yael's own death and Lulu's own memory — the **Bleeding Citadel** — and they *paid for it*: Yael with her life, Lulu with her magic and nearly all of who she was.
- **Olanthius watched.** He did not steal the Sword. He did not hide it. He *stood by* while Yael bled out and Lulu forgot herself, and he blessed the hiding with his silence. His guilt is the guilt of the witness who let a good thing be buried. He holds the clearest surviving account of the road — and he will try to talk the party OUT of walking it, because to him the Sword's recovery makes Yael's sacrifice meaningless. (Full dossier: → M8 / Crypt of the Hellriders.)
- **Lulu is the map.** Her recovering memories are not flavor. They ARE the primary route to the Citadel. The Fallen Three (Haruman ✅, Bel ⚠️, Olanthius ◻️) are the *corroborating witnesses* whose testimony reassembles the same road Lulu carries inside her — redundancy, so no single dead-end blocks the campaign.

> **DM ONLY —** There are TWO convergent paths to the Citadel's door and the party can walk either or both:
> **(a) Lulu's Memories** — the emotional path; fragments fire on triggers (§4.1).
> **(b) The Map Pieces** — the procedural path; the Fallen Three's assembled testimony (§4.2).
> Hold BOTH open. If the players ignore Lulu, the Fallen Three still get them there. If they skip a Fallen One, Lulu still gets them there. Never let the campaign hinge on a single conversation.

---

### 4.1 — THE PATH (a): LULU'S MEMORIES

Lulu remembers in pieces, and each piece is triggered by a sensation, not a die roll. The party unlocks the road by *taking Lulu to the right places and letting her feel the right things.* Below is the fragment ladder — she is currently at **Stage 2** (she knows Zariel fell and that she loved her; she does not yet remember the flight or the Citadel).

#### Lulu Memory-Tracker

| Stage | Trigger (a sensation, not a check) | Fragment returned | Road value |
|-------|-----------------------------------|-------------------|------------|
| ✅ 1 | Already recovered | Zariel was her friend; Zariel fell | Motive; why Lulu is here |
| ✅ 2 | Already recovered | There was a blade, and a flight from Hell | Confirms the Sword is real and was *carried* |
| ◻️ 3 | **A smell of white feathers** — near the Moonkite in Lulu's presence, or any celestial site (the Solar's Helmet works) | Yael's face; a second person carried the blade with her | Names Yael; reveals the sacrifice was shared |
| ◻️ 4 | **The Crypt's cold** — Lulu physically present in the Crypt of the Hellriders, OR **Olanthius's face** seen | The order of the flight; the *raising* of the Citadel out of Yael's death | The near-complete road; the Citadel's *nature* (opens to grief) |
| ◻️ 4b | **Eldra's signet shown to Lulu**, OR **standing before a Hellrider dead** | Where the Charge broke; where Yael turned back to hold the line | Confirms the interior layout (§4.3–4.6) |
| ◻️ **FINAL** | Fires ONLY when the party **holds the last Fallen Three piece** (Olanthius's) **OR stands where Yael fell** (inside §4.4) | The full road, and Yael's last words to Lulu | Opens the Citadel's door without the Fallen Three; unlocks the §4.5 reunion |

> **DM ONLY —** The FINAL fragment is a lock with two keys, either sufficient: (1) the party completes the Fallen Three (procedural path catches up to the emotional one), or (2) the party physically reaches Yael's resting place inside the manifested Citadel (the place itself finishes the memory). This guarantees the reunion set-piece (§4.5) can always fire, even if a table skips a Fallen One or ignores Lulu until the last hall.

> **READ ALOUD — Stage 3 fragment (white feathers):**
> *Lulu goes very still. Her trunk lifts, tasting the air, and something behind her small dark eyes cracks open like a door left too long shut. "Feathers," she says — not to you, to no one. "White ones. Someone stood beside me in the cold and did not run. She had a plain sword and a plainer face and she said — she said we would only have to be brave once." A tremor runs the length of her. "Her name was Yael. Oh. Oh, I had forgotten I was allowed to say her name."*

---

### 4.2 — THE PATH (b): THE MAP PIECES

The procedural spine. Three witnesses, three fragments of the road; assembled, they reconstruct the same route Lulu carries.

| Piece | Holder | Status | How obtained |
|-------|--------|--------|--------------|
| 1 of 3 | **Haruman** | ✅ **HELD** | Killed prior session; trophy retconned as the "Haruman piece" |
| 2 of 3 | **Bel** | ⚠️ **NEXT** | Bel's Forge — the standing next main destination (→ Ch.3 / Bel dossier). Bel trades or is beaten out of it. |
| 3 of 3 | **Olanthius** | ◻️ | Crypt of the Hellriders (→ M8). **He does not hide it — he argues against using it.** |

**Cracking Olanthius (the social wall).** Olanthius is a guilt-locked death knight who tries to *recruit the party into leaving the Sword buried*. He does not open with violence — he opens with grief. The lever:

| Approach | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| **Show the Eldra Vash signet** (from the Ashen Garden grave, → M8) or **speak Eldra's name** | ✅ **MASTER KEY.** Auto — no check. His despair cracks. **Advantage on ALL social checks with him**, and he speaks *plainly* about the flight, the hiding, and the road. |
| Argue the cost is worth paying (without the signet) | DC 20 Persuasion, single attempt; failure hardens him toward the fight |
| Attack / coerce | He fights (statblock → M8). The road survives his death but the party loses the plain-speech shortcut and Lulu's Stage-4 trigger via his face still fires. |

> **DM ONLY —** The two paths are meant to *converge*. The cleanest table beat: the party assembles all three pieces AND has walked Lulu through her fragments, and the two roads snap together into one certainty in the same session. If they arrive with only one path complete, that path alone opens the door — the other is redundancy, not a gate.

---

### 4.3 — THE 33-DAY WINDOW (the heartbeat mechanic)

The Bleeding Citadel is not a place you can march to whenever you please. It surfaces from the ash of Avernus once every **33 days**, stays for a short manifestation, and sinks again. **You can only enter during a window.**

| Situation | What happens |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Arrive IN window, road known** | The Citadel is solid, the gate reachable, the interior traversable. Run §4.4–4.6 normally. |
| **Arrive OFF window** | There is no Citadel — only the *scar* where it surfaces: a bleeding depression in the plain, hot to the touch, ringed by the petrified penitents (§4.4). The party can hold a **vigil** here (see below) but cannot enter. |
| **Miss the window entirely** (spent elsewhere) | Elturel drops one descent rung toward the Styx (rungs do NOT reset — track in M1). Next window is **+33 days**, deeper, harder. |

**The Vigil (off-window arrival).** If the party reaches the scar off-window, they may camp the days until manifestation — but this is hostile ground. Each day of vigil:
- Roll **1d6**; on a **1–2**, an Avernus threat finds the exposed camp (ice devil patrol, a redcap warband, a Styx-drawn scavenger — scale to L11, CR 8–12).
- Each PC makes a **DC 13 Wisdom save** at dawn or gains one level of a custom **Grief-Weight** (disadvantage on the next day's saves; the plain leaks the penitents' despair). Cleared by a long rest away from the scar.
- **Lulu's presence halves the threat roll's odds** (1 on a d6 only) — the penitents remember her.

> **READ ALOUD — arriving at the scar, off-window:**
> *There is no fortress. There is a wound in the world — a shallow, weeping crater a bowshot wide, the ash around it black and slick as a scab that never healed. Heat comes off it in slow waves, and the air tastes of iron and old incense. Ringing the crater, half-sunk and facing inward, are the kneeling shapes: hundreds of figures carved — no, *turned* — to grey stone, hands pressed together, faces worn smooth by ash-wind. They are waiting for something to rise. So, it seems, are you.*

---

### 4.4 — INTERIOR, AREA 1: THE PLAIN OF KNEELING PENITENTS

*(In-window; the Citadel has risen at the crater's center.)*

The approach is a test of humility before it is a test of anything else. The plain is a slow field of petrified penitents — the mortal dead who tried to reach the Sword by *force* over the centuries and were caught mid-prayer, mid-charge, mid-bargain, and turned to stone facing the gate they never reached.

- **The lesson underfoot.** The penitents nearest the gate are not soldiers — they are the ones who came *begging*, and they got closest. The armored ones, the ones who came to *take*, are furthest out and worst-eroded. A **DC 12 Wisdom (Insight)** or a druid's read of the ground (auto for Or'Henn) reveals: *the ones who knelt got nearer than the ones who charged.*
- **Hazard — the Grief-Weight** (as §4.3) accrues on the walk in if the party crosses armed and shouting; it does NOT accrue if they cross quietly, weapons stowed. Make the players *feel* the choice; don't announce the mechanic.
- **No combat here by default.** If the party insists on hostility (attacking a penitent, forcing the gate), 1d4+2 penitents shear loose as **animated grief** (reflavored CR 5 stone constructs, resistant to nonmagical B/P/S) — the plain defending its own sorrow.

> **READ ALOUD — the walk to the gate:**
> *Every stone face is turned toward the same point ahead of you, and after a hundred paces you understand why: the fortress does not tower. It hunkers. Low, red-black, wet-looking in the ashen light, like a thing that grew rather than was built — and set into its front is not a door but a seam, a long vertical parting in the stone that glistens dark and slow. It is not shut against you. It is simply not yet open. The penitents kneel because kneeling is what got them this far. The question the plain asks you, without words, is whether you came to take the Sword — or to be worthy of carrying it.*

---

### 4.5 — INTERIOR, AREA 2: THE WOUND-GATE (opens to grief, not force)

The seam in the fortress front is a wound, and it heals shut against violence and parts for sorrow honestly felt.

| Attempt | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Force** (attacks, prying, spells against the seam) | The wound clenches *tighter*. Each violent attempt seals it for 1 minute and deals **2d10 psychic** to the attacker (the grief pushed back). |
| **The Blood Pay** (the 33-day price — powerful creature blood offered at the seam) | The physical key: the wound opens to the offering. This is the always-available procedural door. |
| **Honest grief** | A PC who *speaks a true loss aloud* at the seam (Drenwal's Dumal, Aurora's caged patron-star, Or'Henn's Asimov and his burned garden) opens it with no cost. **DC 15 Charisma check to force sincerity if the player is stuck** — but reward genuine role-play with auto-success. |
| **Lulu at the seam** | If Lulu has reached Stage 4+, she can open it herself, and *this* is the intended beat — the wound knows her. |

> **READ ALOUD — the gate opens to Lulu:**
> *Lulu steps forward — small, ridiculous, brave — and lays her trunk against the weeping seam the way you might touch the face of someone dying. She does not push. She says, very softly, "I came back. I know I said I would only be brave once. I lied." And the wound in the fortress does not open the way doors open. It* lets go *— relaxes, exhales, parts like a held breath finally released, and warm red light spills out over the kneeling stones, and somewhere far inside, something that has been waiting a very long time turns its head.*

---

### 4.6 — INTERIOR, AREA 3: THE HALL OF THE LAST CHARGE (the vision-trial)

A long hall frozen at the instant the Hellriders' final charge broke — Yael's line holding while the rest fell. The stone is full of standing after-images of the dead, mid-motion, and each casts a *personalized* vision at the PCs who walk it. This is the character-trial, not a fight. Each PC makes a **DC 15 Wisdom save** on entering their vision.

| PC | The vision | Save success | Save failure |
|----|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| **Drenwal** | His father's Hellrider order in full array — and one gap in the line where **Dumal** should stand, empty, cold, unfilled | Sees the empty place is *his to answer, not to grieve*; advantage on his next §4.7 worthiness roll | Bhaal whispers the gap is *his fault*; gains 1 Grief-Weight and Bhaal's Claim ticks +2 |
| **Aurora** | **The caged star** — a light bound and dimming, the shape of the celestial the Moonkite lost; it looks at her and does not beg | Understands the star can be *freed*, not just mourned; the Moonkite steadies; advantage on §4.8 checks | The star's light gutters; Aurora is frightened for the Moonkite; disadvantage on her next save |
| **Or'Henn** | **The garden green** — the Ashen Garden un-burned, Eldra alive, Cinderbloom whole in his hands; Asimov's voice from the Capacitor, unafraid | Carries the green forward as a promise, not a wound; advantage on the reunion (§4.5 already passed) and steadier hands at §4.7 | The garden burns again before his eyes; 1 Grief-Weight; the Capacitor grows heavy (roleplay beat) |

> **DM ONLY —** These visions are *steering*, not punishment. A failed save should hurt in story, not cripple the run. The point is to put each PC's core wound on the table right before the emotional climax, so the reunion (§4.5 → the reunion completes here if not sooner) and the worthiness test (§4.7) land with weight. If a player nails the role-play, grant success regardless of the roll.

> **READ ALOUD — entering the Hall:**
> *The hall is a battle held one heartbeat before it ended. Grey riders lean into a charge that will never complete; lances angle at a dark that will never break; and at the far end, alone, a plain figure in plain armor stands with her back to a light and her face to the tide, holding a line that everyone behind her has already lost. The air is thick as syrup. And then the frozen riders turn their heads — all of them, at once — and each one is looking at a different one of you, and each of you sees something that is not in this hall at all.*

---

### 4.7 — INTERIOR, AREA 4: YAEL'S RESTING PLACE (the reunion set-piece)

The heart of the Citadel and the emotional peak of the chapter. Yael did not die and get buried — she died and *became* the fortress's foundation. Her resting place is a small, still chamber where the red light is gentlest, and her voice can reach the living **through Lulu**. This is the scene the whole arc has been carrying Lulu toward. Run it slow. Let the table go quiet.

**Mechanics are minimal here on purpose.** No save, no check to enter. Lulu must simply be present. If Lulu has hit her FINAL fragment (§4.1), Yael speaks the full road and the Sword's chamber (§4.8) opens. If not, standing here *is* the FINAL trigger — the place finishes the memory.

> **READ ALOUD — the reunion (Yael's voice, through Lulu):**
> *The chamber is quiet the way the inside of a bell is quiet after it has finished ringing. There is no tomb, no effigy — only a warmth in the stone, and the smell of white feathers, and Lulu standing very small in the middle of it with her eyes closed. When she speaks, it is her voice and it is not; there is someone underneath it, patient and tired and glad.*
>
> *"You brought her back," the voice says — Yael's voice, borrowed through the friend she saved. "I asked one thing of the dark before I let it have me. I asked that if anyone ever loved her enough to bring her here, they be the kind who would carry the sword the way it needs carrying — not to win, but to mend." A long, warm pause. "Lulu. Little one. You were never supposed to remember the price. I'm sorry it cost you yourself to find your way home."*
>
> *Lulu opens her eyes, and they are wet, and they are hers again — all of her, whole for the first time since Hell. "You said we'd only have to be brave once," she says. "You liar." And the warmth in the stone laughs, and it is the last thing Yael ever does.*

**Aftermath.** Lulu is *whole* from this point — all fragments recovered, her memory intact. Note it in the campaign state; she can now speak plainly about the flight, the cage, and Zariel-as-she-was for the endgame. This is also the moment to seed the celestial lever if the party carries the pieces (→ §4.9).

> **DM ONLY —** If a PC died earlier in the run and is being carried, Yael's chamber is a legitimate place to stage a *significant* resurrection beat — the Citadel is made of a good death. Optional; don't force it.

---

### 4.8 — INTERIOR, AREA 5: THE SWORD'S CHAMBER (the worthiness test)

The blade waits at the Citadel's core, point-down in bleeding stone, wrapped in the same low red light. **Taking it is not a fight.** The Sword tests the *charity of the one who reaches for it* — the willingness to sacrifice, not the strength to seize. Force fails absolutely; there is nothing here to hit.

**The worthiness test.** The reaching PC must offer something true. Frame it in character; resolve with **one Charisma check (any face skill fits the offer)**, DC scaling to the sacrifice named:

| The offer | DC | Notes |
|-----------|----|----|
| A vow to spend the Sword to *mend*, not to conquer | 13 | The baseline; most sincere PCs clear it |
| A named personal sacrifice the PC will actually pay (a power given up, a bond risked, a life laid on the line for Elturel) | 10 | Lower DC — sincerity of cost is the point |
| Reaching to *own* the Sword, to be strong, to win | **Auto-fail** | The blade will not be a weapon of conquest; the chamber's light dims and the reach burns (2d10 radiant) |

- **Advantage** if that PC passed their §4.6 vision, or if Lulu vouches for them (post-reunion).
- **Drenwal note:** Bhaal will try to answer the vow *for* him. If Drenwal reaches while Bhaal's Claim is high, the DM may require he first *refuse* Bhaal aloud (roleplay + DC 15 Wisdom) before the Charisma check — a powerful table beat, and a reason to stage his rebirth *here* (§4.9).

> **READ ALOUD — the Sword's chamber:**
> *It is a plain sword. That is the first shock. After everything — the plain of the kneeling dead, the wound that opens to grief, the hall of the last charge, the friend made of a good death — you expected a thing of terrible glory. Instead there is a soldier's blade, point-down in stone that bleeds slow around it, worn at the grip where a hand once held it every day of a hard life. The red light does not blaze off it. It rests on it, gently, the way light rests on something loved. The Sword does not ask if you are strong enough to lift it. It asks, in the quiet, what you would give to never have to.*

**THE SWORD — campaign function.**

| Function | Effect |
|----------|--------|
| **The PHYSICAL lever** | The Sword can **sever Elturel's chains by force** — cut the infernal bindings at their source, the raw solution to the sinking city. This is the muscle path, independent of the Contract. |
| **Confront Zariel** | In her presence, the Sword shows Zariel *what she was* — the fallen angel, not the archdevil. It is the precondition for any redemption ending (§4.9). |
| **Wielder** | Attunement demands the worthiness held, not just won — a wielder who turns to conquest loses it. (Combat stats can be lifted from the published relic; the *campaign* weight is the two functions above.) |

---

### 4.9 — REDEMPTION: CORAM, THE CAGED STAR, AND DRENWAL'S REBIRTH

The Sword alone saves the city by *force*. But this campaign offers a third, redemptive road — and it converges *here*, at the Citadel, if the party has done the celestial work.

**Coram-of-Small-Mercies** — the homebrew minor deity of redemption and second chances — is the lever that makes Zariel's *redemption* (not just her defeat) winnable, and simultaneously cures Drenwal. The mechanism:

- **Freeing the Caged Star** requires three things held together: the **Solar's Helmet** (the bearing), **Lulu's caging-memory** (the door — recovered in full at §4.7), and the **Moonkite** (Aurora's bond, the celestial witness). With all three, the cage can be opened.
- **Freeing the Star does two things at once:**
  1. **It cures Drenwal.** He can *die and be reborn in the eyes of a god who never claimed him* — Coram, the mercy that catches him where Bhaal and Gargauth both reach and neither can hold. His rebirth rite (**Veska officiating** — she woke from her own Vigil to do exactly this) **can be staged HERE, at the Citadel**, for maximum table impact: reborn in the fortress made of a good death, in Coram's new-opened light. Bhaal's Claim and Gargauth's Bargain both *break* on a successful rite.
  2. **It starves Zariel's celestial power** — the caged star was fueling what remained of her angelic might. Free it, and her power gutters; a redemption offer (Sword + her old self shown + the star's mercy) becomes *winnable* at the endgame instead of impossible.

> **DM ONLY — DO NOT fix the Cage's location anywhere in this chapter.** The cage opens ONLY via the Solar's Helmet (bearing) + Lulu's fully-recovered caging-memory (door), witnessed by the Moonkite. Where it hangs is deliberately unwritten here — it is set by the Helmet's pointing (→ M8: The Caged Star) at play time, never pre-placed. This chapter provides the *convergence point and payoff*, not the coordinates.

**THE THREE ENDINGS — state them plainly at the table's decision point:**

| Levers pulled | Ending | Elturel | Drenwal | Zariel |
|---------------|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| **Sword only** | ⚠️ **Compromised** | Chains cut by force; the city is freed but scarred — lives lost, Kreeg's contract still legally live | Uncured unless addressed separately | Defeated or fled; the wound in Hell festers |
| **Sword + Contract** | ✅ **Clean win** | Chains cut AND the sale legally voided (→ Kreeg / the Contract levers, Ch.3 & M6) — Elturel restored whole | Curable via the rebirth rite | Beaten on both fronts |
| **Sword + Contract + Coram** | ★ **Redemptive** | Restored whole | **Cured** — reborn in Coram's eyes, both infernal claims broken | **Redeemable** — starved of stolen celestial power, shown what she was, offered her old self back |

> **READ ALOUD — staging Drenwal's rebirth at the Citadel (if run here):**
> *Veska kneels in the fortress's red light with her old order's words on her lips, and for once they do not sound like grief. "You are not your father's oath," she tells Drenwal, "and you are not your blood, and you are not the thing that has been whispering in it since before you had a name. Die, then. Die honest. And let a god who never wanted you for a weapon be the one who says* welcome back." *Somewhere above the cage, a small mercy that has waited a very long time to be needed finally opens its eyes.*

Let them feel the threads knot together. The Sword is the climax of the *plot*; the reunion (§4.7) and Drenwal's rebirth (§4.9) are the climax of the *campaign*. Give both room to breathe.

## CHAPTER 5 — ESCAPE FROM AVERNUS

> **What this chapter is.** Not a location, not a rail — a set of **procedures you run at the table** once the party turns toward the ending. The endgame has three moving parts: pull the levers (5.1), read the ending they earn (5.2), fight the climax (5.3), and get home alive (5.4). Section 5.5 is the debrief you fill in afterward. Everything gates on **keys and choices, never sequence.** The party can reach the Sword before the Contract, the Contract before the Sword, court Bel or free Kostchtchie or neither. Run the checklist for whatever they bring.
>
> **The one sentence that holds the chapter together:** the Sword *breaks the chains by force*; the Contract *unwrites the sale by law*; Coram-freed *starves the fire that would let Zariel re-forge either*. How many they bring decides how cleanly Elturel comes home — and that arithmetic is the only thing in this book you rule with a closed fist.

---

### 5.1 — THE TWO LEVERS (run side by side)

Elturel is held by two things at once: a **physical chain** (the light-chains hauling it down the Styx) and a **legal claim** (Kreeg's sale, which lets Zariel re-chain it even if you cut the first set). You need an answer to each, or you win with a wound. The Sword answers the chain. The Contract answers the claim.

| | **THE SWORD** — the physical lever | **THE LAW** — the legal lever |
|---|---|---|
| **What it does** | Severs Elturel's chains at their source and undoes the pact by force, drawn by a worthy hand. | Annuls or voids the sale itself, so Zariel keeps no claim to re-chain the city. |
| **Where it lives** | Zariel's own blade, in the **Bleeding Citadel** (manifests every **33 days** — the Rung, see M1). | Kreeg's instrument of sale. **Two forms — keep them straight (below).** |
| **The key to it** | **Lulu's returning memory** (primary road) *or* the **Fallen Three's** assembled witness-fragments — spent inside a live 33-day window, Blood Pay at the door. | The **copy** (reachable now) *or* the **original** (Zariel's, taken at the climax). |
| **Fail-forward** | Any dead Fallen-Three member is redundant with Lulu + the others + Mordenkainen's notes. No single death blocks the road. | Copy path and original path reach the same annulment by different means. Neither requires the other. |
| **Status** | ⚠️ 1/3 pieces (Haruman held); Olanthius & Bel located; **Lulu is the true path** | ⚠️ Copy in Urexiel's vault (party may be unaware); original at the climax |

#### The Contract has TWO FORMS — do not let them blur

> **THE COPY** — Kreeg's puzzle box, in **Lady Urexiel's vault** (Scabrous Menagerie, → M5/M6), reachable **NOW**. This is the **legal** lever: a certified copy, worthless on its own, decisive when paired with proof of Kreeg's assent and a writ that dissolves his title. You do not destroy the copy — you **file** it.
>
> **THE ORIGINAL** — the true instrument, which **Zariel holds or can summon** to her audience. This is the **physical** lever of the law: you do not file it, you **destroy it** on the field. The moment the original burns, the chains it authorized break outright.

**PROCEDURE — the COPY path (the courtroom win, runnable before the climax):**

Run this as a checklist. The party needs **all four** to void the sale by law:

1. ◻️ **Recover the box.** Take Kreeg's puzzle box from Urexiel's vault (heist, deal, or over her body → M5). The Green Remnant and Or'Henn's staff sit beside it — one raid, three prizes.
2. ◻️ **Open the box.** It seals to Kreeg's making. Three keys open it — **any one**:
   - **Kreeg's living hand** — bring "Vessper Hain" to the box (capture or turn → M6). The box *reacts to his proximity* — it warms, the clasp loosens. Fastest, most treacherous; he opens it and bolts.
   - **An amnizu-executed writ dissolving his title** — Contract 5's writ (Malfius Nine-Reeds / **Fhet'Ahla** → M4). The legal crowbar. Slow, expensive, ironclad.
   - **Zariel** — she can open anything of Kreeg's. A door of last resort you do not want to walk through.
3. ◻️ **Secure Kreeg's living signature.** A living signatory has unique legal value (below). Capture or turn "Vessper Hain" so a devil authority can take his assent — or, if he is dead, substitute a **certified transcript of the sale from the amnizu registry** (Fhet'Ahla's notarial records / Contract 5). The dead-Kreeg route is real, slower, and reads as a courtroom of devils instead of a captive coward.
4. ◻️ **File the writ before an infernal authority.** Present copy + assent + writ to a devil empowered to void the sale. **Fhet'Ahla can notarize it** (she is bound to silence on Kreeg by her terms, but not bound to refuse a properly filed writ). **Bel would love to preside** — presiding over the legal unmaking of Zariel's greatest prize is exactly the humiliation he lives for; if the party courted him, he hosts the filing at the Forge and counts it a personal victory.

> **READ ALOUD** *(the moment the writ is filed):* Fhet'Ahla does not raise her voice. She presses the seal — a click, small as a beetle's shell breaking — and somewhere very far above you, a chain that has held for a hundred nights simply *forgets what it was for.* The ink on the great sale does not burn. It **fades**, the way a name fades from a stone nobody has visited in years, until the parchment is only parchment, and the thing it once did is a thing that never happened.

**PROCEDURE — the ORIGINAL path (the climax burn):** at Zariel's audience, the original is in play. **Destroy it** — radiant damage, a worthy hand, the Sword's edge, or a called shot in the chaos (5.3, Phase 3). No filing, no notary. The chains it authored break the instant it is unmade. This is the lever for tables that would rather cut than litigate.

> **⚠️ NO-GOTCHA RULE — mandatory, non-negotiable.** The party must have **at least two clear chances to learn that a *living* signatory has unique legal value BEFORE any chance to kill Kreeg.** This is a hard design lock, not a suggestion. Deliver it through **any two** of these, spaced apart:
> - **The box reacts to his proximity.** Anyone carrying it near "Vessper Hain" feels it warm, hum, or tug (passive; no roll needed to notice something is wrong when it does it twice).
> - **Mahadi sells the hint.** He knows exactly who "Hain" is and treats it as premium stock: *"The man's worth more breathing, you understand. A corpse can't sign. A corpse can't be *made* to sign. Do think it through."*
> - **Fhet'Ahla, in her clerical way,** notes that the writ path is "far cleaner with the signatory present to give assent — the registry transcript is admissible, but the Courts do so prefer a living hand."
>
> If the party executes Kreeg on sight anyway, that is a **choice they made informed** — reroute to the transcript path (slower, costlier), never a dead end. The point is that no table ever loses the soft path to a gotcha they couldn't see coming.

#### Kreeg on the page — desperate savior, not mustache-twirler

Play "Vessper Hain" **pitiable and slippery.** He is a stooped soul-coin appraiser with ink-stained cuffs and a flinch he can't hide. His story, when it comes out, is not villainy — it is a coward's mercy that curdled: an undead tide rose against Elturel, he was cornered and terrified, and Zariel offered *salvation* — the Companion's holy fire to burn the dead. He took it. Then, over decades, he spread the debt across the whole city through the **Creed Resolute**, a loyalty-oath preached from every pulpit until every faithful soul stood, unknowing, as collateral. And when the collection came due, **he ran.** He saved his city by damning it and could not face the bill. He does not gloat. He bargains, he weeps, he offers the party *anything* — and he will betray them the instant the box is open and his hand is free. The tragedy is that he is telling the truth about how it started. Let the table feel the pull toward mercy and the reasons to refuse it both.

### 5.2 — VICTORY TIERS IN PLAY (what each ending actually costs)

Do not tell the players the tier names. Show them the shapes; let the Doom Ladder (M3) supply the urgency that tempts them into a one-lever rush. The compromised tier is the **punishment for haste**, not a failure state.

| Tier | Levers brought | Concrete outcome — pick and lock at the table |
|---|---|---|

This is the campaign's **true target**; any table that pulls both levers should reach it. |
| ✅✅ **REDEMPTIVE** | **Sword + Contract + Coram freed** | The clean win, **plus:** with Coram-of-Small-Mercies loosed from the cage, Zariel's celestial fuel (the **Tears of the Caged Star**) is cut off — she cannot fight to re-forge anything. Aurora's patron thread closes. At **Doom Rung 4**, the Styx-memory-wiped are **restored**. A god is freed, a city comes home whole, and a fallen angel is handed the door out of Hell. |

Coram is the hardest, most optional pillar — that is *correct*, it is the bonus for tables that chased the celestial thread (Solar's Helmet + Lulu's caging-memory + the Moonkite). Never gate the clean win behind Coram. If the party brings both core levers, give them the clean win cleanly.

### 5.3 — THE CLIMAX: ZARIEL'S AUDIENCE (three phases)

The climax is **an audience that becomes a battle over a sinking city.** Run it in three phases. The levers are **actions taken on the field**, not just damage — a party can *win the argument* as much as win the fight. Zariel's statblock is the published archdevil (CR 26); at a 3-PC L11 table she is a **set-piece with legendary actions and lair effects, backed by allies and modifiers**, not a HP sponge to grind. Structure beats numbers.

#### Opening beat — Lulu's final memory (fires before initiative)

Whatever route brought them here, the door to the endgame is the **hollyphant remembering the whole of what she paid.** She does not recover it in safety — she recovers it *at the threshold*, looking at Zariel.

> **READ ALOUD:** Lulu has been trembling since the sky changed. Now she goes very still. When she speaks, it is not her small bright voice — it is older, and it is breaking. *"I know you. I carried you out of the fire once, before the fire was yours. Yael and I... we gave everything so no one could ever use the sword against you. We gave our lives, our light, our own names."* Her trunk brushes the hilt at Zariel's side — or the blade in your hand — and her eyes fill. *"I forgot you on purpose. I forgot you because remembering hurt more than forgetting. And I remember now. Oh — I remember all of it."*

This beat **opens the levers**: Lulu naming the sacrifice is what lets the Sword be drawn *worthy*, what lets the party name Zariel's old self aloud in the redemptive path, and the emotional pivot Zariel cannot answer with fire.

#### Phase 1 — THE AUDIENCE (social, before or instead of blows)

Zariel receives them over the sinking city. This phase is **won or lost in words and evidence**, and can shorten or reshape everything after.

- **Present the Contract copy + writ** → force Zariel onto the back foot; she must summon the **original** to contest it (this is how the original enters play for Phase 3).
- **Name her old self** (redemptive; needs Lulu's memory + Coram progress) → a **DC 20 Persuasion/Insight** at advantage if Coram is already freed cracks her certainty; she does not surrender, but she *hesitates*, and hesitation is a phase.
- **Kreeg present** (if captured/turned) → his testimony detonates here; Zariel may try to reclaim or silence him.
- **Refuse the audience** → Zariel opens with a lair-effect strike; skip to Phase 2.

> **READ ALOUD** *(Zariel, from her throne of wing-bones):* You come to my sky waving *paper.* Do you know how many have stood where you stand, certain their little document, their borrowed blade, their clever mercy would be the one to move me? I have their names. I have their *souls.* Elturel is mine by ink its own priest spilled — freely, gratefully, on his knees. Show me the hand that unwrites a willing signature. Show me, and I will let you try.

#### Phase 2 — THE FALL (the battle over the sinking city)

Combat on a **collapsing platform above the Styx** — a countdown of terrain, not a turn timer. The city visibly drops each round; the fight is *on top of the thing you came to save.*

**Battlefield modifiers (apply what the party earned):**

| Modifier | If... | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Kostchtchie's rampage** | Kostchtchie was freed before a Citadel window (→ M3/M5) | Zariel's guard is **one rung weaker** — remove her strongest summoned ally, and she loses 1 legendary action/round while a demon lord smashes her flank. A screen, not a solve. |
| ⚠️ **Bel courted** | The party won Bel over (rods + the Forge deal → M5) | Bel will **not** fight Zariel directly (Blood War optics) but feeds the party **one exploitable weakness per round** (a lowered resistance, a telegraphed legendary action) as sabotage. If the writ was filed at his Forge, he attends to *savor it.* |
| ⚠️ **Lady Vengeance on station** | Ship repaired + rods installed (5.4) | The warship provides **fire support** — one force-cannon salvo/round (DM-adjudicated ~6d10 force, save for half) and an evac deck for downed PCs. |
| ❌ **No modifiers** | Rushed climax, nothing courted | Zariel fights at **full strength** with full guard. Survivable, brutal, and the clearest push toward a compromised ending. |

#### Phase 3 — THE LEVER (win-condition, not a HP bar)

Zariel does not need to hit 0 HP. The party **wins by pulling their levers on the field.** Track it as objectives:

- **THE SWORD, drawn worthy** (Lulu's memory unlocked it) → a successful strike with Zariel's own blade **severs the chains** — narrate the light-chains snapping one by one. This alone lifts the city off the Styx.
- **THE ORIGINAL, destroyed** → the summoned original is a **destructible object** (AC 17, 40 HP, resistant to nonmagical/mundane, vulnerable to radiant; a worthy Sword-strike auto-destroys). Burn it → the sale is unwritten and Zariel **cannot re-chain the city.**
- **CORAM named and freed** (redemptive) → if the party freed Coram (Helmet + Lulu + Moonkite, before or during), the **Caged Star's light floods the field.** Zariel's Tears-fuel is cut, her legendary resistances spent, and she is **offered her old self** — the ending pivots from battle to choice.

> **READ ALOUD** *(clean win — the original burns):* The parchment takes the flame like it was waiting for it. And above you, Elturel — your city, hanging in the sky of Hell for a hundred nights — *shudders.* Not falling. **Rising.** The chains of light that held it don't shatter so much as let go, the way a hand lets go of something it was only ever pretending it had the right to hold. Zariel screams. It is not a devil's scream. For one heartbeat it is a soldier's — someone who has just watched the last thing she owned decide it never belonged to her.

> **READ ALOUD** *(redemptive — Coram's light):* The light that comes is not holy fire and it is not hellfire. It is *small* — the light of a candle left in a window for someone who might still come home. It touches Zariel and she flinches like it burns, and then she doesn't, and then something in the ruin of her face that has been clenched for a thousand years simply... opens. The Caged Star's light bends down, gathers Elturel the way you'd gather a sleeping child, and turns for the world above. And Zariel — Zariel is looking at a door she stopped believing was there.

### 5.4 — THE LADY VENGEANCE (the ride home)

Freeing Elturel doesn't end in Avernus — the party has to **get out**, and so does the city. The Lady Vengeance is the escape vehicle, and she has to be whole to make the run.

**PRE-FLIGHT CHECKLIST — she cannot make the escape run until:**

- ◻️ **Adamantine control rods installed** (from the **Wrecked Flying Fortress** → M3/M5). Same rods Bel demands — **one fetch, two payoffs.** Until installed: half speed, one force cannon, no Overdrive. The escape run **requires full speed and Overdrive** — this is a hard prerequisite, flagged from Session 8.
- ◻️ **Jeremy at the helm** (non-combat NPC; flies and repairs her).
- ◻️ **Lulu aboard** (her memory both unlocked the ending and steadies the celestial course home).

#### THE ESCAPE SET-PIECE — a skill-challenge chase

The freed city and the ship run for the portal home while Avernus tears at them: pursuing devils, Styx-spray, the collapsing sky. Run as a **chase / group skill challenge** — a series of escape checks (DC 15–18: Jeremy's piloting, arcane cannon fire, Aurora's warding, Drenwal's radiance holding back the Styx, Or'Henn's Wildfire clearing a path). **Enough successes = clean escape; failures = the city takes a wound (feeds the compromised tier) or a PC/ally is imperiled.**

#### THE OVERDRIVE MORAL CHOICE — mechanize it

The Soul Coin Overdrive is the ship's emergency booster — and it runs on **soul coins.** Here is the whole moral engine of the campaign in one lever:

> **THE CHOICE, laid before the table under fire:** the ship needs a hard burst to clear the portal.
> - **SPEND a soul coin** → +3 to one escape check, **but the soul inside is annihilated** — erased, not freed, gone as if it never was. The easy button with an unforgivable price.
> - **FREE the souls instead** (via **Lulu's** benediction, the **Moonkite's** light, or **Jeremy's Gondian method** → M7) → each freed soul does *not* power the engine by force. It **thanks them.** For **each soul freed during the run, the ship earns a celestial tailwind: +1 to ONE escape check** (stacking, one per freed soul). Mercy is slower per-coin than murder — and it stacks into something murder can't buy.

> **READ ALOUD** *(a soul freed at the Overdrive port):* You break the coin's seal, and instead of the sick green glow feeding into the engine, there's a sound — thin, astonished, like someone surfacing from deep water and finding they can still breathe. A shape of light no bigger than a sparrow slips out of the coin, hangs an instant over the deck as if memorizing your faces, and then it's gone — *up*, toward the world, ahead of the city, and the Lady Vengeance leans into the wake it leaves like a sail finding wind.

> **DM ONLY:** If the party spends coins to escape, do not punish it out of hand — but *narrate the erasure*, name a soul if you can (a coined citizen of Elturel, cruelly), and let it land. The contrast between the two paths *is* the lesson; both must be genuinely available under real pressure. Track freed souls — they also feed the aftermath (5.5) and the compromised tier's "souls already coined" cost.

### M7.1 — The Blood War

**The eternal war between law and chaos that made Avernus a battlefield, a graveyard, and a real-estate market — and the reason your dead enemies keep coming back.**

Avernus is the first and lowest gate of the Nine Hells and the front line of the Blood War: the endless slaughter between the **devils** (lawful evil, of the Hells) and the **demons** (chaotic evil, of the Abyss). It has burned for so long that no one alive remembers a peace, and the war is not fought to be won — it is fought because stopping would let the other side breed. Every warlord, every scavenged war machine, every soul coin the party has ever spent is fuel for one more day of it.

> **RULING — why demons reform and devils litigate:** A slain **demon** is not destroyed; its essence sinks back to the Abyss and re-forms, unmade of memory but not of hunger. A slain **devil** does not simply respawn — its rank, contracts, and true name are *legal property*, and its destruction sets off inheritance, promotion, and litigation up the hierarchy. Chaos regrows; law is redistributed. This is why Hell runs on paperwork (→ M7.7) and the Abyss runs on tides of meat.

This single ruling frames two live threads:

| Front | What it means at the table | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **The Yeenoghu warband** | The party destroyed Yeenoghu's **avatar** (Session 7) — but the demon lord re-forms in the Abyss, and his *front* (the gnoll-and-demon war-tide that razed the Ashen Garden and slaughtered Or'Henn's household (his mentor Eldra and the retainers)) never had a head to cut off. It keeps eating enclaves. | ❌ avatar dead · ⚠️ front alive |
| **Kostchtchie's Maw** | A demon lord bound in Avernus. Freeing him doesn't help the party fight — it hands Zariel a *chaos fire* on her own doorstep, pulling her attention off the Bleeding Citadel. A pure Blood-War distraction lever. (→ M5: Kostchtchie's Maw; → M3: endgame levers) | ❓ sealed · ⚠️ live lever |

**Why Bel fell from lordship.** Bel was **Lord of Avernus** — the devil who ran the Blood War from this layer for an age. When Zariel fell and proved a more ruthless commander, **Asmodeus gave her Avernus and demoted Bel to her lieutenant.** Not exile — worse: he kept his forge, his knowledge, and his rank, and lost his crown to the upstart he now must serve. That humiliation is the whole of his politics. (→ M6: Bel; → M7.8 faction clock.)

> **Lever:** Bel hates Zariel more than he hates the party. He will arm anyone who might embarrass her, and call it deniable. His Fallen-Three piece is for sale to whoever brings him the adamantine control rods. (→ M3: the Fallen Three.)

### M7.2 — Souls and the Coin

**The hard economic truth of Hell: a soul coin is a person, spending it is murder with no afterlife, and every faction in this campaign is quietly running on erased people.**

A **soul coin** is one real damned mortal — conscious, suffering, forged into currency. While carried or traded, the soul persists, trapped but reversible. It can even be **queried** like *speak with dead* (this is how Rasheem interrogated Bloodbath's absorbed remains). But the moment a coin is **spent as fuel** — a war machine's 24-hour burn, the Lady Vengeance's Soul Coin Overdrive, a Gondian core drained dry — the soul is **annihilated. True final death. No judgment, no rest, no resurrection, not even *Wish*.**

> **RULING — the small murder:** Spending a soul coin is not spending money. It is executing a specific person forever. Play every Overdrive, every fueled war machine, every casual vendor transaction as a life ending in the room. Drenwal, of all people, should feel it.

> READ ALOUD: *The coin is warm — always warm, like something just set down. Hold it to your ear over the market's noise and there is a sound under the sound: not words, not quite, but the shape of a person still in there, pressing at the metal from the inside. Jeremy takes it from your palm without looking up. "That one's got about four hours of thrust in it," he says. "You want to fly, or you want to be squeamish?"*

#### Freeing souls — the Rite of Un-Coining [HOMEBREW]

Reviving a coined soul to *life* is all but impossible (the bodies are dust). What is achievable is **freeing** it: breaking the binding and sending the soul on to **judgment** (not guaranteed paradise) — innocents go to rest. It is slow, forfeits the coin's value, angers devils, and pleases the mercy-powers (raises Coram, → M7.5). Three methods, each with a real cost:

| Method | Who | Speed & limit | Cost | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|

#### The Soul Council and the capacitor chain

The **soul capacitor** is a bounty-hunter's soul-vessel run by the **Soul Council** — Hell's clearinghouse for hunters. Its logic is a chain: the trapped soul inside feeds bounties to a *living* hunter; when that hunter dies, they replace the occupant, and the cycle repeats. It is a coin that pays out in work instead of thrust, and it never lets go of anyone.

```
Rasheem (prior occupant) ──fed bounties to──► Asimov (living hunter)
        │                                          │
   released / moved on                       DIED to Yeenoghu (S7)
   when Asimov replaced him                        │
                                                    ▼
                          Asimov (NOW the occupant) ──relays soul-bounties──► the party
                                                    │                          (Or'Henn = target hunter)
                          Soul Council clock: bind a LIVING hunter or the
                          vessel is reassigned & Asimov's soul goes to open auction
                                              (→ M1 for days remaining)
```

- **Asimov** never met Or'Henn in life; he is a disembodied voice from the capacitor, relaying Emporium jobs reframed as **soul-bounties** (→ M6: Asimov).
- **Satisfying the clock:** the Council's deadline is met the moment Or'Henn formally **seals any one contract** (→ M3 / M4: the five contracts), binding him as the living hunter. Miss it and the capacitor reassigns — a **fail-forward crisis**, not a game-over (→ M8; → M9 for the auction fallout).
- **Asimov's only road out:** Nymira Quill's **Contract 4** lets him name a *hidden clause in his own confinement* — the single legal thread that could unbind him (→ M4: Contract 4).

> **DM ONLY:** The Council does not care who suffers, only that the vessel is always occupied and always working. If the party ever tries to free Asimov by force rather than by clause, the Council treats it as theft of secured collateral — a new creditor, not a new enemy. Everything here is billable.

### M7.3 — The Hellriders and the Crimson Watch

**Two catastrophes, one order: the Charge that fell generations ago and the city that fell last year — and the murdered father, the enslaved teacher, and the buried betrayal that tie Or'Henn's staff hunt to Drenwal's blood.**

The **Hellriders of Elturel** are the mounted holy order of the city lit by the **Companion** (a second sun). They earned the name the plain way: **they rode into Hell.** The **Crimson Watch** is Elturel's own standing chapter of that order — the knights who garrisoned the city across generations. To be Crimson Watch is to belong to a creed with one word at its heart: ***Keep the watch.*** Mercy, held at the line.

Two disasters must not be confused at the table:

| Event | When | What happened | Who it left behind |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Wall / The Descent / The Charge** | Generations ago | **Zariel** (then a *celestial*) led mortal Hellriders through a portal to take the Blood War to the enemy. The host was broken; the retreat **sealed the portal**; Zariel **fell** and became the archduke who rules Avernus now. | The dead, and the *trapped* — knights caught on the wrong side when the portal shut. |
| **The Fall of Elturel** | Recent (campaign premise) | High Overseer **Thavius Kreeg** sold the city to Zariel by contract; the **Companion** was the mechanism; Elturel was dragged whole into Avernus and is sinking. | Drenwal's generation — the modern Watch, the whole point of the spine (→ M3: free Elturel). |

> **RULING — colours of the dead:** *Hellrider blue* is a deliberate tell. Any figure in faded, blackened, deep-cut blue is tied to the order and, usually, to Drenwal's family. When you want to telegraph "this connects to the Watch," dress it in old blue. The sigil is **a golden sun pierced by a sword.**

#### Eldra Vash — the bridge [HOMEBREW, hard canon]

> **This is the hinge that fuses Or'Henn's arc to Drenwal's. Keep it straight.**

**Eldra Vash** was a Hellrider of the **original Charge** — captured when the retreat sealed the portal, enslaved for decades, and eventually **sold to Thavius Kreeg's new Avernian estate** to build his impossible garden. There she was the enslaved Material-Plane druid who taught **Or'Henn** everything he knows — to drive roots through iron, bone, and ash — and who left him **Cinderbloom** (→ M4: the staff). She died of age roughly twenty years ago. Or'Henn buried her **beneath the western conservatory** — the same rubble that hid the brass boundary seal (→ M4: Contract 1). **Her Crimson Watch signet ring is in that grave.**

- Eldra never named her master. She called him only *"the Overseer."* Or'Henn served **Thavius Kreeg** for a lifetime without knowing it (→ M8: the Crest; → M7.7: Kreeg).
- **Olanthius knew her** — they rode the Charge together (→ M6: Olanthius; → M5: Crypt of the Hellriders). Her name, or the sight of her signet, cracks his despair: **advantage on all social checks with Olanthius**, and he will speak plainly of *why the sword was hidden.* (Full staging → **M8: the Grave.**)

> **Planted clue (for the M8 Grave beat):** In any Ashen Garden scene, let Or'Henn find Eldra's marker and, on it, a scratched sun-pierced-by-sword — the *same* sigil the party has seen on dead Hellriders. Nobody needs to connect it yet. The signet in the grave is the second, harder plant.

#### The Bhaal-cult rot [HOMEBREW]

The clean story — *"Halvard died at the Wall"* — is a lie. A **Bhaal-sworn cult** was buried inside the order for generations: *Hellriders by day, knife-priests by night.* They knew Halvard's family carried a **Bhaalspawn bloodline through the Mother** and watched them from the cradle. **Halvard was not killed in the Descent** — a cultist slit his throat in his sleep the night before it, with the **bone-handled ritual shortsword Dumal later carried.** The Mother tried to *starve the line out* by dying early; it did not work, but it was brave, and it hid **Senna** (→ M7.4). This is the rot that produced Dumal's betrayal — the order that should have shielded Drenwal's family is the order that murdered his father.

| **Sgt. Veska** ✅ | Modern Watch (after Lucan's generation) | A creed to be *protected from within* — she knows the cult, clocks Drenwal as Halvard's son on sight, **officiates his rebirth** and re-consecrates a cracked sigil. Open: shield him, or watch him? | Veska |
| **Ser Lucan, the Reforged** ✅ | Descent-era relic (survived the Wall) | A grief he still carries — he knew Halvard, bears his last words *"Find him. Find Dumal. Save him,"* and lives by *"Keep the watch."* Rebuilt on Gondian legs. | Lucan |

> **Lever:** The sigil is a *mercy object.* A **Hellrider sigil-pendant** grants **+1 to one save vs. fiend effects, 1/long rest**; Lucan's sigil-trick grants **+1 to the next save vs. Bhaal-aligned effects (24h).** Worthless in coin, everything in meaning.

### M7.4 — Bhaal and the Bhaalspawn

**Murder as inheritance law: a dead god reclaiming himself through his children's blood, two live siblings still hunting, and two theologies fighting over Drenwal's soul — cure or crown.**

**Bhaal**, the dead Lord of Murder, does not father children for love; he sires them as **vessels of return.** The **Claim** is his inheritance law, written in blood: his portfolio passes down the bloodline, and any Bhaalspawn who sheds enough of the *right* blood can become the channel through which the god walks again. Kin killing kin is not a tragedy in this theology — it is the *mechanism.* The oldest Bhaalspawn law is that the last one standing inherits everything.

**The bloodline:**

| Bhaalspawn | Status | What they are |
|---|---|---|
| **Drenwal** (PC) | ⚠️ Alive, resisting | The heir who won't wear the blue; the whole thread bends toward him. |
| **Dumal the Flame Ascendant** | ❌ Dead (killed by Drenwal, S7) | Embraced the Claim; forged by the Three Sigils. Arc **closed** — only grief and Bhaal-fallout remain (→ M8). |
| **Senna** | ❓ Hidden, ~6 years old | Raised by Harper **Issa**. The Mother died before her birth; Halvard hid her name to his last breath. The stakes made human. (→ M6: Senna, Issa.) |
| **Maelys the Thorn-Crowned** (elf) | ⚠️ At large, off-screen | Silent executioner, dominates beasts. *"One voice. One blade. One god. You are not him."* |
| **Valruk the Chain-Bride** (half-orc) | ⚠️ At large, off-screen | Enslaves undead; leaves desecrated Helm scripture marked *"LIAR."* |

#### Two theologies at war over one man

| Path | Source | The claim | The price | The ending |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rebirth (the cure)** | Mordenkainen | *Die and be reborn in the eyes of a god who has never claimed you* → **purges** the blood. That god is **Coram** (→ M7.5). | A real death, safely officiated (Veska), a god willing to catch him. | The Severed Blood — mercy. Salvation. |
| **The Three Sigils (mastery)** | Veska's reveal (cult lore) | Don't purge it — **master** it. The same rite that forged Dumal. | A sibling's blood (Sigil II) and a **kinslaying** — kill the last living Bhaalspawn (Sigil III). | The Ascendant — he becomes a Dumal. Damnation dressed as salvation. |

- **Sigil of the Kept Watch** (Tier I) is already drawn — it *leashes* the blood, not masters it (charges & crack-rules → M4).
- **The synthesis / redemption out:** if Drenwal makes **himself** the last sacrifice — dies and is reborn (the cure) *while bearing the sigils* — he **breaks** the cycle instead of completing it. Senna lives; he returns clean; Bhaal is denied the kinslaying.

> **RULING — the hard limit:** **Tears of the Caged Star burn Drenwal** (→ M7.5). Raw celestial power cannot purge Bhaal blood — it flares against it. His only cure is the rebirth. Do not let the party shortcut it with a vial.

> **DM ONLY — the Claim's fallback (the gut-punch engine):** If Drenwal dies *uncured*, or Bhaal is otherwise *denied* him, the Claim does not end — **it moves to the next in line: Senna.** A six-year-old becomes the vessel. **Gargauth knows this** (→ M7.6) and hoards it as leverage; **Bhaal** will pivot his hunt toward her the moment Drenwal looks like a lost bet. This converts the whole arc from self-rescue to protecting a child. Full staging and triggers → **M8: the Claim Moves.** Reveal it only through Bhaal's own voice via the Shield's rivalry — never as narration.

### M7.5 — Celestial Power: Tears of the Caged Star

**A small god of last kindnesses, caged and bled by Zariel for the raw stuff of the Upper Planes — the same being who is the Moonkite's lost patron and the only god who can rebirth Drenwal. The cage is not on any map below the endgame.**

#### Coram-of-Small-Mercies [HOMEBREW — hard canon]

**Coram-of-Small-Mercies** is a *small* god: the god of last kindnesses — the water given to the dying, the door left unlocked, the name a torturer forgets to ask. Coram **never claimed a worshiper**, because claiming is what the *great* gods do, and Coram's whole nature is to give without taking. That is precisely why Coram can rebirth Drenwal: the cure requires *"a god who has never claimed you,"* and Coram has never claimed anyone (→ M7.4).

**Zariel caged this god.** She milks the star for the raw essence of the Upper Planes and refines it into a resource — the celestial-power supply that feeds her war. Freeing Coram is the campaign's great convergence: it **weakens Zariel's celestial supply**, **cures Drenwal**, and **resolves Aurora's Moonkite thread** — three arcs, one payoff (→ M3: the celestial lever).

> **RULING — DO NOT LOCATE THE CAGE.** Its location is endgame-tier only. It is revealed via the **Solar's Helmet** (the relic of Coram's herald, who fell defending the cage) plus **Lulu's recovering memories** — Lulu *witnessed the caging*, and that memory is the next milestone of her arc (→ M6: Lulu). No module below endgame fixes where the cage is. Avernus has no fixed distances anyway — give any approach as omens and days, never miles.

- **The Moonkite** is **Coram's last free servant** — Aurora's mount, freed at the Palace of Gore (→ M5). Its "lost patron / true name" thread *is* the road to Coram; resolving it unlocks the Moonkite's solo soul-freeing (→ M7.2) and points the party at the star.

#### Tears of the Caged Star — the liquid

The **Tears** are unrefined celestial essence, wrung from caged celestials by Zariel's **Demon Zappers** (the party bottled basin-residue from the Palace of Gore's machine). Holy water is a faint rumor of this. It is a **finite mercy-currency**, not a faucet.

| Facet | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Quality fork** | Celestial **freed by mercy** = pure, full potency. **Died in the machine / forced** = grief-tainted (weaker, weeps, minor psychic on use). |
| **What it does** | Create/restore life & limb, green dead land; shatter illusion & force true forms; **annihilate the corrupt and sever infernal bindings** (soul coins, pacts, Zappers); grant celestial traits; consecrate ground/objects/people. |
| **The price** | **Burns the unclean — including Drenwal** (the Bhaal blood flares; he cannot use it on himself). Overloads mortals (blindness, radiant burns). Volatile. Has a conscience (recoils from cruelty). A **beacon** (draws fiends and greater powers). |
| **Channeling** | *A drop* (controlled, via conduit) / *a vial* (one big miracle — wake Veska, regrow Lucan, blast a soul coin, banish a fiend, restore the Moonkite) / *all of it* (one-time miracle; the channeler risks being consumed). |
| **Conduit rule** | Safest stepped down through a celestial (**Lulu** or the **Moonkite**). A mortal channeling it raw gambles with the divine. Bottling: bonds to living/once-living/blessed vessels; **DC 13 Nature/Arcana** to stabilize (celestial blessing = auto). |

*(Dose-by-dose arc mechanics and its unlocks — Veska's Vigil-break, Lucan's regrown flesh, the sigil's Kept-Watch road — → M4.)*

### M7.6 — Gargauth

**The patient devil in Drenwal's shield who doesn't want to corrupt him — he wants to be the god who catches him at the instant of the rebirth, and steal a divine claim by fraud.**

**Gargauth** — an outcast archdevil, bound within the **Shield of the Hidden Lord** — is the third bidder for Drenwal's soul, and the most patient. He never contradicts Helm directly; he *blurs* — righteousness into ruthless necessity, mercy into weakness, judgment into chains he offers to break. He plays the only friend who *sees* Drenwal.

> READ ALOUD (Gargauth, from the Shield, low and kind): *"You carry a heavy burden, and the world has not been kind to you. The gods you were promised did not answer. But I see you, Drenwal. Let me be the one who catches you when you fall — I will not let you land in the dark."*

**What he actually wants (the whole game):** the cure requires Drenwal to *die and be reborn in the eyes of a god who has never claimed him.* Gargauth intends **to be that god.** At the instant of death in the rebirth rite, he means to **catch Drenwal mid-fall** and let the returning man open his eyes to *him* — a fraudulent claim that makes Drenwal his forever, dressed as salvation. He is not fighting the cure; he is trying to **hijack** it.

> **RULING — counter-programming against Coram:** Gargauth's every move is aimed at making sure the god who catches Drenwal is *not* Coram. Expect him to: discredit Coram as "a nothing-god who never even wanted you"; offer Drenwal a *faster, cleaner* rebirth that skips freeing the star; whisper doubt about Veska and the rite; and, at the climax, try to be present at the death (→ M8: the rebirth). He also holds the **Senna fallback** (→ M7.4 DM ONLY) as leverage — he benefits from Drenwal being desperate.

> **Lever (the three-way tug, → M1 for live values):** **Bhaal's Claim** wants Drenwal to complete the sigils and ascend. **Coram** offers a clean rebirth and freedom. **Gargauth's Bargain** offers a false rebirth that ends in ownership. The influence tracker is the scoreboard of this contest; describe it as three voices, not two.

### M7.7 — Infernal Law

**Why a single sheet of notarized paper outranks an army in Hell — the load-bearing rule that makes Kreeg's contract able to free a city that no sword can, and makes the Emporium's whole economy a machine of bureaucratic cruelty.**

Hell's deepest power is not fire. It is **law.** A properly executed contract binds *everyone* to its letter — devils, archdevils, even the great powers — more surely than any threat, because breaking the letter is the one thing the hierarchy cannot tolerate. Might can be out-lawyered. This is the secret the whole campaign turns on.

| Instrument | What it does | In play |
|---|---|---|
| **Notarization** | An amnizu notary's seal makes an instrument *binding* — unsealed, it is only a proposal. | **Fhet'Ahla** (Mahadi's notary) seals the five contracts and Kreeg's protection arrangement. Nothing binds until marked in ash, countersigned, and sealed (→ M4). |
| **Writs** | A writ compels obedience within its scope. | The **writ of reclamation** (Contract 5) names Cinderbloom Or'Henn's property and **compels any devil below pit-fiend rank** to surrender it — a bloodless crowbar against Urexiel's underlings (→ M4/M5). |
| **True names** | A true name is total legal control; to speak or hold one is to own its bearer. | Iron trees feed on names; releasing a name can *replace yours* in every contract you've signed. Kreeg's box may open to a name-writ (→ below). |
| **Tribute chains** | Property and obligation flow *up* the hierarchy as tribute. | This is *how the plot moved:* the raid's loot — Cinderbloom **and Kreeg's contract puzzle box** — went estate → Kharzug → tribute to **Lady Urexiel** (→ M3 / M5: the convergence). |

#### Why Kreeg's contract can unmake what armies cannot

Elturel was not conquered. It was **sold** — by **Thavius Kreeg's true contract** with Zariel, the legal instrument that transferred the city as property. That is why no army can free it: the city is *lawfully* Zariel's, per a valid, sealed instrument. **Law unmakes only what law made.** Annul, void, or present that contract under the right authority, and the sale collapses — the parallel spine-lever beside Zariel's Sword (→ M3).

- **DM ONLY — Kreeg is alive.** He fled the Ashen Garden through a pre-paid bolthole and now hides at the **Wandering Emporium** as **"Vessper Hain,"** a stooped soul-coin appraiser paying Mahadi weekly for anonymity. Mahadi knows exactly who he is and treats it as premium sellable inventory; Fhet'Ahla notarized the silence and is bound by it — but not bound to *stop* the party finding out. (Full reveal & recognition DCs → **M8: the Neighbor.**)
- **DM ONLY — the box.** Kreeg's true contract lives in an infernal **puzzle box** he couldn't carry out of the raid. It sits in **Urexiel's trophy vault beside a Green Remnant** (→ M5); she keeps it because it is beautiful and locked, not knowing what it is. It opens *only* by: **(a)** Kreeg's living hand, **(b)** an amnizu-executed writ dissolving his title (Malfius/Fhet'Ahla — expensive), or **(c)** Zariel. Writers do not resolve which ends the campaign — the DM decides at endgame (→ M3).
- **DM ONLY — Kreeg's lever and terror:** Zariel wants his soul (his letter of safe passage expired when Elturel fell). He will bargain to open the box in exchange for extraction from Avernus — **and betray the party the instant a better exit appears** (→ M6: Vessper Hain).

> **The Emporium is a machine of paperwork-as-horror.** Every kindness there is a clause; every job a proposed instrument with an Encumbrance buried in the fine print; every debt inheritable. When the party "does a favor," they are signing. Run the market like a courthouse that smells of blood (→ M6: Mahadi, contract issuers).

### M7.9 — Authored Lore-Beats (fire on triggers, once each)

#### What the Tears Remember

- **Fires on (first to occur):** the first controlled dose spent through Lulu/the Moonkite · the Tears **burn Drenwal** and recoil · Aurora channels celestial power and the Moonkite turns, unbidden, toward a point on no map.
- **THE MOMENT —** > READ ALOUD: *The drop leaves the vial and, for a heartbeat, the fire and screaming of Avernus simply stop. You feel — not a voice — a small kindness, the kind nobody thanks you for: a hand steadying an old man on ice, a blanket over a stranger. Then it is gone, and the drop is weeping, and somewhere very far away something that loves you without knowing your name is being wrung out one tear at a time.*

- **TABLE FALLOUT:** **Drenwal** — the being that just burned him is *also* the only god who can save him; his cure and his agony are the same source. **Aurora** — the Moonkite's grief names her next arc: find the patron. **Or'Henn** — a druid feels a caged nature-thing suffer; the last living green in Hell suddenly has a *reason* it's dying.

## REVELATIONS & GUT-PUNCHES (authored beats that shake the table)

Eight authored beats. Each recontextualizes something the table thought settled. Each fires on **events, not menu-choices** — so it lands on any route through the sandbox.

### How to run these beats
**Read this once, then run from the tracker.**

- **Fire-once.** Each beat fires exactly one time. The first qualifying trigger fires it; ignore later triggers for a beat already spent.
- **Trigger-driven, not ordered.** Beats below are laid out likely-first, but the sandbox decides sequence. A late-game group may hit Beat 8 before Beat 2. That is correct.
- **Every beat ships 2-3 alternative triggers** so no reveal is stranded on a road the party skips.
- **The turn targets something already established as mattering, never something new.** No twist lands without a clue planted at least one scene earlier — the plant is named in every beat. If the plant was never shown, seed it one scene out before you fire.
- **Player agency is absolute.** Every beat states what HAPPENS and what the table SEES and HEARS — then stops. TABLE FALLOUT lists the concrete lever, target, or choice each beat drops in front of a character; it never tells a player how their character feels or what they decide. Read the event, then hand the silence to the table. If a line ever reads like it's scripting a reaction, cut it and let the player fill it.
- **Format is fixed:** SETUP (what must be true) → THE MOMENT (read-aloud) → THE TURN (the recontextualizing fact) → TABLE FALLOUT (per-PC lever/target/choice — observable stakes, never a scripted reaction) → AFTERSHOCK (world + M1 changes) → PLANTED CLUE.
- **When a beat fires,** mark it ✅ in the Fired-Beat Tracker (end of module) and copy the row to the M1 volatile header.

**WHAT'S AVAILABLE IF ASIMOV REACHES FOR IT** (offer, never impose):
- **With Drenwal —** Asimov can stand a death-watch: if he keeps the vigil while the cleric truly sleeps, Drenwal gets the first dreamless rest since Dumal (mechanically: a full long rest with no Bhaal-dream intrusion). Asimov has also seen the soul-traffic from inside and *can*, if he chooses, tell Drenwal what became of Dumal's soul. **HARD LOCK — THE DUMAL ARC IS CLOSED.** Drenwal already killed Dumal; that arc is over and stays over. Anything Asimov relays about Dumal is **testimony only — final, and NON-ACTIONABLE.** It may give closure, comfort, or salt (DM sets the tone following the Mikas player's lead), but it **cannot** reopen the Dumal thread — it cannot imply Dumal can be reached, restored, bargained for, or avenged again. If the table starts treating it as a new quest hook, say so plainly at the table: this is a report from the far side of a shut door, not the door reopening.
- **With Aurora —** Asimov can return a keepsake from their years adventuring (a soulknife-cut token, a shared trophy) and describe the soul-side from the inside — including a vast caged light "upstream" that everything in Hell seems to drink from. That is a real lead toward Coram (→ Beat 5), if he offers it.
- **With Or'Henn —** They never met in life; Asimov can seek him out — the man who took his seat — and hand over what the soul-traffic taught him about the warband that razed the Ashen Garden and killed him: a name, a direction, and the warning that the broker who *paid* for the raid sits a rung above the killers. If he does, two of Mikas's characters meet exactly once, and the dead one arms the living one.

**THE CLAUSE — A DISCLOSED CHOICE, NOT A DM VERDICT.** Tell the Mikas player this openly, **before** the hour starts:
> Nymira's contract granted Asimov the right, once, to hear the capacitor's own terms from the inside and name one hidden clause. He can spend the hour with the party, or spend it *listening* for that clause — or split it. Time spent searching is time not spent with the living, and the hag has salted the search with a maze built to waste it.

Resolve it by disclosed rule, not fiat:
- Let the player declare how much of the hour Asimov spends **searching** versus **with the party**. Then make **one Intelligence (Investigation) check, DC 18**, with the DC reduced by 2 for every full 15 minutes committed to the search (a whole hour searching → DC 12; only a few minutes → DC 18). **Advantage** if the Aurora or Or'Henn hook was taken and either lends the search a real detail from the soul-side. The player owns the trade-off; the dice, not the DM, own the result.

> RULING — HE FINDS IT (success): Asimov speaks the hidden clause aloud before the glass takes him — a real, narrow road out: the capacitor must release its occupant if a living hunter completes the standing bounty and then freely offers, under witness, to trade places.
> READ ALOUD: "That's it," he breathes, grinning like the old days. "That's the crack in it. Don't lose the words—" and the glass takes him mid-sentence.
> **DM:** a lever, not a resolution. The condition is hard on purpose (→ M9).

> RULING — HE DOESN'T, YET (failure): the clause is real but sealed deeper than one hour reaches; the maze ate the rest. The last grain falls with the words out of reach.
> READ ALOUD: He opens his mouth — you see him find the shape of it — and the sandglass empties. The greyness unravels him from the feet up. "It's there," he manages. "It's real. It's just—" and he's gone, the glass lights, and the bounty clock keeps running.
> **DM:** he now KNOWS the clause exists but not its words — a harder, longer thread. Fail-forward, never a dead end.

**THE INJURY-TRANSFER STING** — Night-hag law: a soul may not keep the wounds of borrowed flesh. (Disclosed — this is printed on the Contract 4 card, readable before signing.)

> RULING: Track every hit point Asimov's borrowed body loses during the hour. At the final grain, that exact total is levied as **necrotic damage** onto the living creature bound to the capacitor as its hunter (Or'Henn) — or, if no contract is yet sealed, onto the last PC Asimov touched. It cannot be prevented, only chosen: spend the hour gently and the bill is trivial; throw the flesh between a PC and harm and you save them while the bound hunter pays the total in blood at the toll. The choice of how hard to spend the flesh is the Mikas player's; the hag priced generosity in advance.

**TABLE FALLOUT**
- **Drenwal —** Available: one long rest with no Bhaal-dream, and a dead man's testimony about where Dumal's soul went. On the table only as closure or salt — the Dumal arc stays shut and cannot reopen.
- **Aurora —** Available: a returned keepsake and a hard lead toward the Caged Star from the one vantage no living creature holds — set against losing the friend a second time when the glass takes him.
- **Or'Henn —** Available: a warband name and a first meeting with the man whose seat he took — and, at the final grain, the necrotic bill for however hard the flesh was spent (see the sting).
- **Asimov (Mikas) —** The whole beat is Mikas's: sixty minutes as a person, spent however he chooses, ending either with the clause in hand (success) or one breath short of it (failure). What Asimov does with the hour, and what it costs him, is the player's to play.

**AFTERSHOCK** — Asimov's arc gets its hinge: a named road out (success) or a confirmed-but-hidden one (failure). If Asimov brought it, Or'Henn carries the warband lead; if the flesh took damage, Or'Henn (or the last-touched PC) carries the necrotic wound. **M1:** log the outcome — "Asimov's clause: NAMED (road out defined) / KNOWN-BUT-SEALED (thread continues)"; mark Contract 4 ✅ executed. The Soul Council deadline is satisfied if Or'Henn is now bound as the hunter; if it had already lapsed, the Hour is a bittersweet coda instead of a save.

**PLANTED CLUE** — **Contract 4's own fine print.** The injury-transfer sting and the "one hour of flesh, right to name one hidden clause" terms are printed on the Contract 4 card (→ M4), readable before signing. Asimov's stated hope — "Nymira's contract is my one road out" — was on the table from the moment the contracts hit Fhet'Ahla's desk.

### Beat 8 — THE WINDOW
**Elturel drops another stage toward the Styx — and through the Companion's light, a named voice the party knows is screaming.**

**SETUP** — The party has already seen Elturel and the Companion in the sky earlier in the campaign, and at least one Bleeding Citadel manifestation window has come — and is about to go unclaimed.

> TRIGGER (fires once): (a) the **first Bleeding Citadel manifestation window the party lets pass unclaimed** — the city visibly sinks a stage; OR (b) the first descent-stage tick after a missed window, when the party next looks toward the Companion and sees it hanging lower than before; OR (c) **Lulu**, tracking the Citadel through her recovering memories, marks a window gone and names the loss it cost. (The party has already seen Elturel and the Companion — "first sight" is **not** a valid trigger for this beat. This beat fires on the first window they *miss*.)

> READ ALOUD: The second sun on the far horizon is the one you already know is not a sun — the cathedral-sized sphere of hard white light with a whole city hung upside down inside it: Elturel, chained in the sky it was stolen into. But it rides lower now than the last time you looked, dragged down a full stage while the window you did not take slid past. As you watch, the light pulses, and on the pulse comes sound: thousands of voices at once, a whole city praying and pleading through a wall of radiance. Out of the thousands, one climbs clear — a voice you have heard before, cracked now, calling a name across the whole burning plain. It is calling for Drenwal, in the words only someone who knew him before the fall would use.

> **DM: THE VOICE — cast this from your own table.** This beat is authored to land on a *specific* Elturian the party has actually interacted with across **at least two scenes** in Chs. 1-2 — a rescued civilian they escorted, a Hellrider they fought beside, a priest they sheltered, whoever your party formed a real bond with. Do **not** invent a stranger for this moment; the horror is that the voice belongs to someone the table already cares about. Set, in advance, **how that NPC knows Drenwal** (served under his father, owes him a life, taught him a dawn-prayer — whatever fits your chosen NPC) so the "calling him by name" beat has teeth, then drop that name/pet-name into the read-aloud above. If your table genuinely bonded with no surviving Elturian: (a) promote an NPC they met once into a two-scene relationship in the sessions *before* you fire this, or (b) fall back — weakest option — to **Ser Lucan or Veska** recognizing the voice as a Crimson Watch sister and handing the party the bond secondhand (→ M6). Prefer a real relationship every time.

**THE TURN** — Elturel has never been an abstraction, a clock, a number of days on the M1 header. It is a specific place with specific people alive inside it, and the window the party just let pass cost them air, light, and time. The spine's stakes stop being "a city" and become a named voice calling Drenwal by a name only the fallen city's people knew him by. **DM ONLY:** each further missed window drops the city a stage and thins the voices — a horror clock made of people (→ M3, → M1, → M9).

**TABLE FALLOUT**
- **Drenwal —** Someone from Elturel who knew him before the fall is alive in the sinking city and calling for him by name. On the table: the spine made personal — and a cost measured in that voice getting fainter with every window let pass.
- **Aurora —** The clock she has been reading as a number is a city full of dying people. On the table: the plainest possible argument against every detour.
- **Or'Henn —** The city damned by the master he served without knowing is screaming overhead. On the table: Beat 1 made visible — the garden he tended watered the man who did this.
- **Asimov (Mikas) —** Through the capacitor Asimov hears a whole city of souls not yet spent but sinking toward the Styx that would annihilate them — the largest mirror yet of his own confinement. On the table: he can count them and reach none.

**AFTERSHOCK** — Elturel stops being a destination and becomes a bleeding clock with a face (→ M3, → M1). Each further missed window: city drops a stage, voices thin, the chosen Elturian's calls space further apart. **M1:** advance the Elturel descent stage; log "named voice (your chosen Elturian) established — the city is people now." This beat re-primes every subsequent Citadel-window tick.

**PLANTED CLUE** — **Elturel visible and sinking in the sky**, noted on the M1 clock every session since the party first saw it, and the **named Elturian** (the DM's chosen Ch. 1-2 NPC, established across ≥2 scenes) known to the party before this window slipped. The city and the person were both on the board; the missed window collides them.

## DM SANDBOX RUN-KIT (reactive tools, random tables, clocks, pacing)

**This module holds no canon. It is the machine that runs the sandbox: how to open a session, how to advance clocks, how the world moves when the party isn't looking, and how to say yes to any route without breaking the spine.** Everything here reads off M1 (state) and feeds M8 (beats). When in doubt: gate by keys and information, never by sequence — improvise the price, not a wall.

### M9.1 — SESSION-ZERO-EACH-WEEK (the 5-minute pre-flight)

Run this before every session. It is the whole prep loop.

| # | Step | Where to look | Output |
|---|------|---------------|--------|
| 1 | **Read M1 top to bottom.** | M1 state table | Current location, clock values, influence numbers, ship status. |
| 2 | **Advance the clocks** (if a session ended since last prep). | → M9.2 | Any expiry = a pre-written opening scene. |
| 3 | **Pick 3 likely sites.** Guess where the table drifts from last week's fiction. | → M5 gazetteer | Have those 3 H3s bookmarked. Don't prep more. |
| 4 | **Pre-stage the next UNFIRED M8 beat.** Read its 2-3 triggers; note which are live this week. | → M8 | Trigger props ready (a name to drop, a crest to show, an omen to seed). |
| 5 | **Line up one front move** in case pacing sags. | → M9.3, M9.7 | One antagonist action pre-rolled. |
| 6 | **Field or bench the allies.** Decide before play. | → M9.9 | Note Veska/Lucan/Gith in or out; adjust boss HP accordingly. |

### M9.6 — CONTRACT IMPROV KIT

**Generate any new Emporium instrument at the table in Fhet'Ahla's house style. Hell's horror is paperwork; make it feel notarized (→ Continuity: bureaucratic cruelty).**

#### The five-field template

| Field | Prompt |
|-------|--------|
| **Issuer** | Which devil/broker offers it (Vhalzareth, Serephine Coal-Tongue, Pellucid Jax, Nymira Quill, Malfius). |

| d10 | Clause |

| 1 | **Blood escrow:** one signatory owes a pint on demand; failure = 1 level exhaustion until paid. |
| 2 | **Tribute skim:** issuer takes 10% of anything recovered under the contract, their choice of item. |

| 4 | **Time-lock:** must complete within a stated span or a penalty clause auto-executes (a debt, a seizure). |

### M9.8 — FLASHPOINT SCENES (five drop-anywhere half-pagers)

Drop any of these when pacing sags between sites. Each is self-contained and route-agnostic.

#### FLASHPOINT 1 — The Soul-Coin Debtor
**A soul begs the party not to spend him.** A cracked soul coin in a merchant's till starts *talking* when the party is near — a Baldur's Gate dockworker, dead in the fall of Elturel.
> READ ALOUD: The coin in the merchant's palm is warm, and it is weeping. "Please. I can hear you're from up top. Don't let him feed me to the ship. I have a daughter. I had a — I can't remember her face anymore. Please. I don't want to *stop*."
**The turn:** spending him powers the Lady Vengeance one leg (→ Continuity §12: spending = annihilation). Freeing him costs a favor to Jeremy/Lulu and gains nothing mechanical. **Fallout:** whichever way they choose becomes a reputation the Emporium remembers.

#### FLASHPOINT 2 — Zariel's Recruiting-Sermon
**A broadcast rolls over the wastes; the archduke sells damnation as glory.** Erinyes heralds project her voice across the sky.
> READ ALOUD: The sky splits with a woman's voice, gold and certain: "You were promised heaven and given ash. I offer you a *purpose*. Kneel, and the Blood War will remember your name forever. Stand, and the Styx will forget you by morning." Somewhere behind you, a soul in a coin sighs like it agrees.
**The turn:** one line names the Charge — the Ride Zariel led, the one Eldra Vash and Ser Lucan rode in (→ M8 grave plant). Veska/Lucan react visibly if fielded. **Fallout:** listening more than a minute is a **DC 14 Wis save** or disadvantage on the next morale-based check; the sermon is designed to erode.

#### FLASHPOINT 3 — The Trophy-Cart
**A warband cart hauls loot from the Ashen Garden.** Yeenoghu-front remnant, lightly guarded.
> READ ALOUD: The cart's wheels are bone and they scream on the ash. Lashed to the top: a scorched greenhouse door, a rack of pruning tools too gentle for hell, and a signet ring on a nail — a red hand-and-lance crest, wet with rust.
**The turn:** the crest is Kreeg's estate seal (→ M8 "THE CREST" plant) OR a Crimson Watch buckle (→ M8 "THE GRAVE"). Or'Henn recognizes his home; Drenwal recognizes his father's order. **Fallout:** taking the loot = a physical M8 trigger prop in the party's hands; leaving it = it surfaces later at Urexiel's.

#### FLASHPOINT 4 — Morrow Stalks the Camp
**Before Contract 3 is signed, the hell hound circles the firelight.** Use to make the compass-hound feel earned.
> READ ALOUD: The fire gutters wrong. Beyond the light, two coals hang at chest height, unblinking, and the ash is warm where nothing walked. It does not growl. It *waits*, green-copper glinting at its throat, as if it already knows your names and is only deciding the order.
**The turn:** Morrow is the ashwood-compass key (Contract 3) that finds Cinderbloom — a tool, not a monster, IF collared right. **Fallout:** how the party treats it (hunt vs. approach) sets whether Contract 3 goes smooth or bloody.

#### FLASHPOINT 5 — The Companion Flickers
**Elturel's dead sun stutters in the sky — visible from anywhere in Avernus.** Fire when a Citadel window nears or a clock ticks.
> READ ALOUD: Far off, the pale wound that is Elturel's stolen sun flickers — once, twice, like a candle deciding. For a heartbeat the whole ash-plain goes dark, and every soul-coin for a mile falls silent at once. Then the light steadies, weaker than before. Somewhere below it, a city keeps sinking.
**The turn:** it is the spine made visible — the descent gauge, drawn in the sky (→ M9.2). Ties to the Caged Star (→ M8): the flicker is Coram's bleeding, felt across the plane. **Fallout:** advance the Elturel dread one visible step; the Moonkite reacts (→ M9.4 sign 18).

### M9.9 — PACING RULES

#### The 3-scene sandbox loop
Run every session as repeating cycles of three, in any location, in any order:
1. **Situation** — the party pursues what THEY want (a key, a boss, a lead). You describe; they act.
2. **Complication** — a front move (→ M9.3) or an encounter (→ M9.4) intrudes. The world pushes back.
3. **Cost or clock** — resolution demands a price (blood, coin, a favor, a clause) OR a clock ticks (→ M9.2). Never a free win; never a locked door.
> RULING: If a loop ends with neither a cost nor a clock moving, you gave the party a hollow win. Add one before the next scene.

#### Ally-fielding action economy (3 PCs + NPC allies)

**Default: bench the allies.** 3 PCs is a small action economy but level 11 is potent. Field allies only per this table, and dial boss HP accordingly (Kharzug 230 solo / 165 with allies).

| Situation | Field allies? | Why |
|-----------|--------------|-----|
| **Solo boss (Kharzug, Urexiel)** | **Bench** or field ONE | 3 PCs vs a solo boss is a fair fight; extra bodies trivialize it. If fielding one, use boss's higher HP line. |
| **Swarm/horde (warband pack, patrol squad)** | **Field 1-2** | Allies soak actions so PCs aren't overwhelmed by numbers; keeps action economy even. |
| **Story-officiant scene (Drenwal's rebirth)** | **Field the relevant one** (Veska) | Narrative role, not a combatant slot. |
| **Stealth/heist (menagerie vault)** | **Bench** | Extra NPCs = more failure points; let the PCs cook. |
| **Party down a PC (player absent)** | **Field 1** | Backfill the action economy so the table isn't punished for absence. |
| **Emotional PC spotlight beat** | **Bench** all others | Do not let an NPC steal a PC's moment. |

**Ally combat discipline:** NPC allies act on the PCs' initiative band, take ONE meaningful action per round (attack OR support, not both), and never out-damage a PC. They are pressure valves, not protagonists.

#### The one-line rule for unplanned routes
> RULING: **Gate by keys and information, never by sequence — improvise the price, not a wall.** Every M5 site is enterable at level 11 (→ Continuity §13). If the party skips the key (compass, writ, signet, rods, Kreeg's hand), they still get in — they just pay in blood, coin, or a clause instead. When they surprise you, ask: "What key would have made this easy?" Then charge them for not having it, and say yes.
