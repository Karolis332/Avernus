# The Wandering Emporium — Economic & Goods Reference

**Companion to `session-8-wandering-emporium-primer.md`.** The primer runs the *scene* — arrival, Mahadi's welcome, the truce, the recruitment, the bounty board. **This document runs the *store*.** It is the table-ready economic layer: every shop's full stock and services, prices in soul coins and gold, per-merchant haggle DCs, and the negotiation system that ties them together. Pull it up any time the party is *buying, selling, repairing, borrowing, or bargaining* at the Emporium — this visit (repair the Lady Vengeance, resupply, gather intel) or any future one. It does **not** restate the five soul-bounties' fine print (that lives in `orhenn-dm-primer.html` / the primer) and it does **not** re-stat NPCs (see `npc-reference.md`); it points at both and gets you to a number fast.

> **How to use it at the table:** find the shop, read the attitude line, hand the party the stock table, take one haggle check against the listed DC, apply the discount band, done. The **Quick Prices cheat-sheet** (§8) is the at-a-glance version when you just need a number *now*.

## 2. The soul-coin economy [HOMEBREW numbers on a D&D CANON frame]

The Emporium is the **one place in Avernus where commerce works** — but it does not run on gold. It runs on souls.

### The currencies (in order of how much the market respects them)

| Currency | What it is | How the Emporium treats it |
|---|---|---|
| **Soul coin (SC)** | [D&D CANON] A warm iron coin holding **one real, conscious, suffering damned soul.** The coin of the realm. | **Principal currency.** Everything is priced in SC first. |
| **Bargains / favors / obligations** | [D&D CANON] A promise, a service owed, a marked debt. | **Prized above coin** for the *good* stuff. A favor can beat any gold price — but it becomes a *debt* (→ possibly a Fhet'Ahla contract). |
| **Gold (gp)** | [D&D CANON] Mostly worthless in Hell; grudgingly spendable *only here.* | **Accepted at a poor rate**, with a sneer and a "mortal surcharge." Never buys premium or soul-goods. |
| **Soul-goods** | [HOMEBREW] Larvae, soul-stock, heartstones, un-notarized secrets — soul-value in kind. | Traded like commodities (see Z'neth §7.6, Mahadi §7.1). |

### The conversion (set this once, tell the DM) [HOMEBREW]

> **1 SC ≈ 100 gp of infernal purchasing power.**

- This is a *deliberate* scaling choice so a soul-coin-capable, level-11 party faces **meaningful** costs — a soul coin should feel like real money, because it *is* a real soul (see the moral cost below).
- **Gold surcharge:** paying in gp costs **~2× the SC-equivalent** (i.e. gold trades at roughly **half value** — call it **~200 gp per 1 SC**), and comes with attitude. Some things (premium services, all soul-goods, Mahadi's best information, war-machine fuel) are **SC-or-bargain only** and *cannot be bought with gold at any rate.* Those rows are marked **SC/bargain only** in the tables.
- **Rounding for play:** when a table lists both, the gp figure already bakes in the surcharge. Use SC when the party pays in coin; use the gp figure when they insist on gold.

### The moral cost — say it once, then let it sit [D&D CANON, per `souls-and-celestial-power.md`]

> **Spending a soul coin as *payment* is not the same as spending gold.**

- **Trading or handing over** a coin: the soul persists, still trapped — **reversible.** (This is how most Emporium purchases work; the coin changes hands, the soul suffers on.)
- **Spending a coin as *fuel*** (war-machine fuel, the Soul Coin Overdrive, feeding it to a Gondian core, draining it dry): the soul is **annihilated — true final death, no afterlife, not even *Wish*.** The Lady Vengeance *refuel* line (§7.2) is exactly this: every fuel-coin Rassh loads **erases a person forever.**
- **DM cue:** the merchants don't care. *Drenwal* should. When the party pays a fuel price in coins, remind them what a coin is. That is the price behind the price.

### What the party can pay with, this visit

They arrive holding (per the primer / digest): **loose soul coins**, **Red Ruth's soul-stock**, her **heartstone**, and the **Mordenkainen letter** — plus a party purse of gold that Avernus barely wants. Mahadi covets the last three (leverage/currency; §7.1). Every table below assumes SC as the default and notes when a **bargain** or **soul-good** is the smarter pay.

## 3. The haggling system [HOMEBREW]

One market, one clean subsystem. Define it here; each shop just supplies a **Haggle DC** and an **Insight DC**.

### The check

When a price is on the table, a player may attempt **one** social check to move it. The player **picks the angle** and rolls that skill vs. the merchant's **Haggle DC**:

- **Persuasion** — reason, rapport, repeat-customer goodwill, "Heroes of the Last Hill."
- **Intimidation** — leverage, menace (careful: the truce means *no violence*, so this is pure social pressure; overdo it and attitude drops).
- **Deception** — bluff a competing offer, feign disinterest, misrepresent what you carry.

**One serious attempt per item per visit.** A second angle on the same item is at **disadvantage** (they've heard the pitch). Group "help" gives advantage only if a second PC plausibly contributes (a good-cop/bad-cop, a shared history). Party face + one assist is the intended shape.

### The result bands

| Roll vs. Haggle DC | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Beat DC by 5+** | Best case: **~30% off**, *or* the listed discount **plus a thrown-in extra** (a minor consumable, a free small service, a scrap of information). Attitude ticks **up**. |
| **Meet or beat DC** | **10–20% off** the listed price (DM picks within the band; use 20% on a clean beat, 10% on a squeaker), *or* a thrown-in extra of similar value. |
| **Fail (miss by 1–4)** | **Price holds.** No deal sweetener. Merchant's attitude **drops one step** for this visit (see tiers). The offer is still *available* at list. |
| **Fail by 5+** | **Soured.** The offer may be **withdrawn** for the visit, *or* the merchant slaps on an **inflated "mortal tax"** (+25% and gp-only). Relationship damaged into the next visit. With Mahadi/Fhet'Ahla, a bad fail can *cost a favor* to smooth over. |

- **Discounts stack with nothing.** One check, one band. Don't let a party re-roll a room.
- **Cap the discount.** The bands above are the ceiling. Some merchants **won't flex past a hard floor** (noted per shop) — beating the DC by 5+ on an inflexible item gets a *thrown-in extra*, not a bigger cut.
- **You barely haggle the apex predators.** **Mahadi** and **Fhet'Ahla** have high DCs *and* low caps — see their sections. Mahadi in particular treats a haggle as a *game he's letting you win*; the "discount" is usually a courtesy that deepens the hospitality debt.

### Attitude tiers (per-merchant, per-visit)

Each shop starts at a listed attitude. Haggle results, roleplay, and what the party carries move it. Attitude sets the *feel* and shifts the effective Haggle DC by ±2.

| Tier | Behavior | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** | Genuinely glad of the custom (Burney's default; any merchant after a great beat). | Haggle DC **−2**; volunteers a tip or a small favor. |
| **Cordial** | Professional, transactional. Most merchants' default toward the "Heroes of the Last Hill." | List prices; DCs as written. |
| **Wary** | Guarded — a fumbled haggle, an implied threat, or carrying something they want. | Haggle DC **+2**; gp-only for premium rows. |
| **Cold** | Insulted or spooked (fail-by-5, truce-breach vibes). | No haggling; list or *mortal-tax* prices only; may refuse a sale. |

### Insight — read the merchant before you push

Each shop lists an **Insight DC** to clock its *real* attitude, its hard limits, and whether it wants something the party has. A success tells the player **where the floor is** (so they know when to stop pushing) and flags any hidden hook. This is the *safe* way to scout a haggle before committing the check.

### Paying in bargains / favors (the off-ledger channel) [HOMEBREW]

> **A favor can beat any gold price.** It can also beat a soul-coin price — but it is never free.

- Offering a **favor, service, or obligation** in place of coin can knock a price down hard (DM's call; often equivalent to a **30%+ discount** or a waived fee) **or** unlock a **SC/bargain-only** item that gold simply can't buy.
- The catch: the favor becomes a **debt**, tracked. Small favors settle in play (deliver a message, certify a thing, spread a rumor). Large ones — anything Mahadi or Fhet'Ahla brokers — should be written down and, if big enough, **notarized into an infernal contract** (→ Fhet'Ahla, §7.4). An unpaid Emporium debt *compounds* and *follows the party.*
- **Mahadi's hospitality is the trap version of this** (§7.1): the "free" thing is a favor **you didn't agree to price**, and he sets the rate later.

## 7.1 Infernal Rapture — Mahadi [D&D CANON]

**Proprietor:** **Mahadi**, rakshasa (CR 13; Magic Resistance, Limited Magic Immunity, Innate Spellcasting — see `npc-reference.md` and the primer's Truce appendix; never needed unless the truce breaks). Restaurant, spa, sanctuary, information hub, and procurement broker — the beating heart of the caravan.

**Attitude:** **Cordial**, warm on the surface (he receives the party as returning "Heroes of the Last Hill"). Genuinely gracious; genuinely calculating. **Insight DC 18** [D&D CANON] — he fears angering Asmodeus, and *every kindness is an investment*; a success reveals that his "free" offers are priced, just not yet.

**Haggling Mahadi:** you barely do. His Haggle DC is **20** and his **discount cap is 10%** — and even a success he frames as *"for you, of course"*, i.e. a courtesy that **adds to the hospitality debt** rather than truly saving coin. He will *never* be intimidated (Intimidation vs. Mahadi is at disadvantage and risks a Wary drop); Persuasion and Deception are the only real angles, and rapport/Red-Ruth-leverage matter more than the die.

### ⚠ Hospitality-debt table [D&D CANON engine, HOMEBREW rates]

**Nothing here is free.** Each "on the house" kindness places the recipient in Mahadi's debt; he sets the price later, and each return visit **compounds** it. Track it. Refusing to settle escalates toward an infernal contract.

| The "free" thing | What it actually costs, later |
|---|---|
| The free first course | A **truthful answer** to one question Mahadi asks (about the party's business, their cargo, their plans). |
| A free soak / grooming referral | A **small service** owed — carry a message, vouch for him, drop a word where he wants it heard. |
| A gifted feast / free long rest | A **named favor "to be called"** — undefined now, defined by Mahadi when convenient. The dangerous one. |
| Accepting his "generosity" a *second* visit | The prior debt **compounds**: what was a favor becomes a *favor plus interest* — often the thing he actually wants (Red Ruth's soul-stock, the heartstone, the letter). |
| Letting a debt sit across the arc | It hardens toward a **Fhet'Ahla-notarized obligation** (§7.4). Now it's enforceable. Now it has a default clause. |

#### Playing Mahadi [D&D CANON presence / HOMEBREW voice]

- **Presence:** immaculate, unhurried, radiant with hospitality. The *hands are clasped backwards* — palms facing out, fingers folded the wrong way — and he never hides it, because he never needs to. He remembers every name, every prior order, every debt, and produces them like small gifts: *"The heather-and-ash tea again? You have excellent memory. I have a better one."*
- **Voice:** warm, low, faintly musical, with the cadence of a maître d' who has all the time in the underworld. Never raises it. Never cackles. Speaks in courtesies that turn out to be terms. **Catchphrase:** *"For you, of course."* (Every discount, every kindness, every trap wears it.)
- **Delighted by:** being *shown* something rare — a soul-good, a secret, Red Ruth's leftovers, a name he didn't have. Genuine appreciation of his food and his floor. A guest who plays the game well (he loves a worthy counterparty).
- **Irritated by:** vulgarity — threats, raised voices, haggling like a fishwife. He does not scowl; he *cools.* Insult the hospitality and the warmth stays on his face while it drains from the room.
- **The tell:** watch the pen and the smile. When the smile stays but the eyes go *still* — when he stops volunteering and starts only answering — the mood has turned Wary. When he sets his fingertips together and says *"Let us not spoil a lovely evening,"* the party has one sentence left before Cold.
- **As "Heroes of the Last Hill":** he receives them as honored returning guests — a reserved table, the good vintage, the first course pressed on them before they can decline. The reputation buys warmth and access; it does **not** buy off the debt. He is *investing* in heroes, and investors expect returns.

#### Bargaining — sample lines

- **Greeting:** *"The Heroes of the Last Hill, returned to my floor. Sit — no, I insist, the good chairs. The first course is already on its way; we can discuss coin once you've eaten. One never bargains hungry."*
- **Meet/beat the DC (10–20% off):** *"For you, of course — call it a courtesy between friends. And take the vintage; it pairs with the venison. Consider it... noted, and forgotten."* (The discount is real; the "noted" is the hook.)
- **Beat by 5+ (best case):** *"Ah, you *are* good. Very well — my best price, and the private room for your rest, gratis. It pleases me to be generous with people who understand the value of a thing. We are going to do *such* business, you and I."*
- **Fail (price holds):** *"A fair try, and I mean that. But this is my price, and my prices are as gracious as I am. Shall we say yes, and move on to pleasanter things?"*
- **Fail by 5+ (soured):** *"...I think we have misunderstood one another. The offer stands at the door price — in gold, and with the mortal's surcharge. Do enjoy the rest of your evening."* (Warmth on the face; winter in the voice.)
- **The hard won't-budge (the debt itself):** *"Waive it? My dear, the coin was never the point. The *courtesy* was extended; the courtesy will be returned. That is not a fee I can discount — it is simply how the world balances. You understand."*
- **Close / walk-away:** *"Go with my blessing, and come back soon — you always do. The caravan remembers its friends. And its ledgers."*

#### ⭐ Signature item — A Bottled True Memory [HOMEBREW]

A single perfect memory, drawn whole and unblemished from a soul and sealed in a phial of warm smoked glass that shows the scene playing silently inside. Mahadi keeps a small, exquisite cellar of them — *"the vintages that matter,"* he calls them. He will part with one only for the right guest, and only for the right price.

- **What it is:** a chosen true memory made *object.* Whose memory, and of what, is the transaction — Mahadi has, or can *acquire*, a memory relevant to the party's business (a warlord's true face, the last hour of a dead ally, the location seen by a soul now in a coin, the words of a contract as they were truly spoken).
- **Rules effect (level 12):** shattering the phial as an action lets one creature *live* the memory as though present — gaining, at the DM's discretion, a **definitive answer to one question** the memory can address, **or** advantage on all checks against a specific person, place, or contract for the next 24 hours (the party now *knows* them). A memory of a place functions as a one-use, perfectly reliable *scrying* of that place as it was. Single use; the memory is consumed.
- **Price:** **15 SC, or (far more likely) a bargain** — a named favor to be called, plus something *shown* to him (Red Ruth's leftovers, a secret, a name for his cellar). **SC/bargain only.** The favor route is how he *wants* to sell it; the coin price is deliberately steep so the party trades in obligation instead.
- **Haggle:** barely (DC 22, cap 10%). You do not talk Mahadi down on a vintage. You can only choose *which currency* deepens the debt.

**Won't flex on:** the *fact* of the debt. He'll happily "discount" coin prices as a courtesy (which deepens the debt); he will **not** waive the obligation attached to hospitality. **Signature hook:** the hands clasped backwards, the perfect memory, the bill you didn't know you were signing. The Red Ruth gambit (primer Act 2) is transacted *here.*

## 7.2 The Firesnake Forge — Rassh [D&D CANON]

**Proprietor:** **Rassh**, salamander smith (CR 5 salamander; the forge is worked by **fire snakes**). Buys and sells weapons and armor, **repairs equipment**, and — canonically — **repairs AND refuels infernal war machines.** The honest transaction on the floor: burns hot, talks little, delivers.

**Attitude:** **Cordial**, blunt. No small talk, no games — a straight trade. **Insight DC 12** (he's the easiest read in the market; a success confirms his prices are firm-but-fair and he genuinely respects competent fighters, which is a small Persuasion angle).

**Haggling Rassh:** Haggle DC **14**, cap **20%**. He respects a good argument and *loathes* a bluff — **Deception vs. Rassh is at disadvantage** (fire snakes hiss; he knows metal and he knows liars). Persuasion (respect, repeat custom, a warrior's rapport) is the best angle. A thrown-in extra from him is usually **free basic repairs** or a handful of ammunition.

#### Playing Rassh [D&D CANON creature / HOMEBREW voice]

## 9. Running the floor

The Emporium is not a dungeon and it is not a shop with a counter — it's a *room full of charming predators* the party has to move through with their goals in their pockets and their tempers on a leash. Run it as **open shopping with a pulse**: the party wanders, the DM cuts to whatever stall or face is nearest to their intent, and every few beats you drop a floor event (below) to remind them the room is alive and watching. Keep the camera loose. Let players split up to work different stalls; the floor rewards a party that divides and gossips.

**Pacing the open floor.** Don't roll for every step. Roll (or pick) an encounter/event when: the party lingers, the party *asks around*, someone flashes coin or a soul-good, or a scene needs a spark. Two to four floor events across a market visit is plenty — this is texture between the set-piece scenes (Mahadi, Or'Henn, the bounties), not a slog of random tables. When in doubt, pull an **Opportunity** to reward initiative and a **Complication** to raise the stakes, and let the rest breathe.

### Timekeeping — the caravan's clock [D&D CANON + HOMEBREW]

**[D&D CANON]** The Wandering Emporium is *temporary and mobile.* It stops where the paying is good, unfolds off its war machines, does business, then packs itself back onto its wheels **in about an hour** and rolls on to the next patch of Hell. It is never a fixed location; it is a window that closes.

**[HOMEBREW — the visit clock.** Give the party a runway so the market has pressure without a stopwatch. Default: **the Emporium is parked at the Last Hill for roughly 24–36 hours** (one long-rest-plus-a-morning of business). Announce it in-character through the greeter or Mahadi — *"We move at the next tide of ash. You have until then."*

**Departure triggers (it leaves when any one fires):**

| Trigger | What it means at the table |
|---|---|
| **The clock runs out** (24–36 hrs of business) | Default. The tents fold; the party gets a "last call" warning ~1 hour out. |
| **The floor stops paying** | If the crowd thins and no big deals are moving, Mahadi calls it early — the party's window shrinks. |
| **A bigger power summons it** | 🎲 DIAL: Asmodeus, an archdevil, or a warlord with real coin can pull the caravan away *now.* Good for cutting a session that's dragging. |
| **The peace is broken badly** | A truce-break severe enough that Mahadi wants the offenders *gone* can end the party's visit on the spot (expulsion). |

**Lingering costs — what it costs to overstay or push the window:**

- **Hospitality compounds.** [D&D CANON] Every extra "on the house" course, soak, or shave taken while lingering **deepens the debt to Mahadi** (see hospitality-debt engine in the primer). Time spent basking is time spent owing.
- **The room learns you.** The longer the party stays, the more the *rival buyers, brokers, and old faces* clock what they're carrying (Ruth's leftovers, the capacitor, soul-stock). Attention is a lingering cost. Overstay and the Red Ruth bidding war heats up whether they wanted it to or not.
- **Miss the window, chase the caravan.** If the party dawdles and the Emporium departs before they finish (repairs, recruiting Or'Henn, sealing a bounty), the unfinished business becomes a *travel problem* — they must catch the caravan on the road (a Wasteland encounter, d20 result 13) or wait for its next stop, burning days off Asimov's one-week clock. **The visit clock and the Soul Council clock are meant to squeeze each other.**

### How the wards and truce feel to move through [D&D CANON + HOMEBREW]

**[D&D CANON]** Violence is forbidden; Mahadi and Asmodeus's backing enforce it. Make the *peace itself* a sensory thing so players feel the wrongness of a Hell where no one is fighting:

- **The hum.** Under the market noise there's a low, warm, tuning-fork hum — the supernatural wards. It's the same note as a soul coin, magnified. Players with a passive ear (or a **DC 12 Perception**) notice it never stops.
- **The weight on a raised hand.** [HOMEBREW] When a PC *reaches for a weapon or begins a hostile spell*, describe the ward pressing back: the air thickens, the hum sharpens to a whine, the nearest devils go still and *interested.* No mechanic fires yet — it's a felt warning. A **DC 15 Insight** confirms: everyone here will side with the house, instantly, against a peace-breaker. The truce is not politeness. It's physics with teeth.
- **Feuds check at the door.** The greeter's "any *quarrels* today?" is a real ritual. Grudges don't vanish — they're *held*, pressurized, waiting for the road. Play the room as full of people who would very much like to kill each other and cannot, which is exactly why the smiles are so wide.

### Set-piece social encounters

Three fully written scenes. Run any as a spotlight; all resolve socially, and any flirtation with violence resolves via Mahadi's enforcement, not a fight the party can win.

#### Set-piece 1 — The creditor calls the debt [HOMEBREW]

**Who:** **Malfius Nine-Reeds**, Fhet'Ahla's clerk (or Mahadi's smiling steward, if you'd rather keep the notary clean for Act 5), approaches between courses with a slim folio.

> *"A trifle. Truly. But my ledger is a jealous thing, and it says the House extended you a *courtesy* — a rest, a repair, a first course 'on the house' — and courtesies, like coins, are meant to circulate. I've come to help it circulate. Nothing so vulgar as a demand. Merely... a reminder that a balance exists, and that balances prefer to be settled while the settling is *cordial.*"*

**The beat:** The debt is real but not yet urgent — it's a *lever*, not a chokehold. The clerk wants the party to feel the weight of what "free" cost them, and to nudge them toward paying in the coin Mahadi actually wants: a lead on Red Ruth's leftovers.

**How it resolves:**
- **Pay it plainly** — a soul coin, a favor, a marked obligation. Clean, and the clerk beams.
- **Renegotiate** — **DC 16 Persuasion** to convert the debt into a *service on the road* (a bounty, a delivery) rather than immediate payment. Success buys time; the debt follows them.
- **Deflect toward Ruth** — offer *information* about Red Ruth's death or effects in lieu of payment. The clerk's pen goes still; suddenly the debt is very forgivable. (This is the gambit closing its jaws softly.)
- **Refuse cold** — the debt escalates to Fhet'Ahla's desk and becomes a *written* instrument next visit. Never lethal — it's a rakshasa's patience, not a brawl. But the tab now has teeth.

**Do not** let the debt be waved away for free. The peace, and the hospitality, only mean something if they cost.

#### Set-piece 2 — The Mephistophelian agent, sniffing around [HOMEBREW — ties to the Mephistopheles thread]

**Who:** A cambion in blue-black livery, cold and courteous, who has been *watching* the party since they arrived. He introduces himself as an "actuary" and asks to buy them a drink.

> *"You were at the Last Hill. No — don't be modest, it's not becoming in heroes. My lord takes an interest in what happened on that mountaintop. A certain... *asset* of his walked off that mountaintop when he ought to have burned on it. Dumal, I believe he was called. My lord does not misplace an investment — he *recovers* it, and he is ever so curious where a wounded flame crawls off to."* A thin smile. *"I'm simply confirming names for the file. Yours, for instance. It's such a small thing, a name."*

**The beat:** Mephistopheles has a **fresh interest** in Dumal's escape (thread) — his asset is loose and wounded — and this agent is here to confirm the party's identities and learn what they know of where Dumal fled. Nothing binds; nothing threatens outright — the truce forbids it. The menace is entirely in the *filing.*

**How it resolves:**
- **DC 16 Insight** — read the livery and the danger: this is the eighth Hell's cold, and the party is being *catalogued* for later.
- **DC 15 Deception** — deflect, downplay their role, or feed him a bent name. Success buys distance; failure means Mephistopheles's ledger gets a clean entry.
- **Refuse to talk** — perfectly safe (truce), but the *refusal itself* is data: guilty men go quiet. The agent bows and files that, too.
- **Threaten him** — the wards hum, the room stills. He *wants* this; a truce-break by the party is a gift to his lord. Enforcement would land on the *PC*, not the agent. Play the temptation; make the restraint the player's.

**Leaves behind:** the sense that a very cold, very patient archdevil now knows their names — a thread to pull across the arc.

#### Set-piece 3 — The truce-baiting provocation [HOMEBREW — ties to Last-Hill survivors]

**Who:** A **maw demon or hobgoblin survivor** of the warband the party broke at the Last Hill (clientele 16), who spots them across the floor and starts talking — loudly, for an audience.

> *"There they are! The 'Heroes of the Last Hill.'"* Spit on the word. *"You want to know what a hero smells like? Ash and cowardice. My brothers are meat on that ridge because of you. Come on, then. Come *finish it.* You've got the steel on your hip. I can see you want to. Right here. Right now. In front of everyone."*

**The beat:** This is the pressure the whole session is built on. The survivor *cannot* attack — the truce binds it too — so its revenge is to make **a PC swing first.** If a party member breaks the peace, the survivor gets to watch Mahadi's enforcement fall on the *hero*, and dies (or doesn't) satisfied. The bait is the point.

**How it resolves:**
- **Hold the line** — **DC 14 Wisdom save** for a PC riding the battle-high, or simply roleplay the restraint. The wards press; the room leans in; a held temper is a *win* here.
- **Answer with words** — insults, cold dismissal, even intimidation are all *free* (a first verbal offense earns only Mahadi's warm smile). Let players vent verbally; the peace survives words.
- **Draw a weapon / ready a spell** — the market goes silent, enforcers drift close, **last warning.** The player still has one beat to stand down.
- **Strike** — the truce breaks *on the party.* Reach for **expulsion first** (Red Ruth deal poisoned, bounties revoked, Or'Henn recruitment jeopardized, the session's goals gone) — never a free fight, never a fair one. Lethal enforcement only if the table wants that wall (TRUCE appendix). The survivor's revenge is complete either way.

**DM note:** Play the survivor as *pathetic and provoking*, not threatening. The horror is that the party could ruin everything by being *right* about wanting to kill it.

#### Set-piece 4 — The too-good soul-coin deal [HOMEBREW]

**Who:** A gambler-broker (clientele 2) or a hungry broker (clientele 3) sidles up with an offer that's *almost* insulting in its generosity.

> *"You look like people with an eye for value. So: three warm coins — three, feel the heat on them, real as your heartbeat — for that little trinket you're carrying. The heartstone, the soul-flake, the odd bit of Ruth's stock, whatever's lightest in your pack. Three coins for a bauble. Where's the harm? I'm overpaying and we both know it. That's how much I *like* you."*

**The beat:** The deal is genuinely favorable — which is exactly the problem. Either the broker is a front for a rival buyer (or Mahadi himself, testing what the party will part with), the "coins" carry a rider, or the "bauble" is worth vastly more than the party realizes and the broker knows it.

**How it resolves:**
- **DC 17 Insight** — the price is *too* good; someone up the chain wants this badly, and the party is being underpaid for something pivotal (Ruth's leftovers, soul-stock).
- **Ask Asimov** — from the capacitor, he reads any rider on the coins or the true worth of the good, cold and clear (his rules-lawyer role).
- **DC 15 Investigation** on the coins — reveals a hidden clause: a mark, a summons, a debt that rides the coin to whoever spends it.
- **Take the deal blind** — that's the game. Let them. The consequence surfaces later, and Asimov gets to say *"you should have asked."*

**DM note:** Never let a too-good deal be *clean.* The lesson of this floor is that generosity is the most expensive thing in Hell.

## 13. DM tips for the Emporium

A punch-list for keeping the floor sharp:

- **Keep Mahadi gracious, never cackling.** The warmth is 100% sincere on the surface; the menace is entirely in what the warmth *costs.* A rakshasa who twirls a mustache is a wasted rakshasa. The bill is the villainy.
- **Make debts feel real.** "On the house" is never free — track every courtesy taken, and let it compound across visits. When a debt is called (Set-piece 1), it should have *weight*, not be waved off. A debt the party forgets is a debt you're not running.
- **Never let a truce-break be free.** The peace only means something if breaking it *costs.* Reach for expulsion first — poison the deals, revoke the bounties, cost them the session's goals — before you ever reach for a statblock. Lethal enforcement is a wall, not a boss fight, and only if the table wants it.
- **The temptation IS the drama.** Most encounters here are social because the pressure is *restraint.* The Last-Hill survivor (Set-piece 3) exists to make a hero *want* to swing. Put the choice on the *player*, then honor whichever way they go.
- **Reward working the room with real leads.** A party that gossips, spends soul coins on brokers, and asks around should get *true, usable* information (the Or'Henn pointer, the rumor table, road-contacts). Don't make initiative feel like flavor — make it pay.
- **Weave the live threads in, don't dump them.** Mephistopheles counting the party, Mahadi wanting Ruth's leftovers, Smiler's owed banishment, the soul-council clock — plant one per scene as *texture.* Let them breathe; collect on them across the arc.
- **Let the two clocks squeeze.** The caravan's departure window and Asimov's one-week soul clock are meant to press on each other. If the party dawdles, tick both louder (Complication 10, clientele 17).
- **Kindness is the rarest currency.** Burney is the one genuinely good soul on the floor. If a PC is decent in a place that punishes decency, *notice* it — quietly. It's a small warm mystery to bank, not spend.
- **Split the party on purpose.** The floor is one of the few safe places to let PCs wander off alone and work different stalls. Cut between them; let gossip from one table land at another. A divided party on this floor is a *feature.*
