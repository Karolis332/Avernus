Approach — The Edge of the Brambles

Context Ralzala sent them here. Red Ruth knows how to break a dao's pact with an archdevil. The Bone Brambles lie roughly 8 to 14 hours west of the Demon Zapper. DC 15 Nature or Survival halves the travel time. The party can fly the Lady Vengeance over the bramble canopy — but the brambles repel airborne approach (see below).
The ash-flats end at a wall of black thorn. It stretches in both directions farther than you can see — twenty feet tall, woven from bone and bramble in equal measure. Calcified fingers reach out of the thicket as if pleading. Vines as thick as a man's wrist twine through the rib-cages of horses, of devils, of things you do not have names for. The thorns are six inches long and barbed, and they are red. The smell is wrong. Not rot. Something older. Like the inside of an animal that has been dead long enough to dry. A single path opens at the wall — narrow, exactly wide enough for one. The brambles part for it as though by old agreement. The forest is letting you in. Somewhere far inside, something laughs once. A woman's voice. Then nothing.

Airborne approach (Lady Vengeance)

The brambles reject air travel. If the ship flies over the canopy, vine-tendrils lash up — DC 16 Dex save (or pilot Athletics, war machine handler) or the ship is snagged and dragged down. 4d6 falling damage to the ship; crew DC 14 Dex or take half. Three failed saves = ship is grounded in the brambles, recoverable only after Red Ruth's audience.

Better: moor the Lady Vengeance at the bramble's edge with the crew. The party walks in. Vix, if still around, refuses to enter — "His Ascendancy did not anticipate I would visit the witch today. Apologies. I will wait."

The path narrows

For the first hour the path is wide enough for two abreast. After that, single file. Mounted Aurora on the Moonkite: she must dismount (the Moonkite is too large; it will wait at the edge, or fly above the canopy at 200 ft to avoid the snagging vines).

The Four Hazards

Roll for one every 2 hours of travel, OR run all four if the path was failed. The forest tests intruders before granting audience. Survive them and Red Ruth respects you (+1 disposition each cleared).

Hazard 1 — The Bone Choir CR 8

The brambles thicken. The path opens into a clearing thirty feet wide. The clearing is made of singing. Suspended in the brambles overhead are twelve skulls, threaded on red sinew, each cracked open at the jaw. As you enter, they begin to sing in unison — a low note, a held drone, neither pleasant nor unpleasant. And the brambles beneath the skulls begin to move.

Encounter — Bramble Wights ×3

Custom: bone-and-thorn humanoids animated by the Choir. AC 14, HP 95 each, CR 5.

  • Multiattack: 2 thorn-claw attacks (+7 to hit, 2d6+4 piercing + DC 14 CON or take 1d6 necrotic on next turn from blood-loss bramble)
  • Bramble Lash (recharge 5-6): 15-ft cone, DC 14 STR save or restrained until end of next turn (vine grapple)
  • Life Drain (1/day each): +7 to hit, 3d6 necrotic + max HP reduced by damage taken (until long rest)
  • Sunlight Sensitivity (won't matter in Avernus)
  • Drop: they collapse when the Choir is silenced. Smash the skulls (AC 12, HP 5 each — Aurora can do this with a single arrow each) and any remaining wights drop instantly. This is the puzzle clue: don't fight the wights, fight the music.
Clue for the players (DC 12 Perception)The wights pulse in time with the choir's note. Silencing the choir kills them. Drenwal — DC 13 Religion: these are spirit-anchored constructs. Break the anchor (skulls), break the bodies.

Hazard 2 — The Hadar Pocket Environmental

A section of path drops into a hollow. The brambles arch overhead, closing off the sky. The air gets colder, then colder still. The light dies. You are standing in a six-foot circle that is the only place in this pocket of forest where the dark hasn't taken hold. You can hear something chewing.

Mechanic — Hunger of Hadar Aura

  • The pocket is a 60-ft radius, lasts 80 ft of path
  • Movement: difficult terrain (half speed). Anyone moving more than 30 ft in a round through the pocket takes 2d6 cold damage
  • Each round inside: 2d6 acid damage (otherworldly mouths) — no save
  • Vision: blindness; can only see 5 ft
  • Sound: heard in the dark — DC 13 WIS save at end of each round inside, or take 1d4 psychic from the things that whisper to you. Drenwal's mother's voice. Aurora's first lover's voice. Asimov's birth-name.

How to cross

  • Run it: 2 rounds of damage, ~4-6d6 cold + 2-4d6 acid each. Brutal but survivable.
  • Dispel Magic (5th-level slot): Aurora can suppress the aura for 1 minute. Costs her highest slot. Best option.
  • Light spell or sunlight-equivalent: 10-ft sphere of safety. Drenwal's Light cantrip cuts the damage in half within the radius.
  • Crawl through with eyes closed: 3 rounds. More damage. Lulu refuses to enter — she flies the long way around.

Hazard 3 — The Grapple Brambles Skill challenge

The path tightens to a tunnel. The brambles close above. Your shoulders brush thorns on both sides. You can hear your own heartbeat. The vines are watching you. One of them moves.

Mechanic — Bramble Grapple Trap

  • Triggers on anyone walking through (no save — it's a clamp ambush)
  • DC 18 Athletics to break free (was 16 in canon; upscaled for L11)
  • While grappled: 2d6 piercing per round from thorn pressure + cannot use somatic spell components
  • Triggers in sequence — the first PC through gets grappled, the second PC ahead might trigger another 20 ft on, etc. Resolve as a chain.
  • Aurora at STR 3: auto-fail Athletics. She needs another PC to cut her free. Drenwal Str-cut OR Asimov DC 16 Sleight of Hand to dismantle the trap.
  • Each round of grapple: the vines speak. "Stay. Stay with us. We do not bleed in here. Stay."
Aurora's setbackIf Aurora is the one grappled first, the brambles take a particular interest in her — they sense the Moonkite-bond. "Celestial. Pretty. We have not had a pretty one in centuries." Add psychic 1d6 from temptation-whispers each round.

Hazard 4 — The Mirror Pool RP / Optional

The path opens to a tiny clearing — five paces across. In the center, a pool of perfectly still blood. Not water; blood. It does not ripple. It does not reflect — except for one moment, when you each look in, and see yourself as you would be if you had never come to Avernus.

What each PC sees

  • Drenwal: himself, twenty years older, smiling, hair grey, standing in a chapel of the Morninglord. A child plays at his feet — a boy with his eyes and Senna's hair. Then the boy looks at the pool, and his eyes are yellow.
  • Aurora: herself on a moonlit ridge, the Moonkite beneath her, but her own face — her own face is a mask, and behind the mask is something else. The vision lasts a heartbeat too long.
  • Asimov: himself in a featureless white room. The lamp is on a table across from him. The lamp is empty. He is the lamp.

Optional roll — DC 15 WIS save

Success: see the vision, shrug it off. Failure: −1 to the next 3 saves you make against fear or charm. Drenwal especially should fail this — lean into it.

Red Ruth made this pool. Anyone walking past it without looking earns +1 Ruth Disposition (she respects discipline). Anyone who lingers loses 1 (she enjoys breaking you).

Optional — The Captive Hellrider

Drop this between Hazard 2 and 3 A bonus encounter to seed lore + offer a moral choice. Tied loosely to Drenwal's arc — this Hellrider knew his father.
Off the main path, half-hidden in the brambles, you see a man. He is fused with the brambles from the chest down. His arms are still free. His armor is Hellrider blue, blackened with age. His face is alive — burned, sun-blistered, eye sockets weeping pus and tears — but his lips move. He sees you. He starts to weep. "Mercy. Please. Mercy. I have been here since the Wall."

  • Former Hellrider, survived the Descent only to be caught by the brambles two days later
  • Has been here ~30 years. The brambles slow-feed on him.
  • Cannot die — the brambles keep him alive specifically to suffer (Red Ruth's design — she likes him)
  • He knew Drenwal's father, Halvard. If Drenwal speaks his own name, Lucan's eyes focus: "Halvard's boy. You have his cheekbones. He spoke of you. Both of you. He was so afraid. So afraid."

The Hollow Tree — Red Ruth's Lair

The path ends. You stand in a clearing fifty feet wide, ringed by the brambles like a wall. In the center is a tree — though "tree" is not exactly the word. It is the corpse of a tree, white-grey, hollowed out, bigger than a house. The opening in its trunk is shaped like a screaming mouth. Smoke rises from somewhere inside, smelling of cooked meat and lilies. Bones decorate the clearing — femurs in geometric patterns on the ground, ribs tied into small wind-chimes that do not move. The chimes sing when you look at them. They stop when you look away. A voice from inside the hollow: "Come in, dears. I have been expecting four of you, and you have brought three. The fourth one is dead, isn't he? Yes. I felt him die. I am sorry." (Pause.) "...are you coming in or not? My kettle is going."
The "fourth one" reference Red Ruth knows the party used to have 4 PCs (Xebeche and Rebecca, per campaign-state.md, had earlier arcs). She's testing them — does she mean one of those, or someone else? Her oracle is real. Run with it. Could mean Aldwin (the body they didn't identify), could mean a future loss, could mean Lulu's eventual fate. Player paranoia is the goal.

Inside the hollow

Comfortable. Disturbingly so. A small fire in the middle, kettle bubbling. A woven rug of red hair. Three chairs (she expected three). A fourth chair, knocked over, in the corner — pointedly empty.

Red Ruth herself: appears as a stooped old woman in a stained shawl, with thirty-six teeth all human and not all her own. Her hands are red — perpetually wet with blood that isn't her own. She moves like someone half her age.

Audience & Demands

Red Ruth knows why they came. Ralzala already told her telepathically. (Red Ruth has hag-coven-shaped magic + scrying.) She'll skip the explanation, jump to terms.

Red Ruth: "The dao wants out. Of course she does. Zariel does not honor her oaths to small things, and dao are very small things compared to a fallen angel. (sip of kettle-water) I can tell you how to break her pact. I have the rite written in my own hand, here, somewhere — (gestures vaguely at a shelf of bottled organs). But I do not give. I trade. That is the rule. Always the rule. I would break it for no one. (beat) Maybe for you. We will see. Here is what I want."

Negotiation

  • DC 22 Deception: she sees through every lie unless the roll meets it. Failure: she punishes by ageing the deceiver 1d4 years cosmetically (no mechanic, just flavor).
  • If Drenwal volunteers his Bhaal-blood: she's thrilled. Toll counts double. +5 Ruth disposition. But: Bhaal +3 (he gave his blood to a Hell-aligned creature; she's not Bhaal, but she's not friendly either). Net: probably worth it.
  • If they refuse to pay: she shrugs. "Then you may leave. The path remembers you. Walk back. Do not return." Quest fails. Ralzala can't be freed.
Bhaal sensitivityRed Ruth recognizes Drenwal's blood immediately. She also hates Bhaalspawn — a sister hag was killed centuries ago by a Bhaal cult. This gives her TWO conflicting impulses: she wants to help him hurt Bhaal (by breaking a Zariel pact, which weakens Hell generally), AND she wants to bleed him dry. Play this as ambivalence — she's nicer to Drenwal than expected, but pushes him hardest.

Once paid: the audience

Red Ruth: "Good. Good. Now sit. (she sits) The rite. Ralzala's pact was sworn in three tongues — Common, Infernal, Terran. To break it, you must undo each tongue. Three speakings, three offerings, three witnesses. (beat) You will need three things — (she ticks them off on bloody fingers): One. Blood of an enemy of Zariel. The Forge will give you what you need. Or — closer, harder — the river-mud from where the River Styx meets the boundary of Cania. Mephistopheles's water, not Zariel's. Pick. Three. Ralzala's true name, spoken in three tongues at midnight, by three voices." (pause) "I will give you her true name. It is part of the price. You came here for it."

Optional — Red Ruth's Oracle Reading (For PCs)

This is bonus content Red Ruth is a canonical oracle. If the party asks her to read for them (one specific question), she will — for an additional toll. Use this to feed the players a real piece of plot foresight. Limit one question per party member, ever.

How the reading works

Red Ruth cuts the asker with a bone-knife (2d6 damage, no save) and watches the blood pool on the stone floor. She reads the sanguine movements. The reading is true but framed as cryptically as the DM wants.

Spectral Sisters — Simulated Coven CR +2 escalation

What this is Night hags traditionally form covens of 3 to share spell access. Red Ruth's two sisters are dead — one killed by a Bhaal cult three centuries ago, the other lost to a wasting curse from a rival coven. She has been a coven of one for a long time.

When she really needs the old powers, she pulls their essences from the Ethereal Plane through her heartstone shard. They manifest as translucent specters in the shape they died — Selvia with the cult dagger still in her chest, Marsha with rotted lungs leaking grey vapor. They cannot stay long. The summoning costs Red Ruth.

Hag Coven Concentration — Up to 2× SIMULTANEOUS

The big mechanical wrinkle Hag covens canonically distribute concentration across the sisters. While the coven is manifested, the sisterhood holds TWO concentration spells at once.
  • Slot 1 — Red Ruth's own concentration. One of her solo spells: Hunger of Hadar, Black Tentacles, Geas, etc. Held by Ruth herself, broken if she takes damage and fails a Concentration save (CON +4).
  • Slot 2 — A coven concentration spell. Held by either Selvia or Marsha on Red Ruth's behalf. Hold Person, Bestow Curse, Phantasmal Killer, Polymorph, Scrying, or Eyebite.
  • Counterspell and Lightning Bolt do NOT use concentration — Counterspell is a reaction, Lightning Bolt is instantaneous. The coven can fire these freely on top of the 2 concentration slots.

This is a real escalation. Where a normal CR 13 caster can lock one PC down, this coven can lock two down simultaneously while still firing damage spells through them.

Concentration tracking — practical

  • Selvia's concentration ends if she takes damage (CON save DC = 10 or half damage taken, whichever higher). She has no STR/CON listed because she's incorporeal — use her CHA mod (+3) for Concentration. Easier to break than Ruth's.
  • Marsha's concentration same — CHA mod +2 for Concentration save.
  • If either sister is destroyed: her concentration spell ends immediately. Free the PC she was holding.
  • If a sister is concentrating, only ONE sister can — the other is free to attack each round. Players figuring this out: focus fire on the concentrating sister to break the lockdown.
Updated round-by-round combat tactics — 2-concentration shape
  • Round 1 (summon round): Ruth bonus-actions the summon. Sisters appear adjacent. Ruth casts Hunger of Hadar on the party's grouping (her concentration). Selvia immediately concentrates on Hold Person against Drenwal (DC 14 WIS — likely fails). Marsha closes for melee. Counterspell reaction available if the party tries a 4th+-level spell.
  • Round 2: Aurora and Asimov take damage if in Hadar. Drenwal paralyzed by Selvia's Hold Person. Marsha attacks Aurora (focus the weakest STR). Ruth bonus-action moves via Bramble Bond, action Lightning Bolt through the party line (no concentration, free with active concentration spells).
  • Round 3: If Drenwal saves Hold Person, Selvia drops it and switches to Eyebite (Sleep variant) — DC 14 WIS, Drenwal auto-fails (Bhaal pressure). Ruth still concentrating on Hadar. Two concentration spells active: Hadar (Ruth) + Eyebite (Selvia).
  • Round 4: Marsha uses Plague Breath (no concentration) in a cone. Ruth drops Hadar, casts Phantasmal Killer on Aurora (Ruth's concentration shifts). Selvia maintains Eyebite. Both concentration slots in use again, different spells.
  • Round 5+: Steady-state. Each round: Ruth's concentration spell + a sister's concentration spell + reaction Counterspell + sister melee/breath actions. The fight is brutal until a concentration spell is broken.

Win condition for the party: break the spectral sister holding concentration (focus fire). The 2nd concentration slot drops. Either Ruth or the surviving sister is now holding one spell instead of two — half the lockdown is gone.

Strategic Note for the DM

The coven is the shape of the boss fight if combat happens. Without it, Red Ruth is a strong solo (CR 13). With it, she's effectively CR 15–16 — Deadly for L11 party of 3. Use it only if the party provokes her, and use it as a clear escalation telegraph: "You should not have done that. Selvia. Marsha. Come, sisters."

If the party negotiates well, the coven never appears in anger. It might appear briefly in RP as a moment of vulnerability from Ruth — "Let me show you something. Stay still. They will not hurt you."

Breaking Ralzala's Pact — The Rite of Three Flames

Design note Single-moment ritual. One image. One stone, three flames, one breath. The whole rite resolves in 60 seconds of in-fiction time. Run it slow at the table. Drop the music, dim the lights if you can.

The Pact-Stone

Red Ruth has given them the only thing they really needed: knowledge. The pact is not in Ralzala's name. It is not in her oath. It is in a stone she carries inside her chest — a small fist of petrified essence she pledged to Zariel the day she swore. It has been there for centuries. It is heavy. It is hot. She hates it. To free her: cut it out, and burn it in three flames at once. That is the rite. There is no chanting. There is no midnight. There is no circle. There is only the stone, and the three fires, and the dao who must let it go.

The Rite — Step by Step

1 · Cutting the Stone

Returning to the Demon Zapper, the party finds Ralzala waiting. She steps from the earth, bows, and bares her chest. The stone is visible just under her skin — a black mass veined with red, the size of a small fist.

  • Drenwal (or any PC) extracts it with a blade. DC 14 Medicine — a wound, not surgery.
  • The cut costs Ralzala 50 HP (she has ~150 to spare — she'll survive). Real blood, dao-blood: pale gold, smells of iron and storm.
  • Failure of the check: botched extraction. The stone is removed but Ralzala takes 100 HP instead. She's still alive but unconscious — the rite continues on her behalf. Drenwal can heal her after.

2 · The Three Flames

The stone must be touched by three different fires at the same instant. If even one is missing, the stone resists.

FlameSource options
Mortal FireA torch held by a PC. Or Drenwal's Sacred Flame, Light cantrip with substance, or a lit oil from the ship. Trivial. Anyone can provide.
Fey FireRed Ruth gave them a small clay vial of purple flame as part of her toll-package. Open the vial, the fire pours out, doesn't go out — it burns on memory rather than fuel. One-shot use. If they didn't take it from Ruth, they need another fey source: a will-o-wisp's flicker, a stolen pixie-flame, etc.
Celestial FireThree viable sources, ranked by cost:
(a) Drenwal channels it himself via Channel Divinity (Radiance of the Dawn or any radiant cleric channel). Costs his Channel Divinity slot for the day. +3 Eldath (overt celestial channeling for mercy).
(b) Mooncolor's horn (if rescued + accompanying) — she touches the stone with her horn for a heartbeat. Brief flicker of celestial flame. +5 Eldath.
(c) Lulu's breath — if used, Lulu takes 15 HP damage from the effort. She'll do it if asked, looks at Drenwal first.

3 · The Burning

All three flames touch the stone at the same instant. The party must coordinate.

  • DC 14 Religion or Arcana (group check — at least half succeed) to time the convergence correctly. Drenwal/Aurora favored.
  • Success: the stone cracks. Black flame bursts upward two feet, then collapses inward. The stone burns away over six seconds. What remains is a smaller, pale stone — Ralzala's freed essence, no longer pledged.
  • Failure: the stone holds. Ralzala convulses. The rite can be repeated — but only once more (the stone is too damaged to try a third time). Try again immediately or wait for Red Ruth to procure a stabilized version (3 in-game days, costs another toll).

4 · The After

Ralzala stands. You watch her change. The mark on her brow — a small spiral of Zariel's flame-script — burns away in pale smoke. Her shoulders lose a weight you didn't notice she was carrying. She is taller. Or you are looking at her differently. You are not sure. She picks up the smaller, pale stone — her essence, returned to her — and tucks it back into her chest. The wound closes around it. She bows. Deeper than necessary. She is not required to bow. "Friends. I have not been a friend to anyone in a thousand years. Forgive my form. I will get better at it." She produces, from somewhere, a letter sealed with iron-grey wax. The seal is a hammer-and-flame. "To Bel of the Forge. He owes me a favor older than your grandmothers. Use it. He will not turn you away." She gives them the letter, and she gives them her name — her real name, not the one in three tongues — "It is Ralzala'iri. The 'iri matters. Don't say it to anyone else." Then she sinks into the earth, easy and quiet, and is gone.

The path opens for them on the way out — if Ruth was paid. If they fought her, the brambles are hostile on exit (re-run any Hazards they skipped, +2 to all DCs).

Loot & Treasures — Red Ruth's Lair

  • Theft: DC 18 Sleight of Hand or DC 20 Stealth (passive Perception 20). Caught = −5 disposition + a cursed item ends up in their pack anyway.
